FACTION GAPS (2026-10-17, from the shuffled tournament and the balance report of study-2026-10-17b)

What each of the four factions has, what it lacks, and what to add, so each gets a gameplay tone of its own (the user). The
numbers come from two studies run the same evening, after every card went into a deck:
  • The shuffled tournament (Shuffled Tournament.txt): 40 random 30-card decks from the whole pool, 1560 games between the
    Very Hard AI. "Win share" is the share of games a card's side won when it played the card: a card's potential, free of the
    decks we built. "Pool" is how much of a faction's cards play a role, against the whole pool's share (in points).
  • The balance report (Balance Report (AI games).txt): the built decks against each other, 7900 games.
A faction here is one of the four; its subfactions are the archetypes inside it, as Slay the Spire's Ironclad is Strength,
Block and Exhaust at once. The point of the regrouping: a faction's subfactions should cover each other's holes, and the
faction as a whole should keep one or two on purpose, as its tone.

THE PICTURE IN ONE TABLE

  Faction          Cards' win share   Built decks   In pairs   Card edge (mirror games)
  Establishment          51%              45%         50%            48%
  Capital                46%              44%         50%            51%
  Enlightenment          49%              49%         49%            47%
  Digital Society        47%              68%         44%            45%

  Digital Society's built decks win most (Terminally Online 84%, Moods of Modern Life 75%) while its cards, one by one, are the
  weakest of the four: its decks are tuned and its swarm strategy (74%) is the strongest in the game right now, not its cards.
  Establishment's cards are the strongest one by one and its decks middling: Order & Paperwork 81%, but Shock and Awe,
  Licence to Kill and Black Budget at 35%, 31% and 28%. Strategies: Swarm 74%, Midrange 74%, Control 57%, Engine 36%, Aggro
  32%, Combo 30%. Midrange and Swarm beat Aggro 92% and 89% of the time: the fast decks lose to boards.


1. ESTABLISHMENT (Statecraft, Myth, Shadow Ops.): the old powers. Slow, sturdy, inevitable; hidden knives behind.

  Has: the best set-up cards in the game (slots, Trends, Watchdogs: 60% win share), Statecraft's walls and support (58%),
       Myth's removal (60%) and beaters (57%), Shadow Ops.' board damage (67%) and traps (60%). The most cards (162).
  Lacks: accelerators (−6 points: Statecraft −10, Shadow −5, Myth −3), buffs (−6: all three below the pool), logistics
       (41% win share, Shadow Ops.' 35% the worst in the game), trickery (38%). Shadow Ops. has no walls (−8), no beaters
       (−7), no support (−7): it is glass through and through, and its decks show it (Licence to Kill 31%, Black Budget 28%).
  Its tone: the status-quo faction. It should stay slow and draw-poor; that's the price of its walls and inevitability. Myth and
       Statecraft hold the board, Shadow Ops. is the knife. The holes to leave open: accelerators and buffs.
  To add (within the tone):
    • Shadow Ops. needs a body or two that survive a hit, so its knives have time: a 2/5 Undercover agent, a Safe House wall
      with Concealed (the cover is the toughness). Not healing: the faction can't heal.
    • Cheaper logistics for Statecraft (it has the set-up but not the moves): a Redeployment that moves a creature and gives it
      Armor, a Convoy that swaps two of yours. Myth's logistics stay poor on purpose (it can't move its creatures).
    • A Statecraft removal that isn't a wrath: it has −5 removal and leans on Preemptive Strike. A Court Martial (destroy a
      Revealed or Deploying enemy creature) fits its paperwork.
    • Shadow Ops.' Aggro deck (Licence to Kill) should become a Control deck: Aggro loses to every board (32%); Black Budget
      is already Control but has no body to control from.

2. CAPITAL (Corporate, Tech): money and the machines it buys. Draws the most, strikes in windows, can't heal.

  Has: accelerators (+21 points, Corporate +26: a third of the game's draw), support buffs that win (63%), debuffs (57%),
       Tech's set-up (+12: Watchdogs, slots) and removal (+8), Corporate's board damage (+9). The best card edge in mirror
       games (51%): its cards do what they say.
  Lacks: ability creatures that win (40%) and trickery (39%); heal/armor support (−7); its decks are the problem, not its
       cards: Shareholder Value 35%, Hustle Rush 32%, Threat Model 32%; only Move Fast and Break Things (76%, Midrange) wins.
       Both Corporate decks are Engine/Aggro, the two strategies that lose to boards (36% and 32%).
  Its tone: the timing faction. Draw, set up, strike in a window. Its hole stays: no healing, no walls to speak of (−2).
  To add (within the tone):
    • Nothing to the card pool first: rebuild Corporate's decks around Midrange (the draw feeding 3/5 bodies and Inflation),
      the way Military-Industrial Complex (92%, Statecraft + Corporate) already plays it. The pool has the pieces.
    • Tech is thin (22 cards): a second Midrange deck's worth of 2-to-3-deploy bodies with Watchdogs on arrival, and one more
      SysAdmin-style wall. Its Combo deck (Threat Model 32%) needs a payoff that ends games, or to become Midrange too.
    • One or two Corporate creatures whose ability earns the draw back (a Headhunter-like body: draw when it kills), since
      its ability creatures sit at 39%.

3. ENLIGHTENMENT (Utopia, Mind): the mind and the future it designs. Slow, strongest late, never a fair fight.

  Has: board damage (65%: Utopia's physics), beaters that grow (56%), buffs (+6, Utopia +15: the most in the game), walls
       (+6), debuffs (+6). Its decks are the most even of the four: 49% alone and 49% in pairs; All in Your Head 60%.
  Lacks: accelerators (−7: Utopia −8, Mind −5), ability creatures (−5, Utopia −8), mobility and intel that win (36% each),
       and Mind has no removal of its own (−11) and no buffs (−8): it bends, it doesn't kill, as designed, but then Utopia
       must kill for it, and Utopia's kills are slow (Engine 48%, Aggro 51%).
  Its tone: the faction that wins late and sideways. Keep it draw-poor (its research is slow) and keep Mind without raw damage.
  To add (within the tone):
    • Prophecies that pay sooner (the user's ask, still open): Utopia's experiments as 1-Day Prophecies (Mox Nox-style
      timing) so the slow faction has a window before Day 6. Prophecy cards win 62% of their games already: more of them.
    • One cheap Utopia draw (a Peer Review that draws 1 and gives a creature +1/+1), its research paying in cards, not only
      stats: the faction's −7 accelerators is the biggest brake on a late-game plan.
    • Mind's removal stays indirect, but it needs one more finisher that converts its control: a Trolley Problem-style
      destroy with a cost, or a debuff that stacks to 0 Attack and keeps the creature as a wall for us.

4. DIGITAL SOCIETY (Online, Offline): the culture, on the internet and off. Momentum, comebacks, swarms.

  Has: beaters (+8, Offline +14: the game's biggest hitters), ability creatures (+9, Online +17), logistics that work (52%),
       and the two best built decks (Terminally Online 84% Swarm, Moods of Modern Life 75% Midrange); Clickbait and Dogpile
       carry the swarm (40 and 28 damage a game).
  Lacks: walls (−5, Online −7), heal/armor support (−8: Online −11), removal in Online (−10), and its cards win least one
       by one (47%; Online 44%, the lowest subfaction): the decks carry weak cards. In pairs it drops to 44%: its cards don't
       travel; the swarm needs the whole deck.
  Its tone: the faction that lives in the moment: no healing, no walls, no intel (as designed). Its swarm is the game's best
       strategy today, which is as much a problem for the others as a strength of its own: watch it after the rebalance.
  To add (within the tone):
    • Online removal that fits the mob: a Pile-On (destroy a creature with 3 or more of yours facing or beside it), a Ratio
      (−X Attack for each of your creatures). Not hard removal on its own.
    • Offline's second deck (Underdogs, 60%) is a Swarm that loses to Terminally Online 0%: give it the comeback identity
      instead (Underdog, Plot Armor, Generational Trauma) as a Midrange deck, so the two subfactions play differently.
    • A few more Online cards (24, the smallest pool after Tech): the subfaction can't fill a 40-card deck without doubles.


WHAT THE WHOLE POOL LACKS
  • Aggro, Engine and Combo decks all lose to Midrange and Swarm (8% to 18% against them). Either the fast decks get reach
    (Face damage wins 53% and there are 4 such cards in the game) or the boards get something to fear: a few more
    cheap removal spells across Capital and Enlightenment, and a Trend that punishes wide boards.
  • Trickery (copies, steals, trades) wins 42% and Mobile (Leapfrog, Swap) 40%: the clever cards don't pay. Either their
    numbers go up (a Mimic that keeps the copy, a Swap that gives Speed) or they stay as flavour and the decks carry fewer.
  • Intel (41%) and swarm summons (41%) are weak as cards but win as strategies (Terminally Online): they need the deck
    around them. Fine for the built decks; they shouldn't be the adventure's reward picks for a new player.
