**0.A.1. Keyword Glossary**

**USAGE GUIDELINES FOR THIS KEYWORD GLOSSARY:**

*   **Comprehensive List:** This section contains the definitive list of standardized keywords and recurring game mechanics for this game.
*   **Stability of Definitions:** The names and core definitions of these keywords are stable. Do not alter without explicit design approval, as per the main GDD guidelines.
*   **Adding New Keywords:** New keywords should only be proposed if a fundamentally new, recurring mechanic is required that cannot be adequately represented by existing terms or combinations. New keywords require design approval before being added here.
*   **GDD Context:** This glossary provides concise definitions. The main body of the GDD explains how these keywords interact within the broader game systems. Refer to the GDD sections cited for each keyword for more context.

---

*   **Armor:** A temporary Health point pool that takes damage before a creature's Health. Recoil damage also hits Armor first. Armor can be removed by specific effects. *(GDD Context: III.F.3)*
*   **Banish:** Permanently removes a card (from hand, board, discard pile, or deck) from the game. Banished cards are outside all game zones and irretrievable. *(GDD Context: II.E.5)*
*   **Cleanse:** An action or effect that actively removes one or more status effects from a target. Specific cleanse effects will define which types of statuses they can remove. *(GDD Context: III.C.4)*
*   **Concealed Action:** An action that is not visible to the opponent in any pre-resolution preview and appears as '???' in the opponent's Turn History upon resolution. Generated by the `Concealed` status or an `Undercover` ability. *(GDD Context: I - Fog of War; Status Effects Compendium for `Concealed` status)*
*   **Conversion:** An effect that allows a player to gain permanent control of an opponent's creature. *(GDD Context: IV.D)*
*   **Creature-Targeting:** An effect or action that targets a specific creature. If the targeted creature moves to a different slot before the action resolves, the action will still affect that same creature in its new location (homing). If the creature dies before resolution, the effect redirects to the slot it last occupied. *(GDD Context: III.F.5)*
*   **Deployment Time:** A state applied to creatures upon entering the battlefield, lasting X turns, during which they typically cannot act or trigger passive abilities. Its duration can be manipulated. *(GDD Context: II.C; Status Effects Compendium for full definition as a state/status)*
*   **Interference:** A category of pre-set effects that trigger during the Pre-Resolution Interference Phase to modify an opponent's queued action. Types include `Nullify (Interference)`, `Reorder (Interference)`, and `Redirect (Interference)`. *(GDD Context: III.D.4)*
*   **Nullify (Interference):** An `Interference` effect that causes a specific opponent's pending action to be marked as "Nullified," resolving with no game effect.
*   **Reorder (Interference):** An `Interference` effect that alters a pending action's `Priority (Action)` or other properties influencing its resolution order.
*   **Redirect (Interference):** An `Interference` effect that changes the target of a specific opponent's pending action.
*   **Priority (Action):** A value assigned to an action that determines its resolution order within the Action Queue, superseding creature Speed. Higher `Priority (Action)` actions resolve before lower `Priority (Action)` actions. *(GDD Context: II.F)*
*   **Recruitment:** An effect that allows a player to gain temporary control of an opponent's creature. *(GDD Context: IV.D)*
*   **Requisite:** A condition or set of conditions that must be met (e.g., sacrificing a creature, discarding cards) before a card can be played or an ability activated. *(GDD Context: II.E.8)*
*   **Revive:** An ability or Spell that returns a creature card from a player's Discard Pile to an empty battlefield slot. *(GDD Context: III.E.1)*
*   **Scheduled Draw:** The primary mechanism for drawing cards, occurring at the Start of a player's Turn. The number of cards drawn is based on a base amount plus modifiers. *(GDD Context: II.E.1)*
*   **Slot-Targeting:** An effect or action that targets a specific battlefield slot. If a creature in that slot moves, the effect impacts whatever is in the slot at resolution. *(GDD Context: III.F.5)*
*   **Strategic Disclosure:** A beneficial effect that triggers for the player who successfully reveals an opponent's concealed information (via an Intelligence action or applying the `Revealed` status). *(GDD Context: IV.A.1)*
*   **Trap (Counter-Intelligence):** A detrimental effect that triggers against an opponent when they reveal specific concealed information or interact with a trapped action/slot. *(GDD Context: IV.A)*
*   **Undercover (Ability):** A passive creature ability causing all actions queued by that creature to be `Concealed Actions`. *(GDD Context: VI.B for example; see `Concealed Action` in this glossary section)*

I. Core Concept & Vision

Premise: A card game focused on outsmarting opponents via strategic planning, information control, and tactical positioning on a constrained battlefield.

Genre: Roguelite Deckbuilder. Players build and modify their deck during a run. Consequently, card choices and adapting to challenges are crucial.

Central Conflict: The core tension revolves around Intelligence versus Counter-Intelligence. Thematically, this is mirrored by a multifaceted conflict:

Established pyramidal powers (Statecraft, Myth, Corporate) strive to impose and maintain their specific paradigms of order and control. They often achieve this by manipulating information, enforcing dogma, and preserving hierarchical structures.

In contrast, decentralized, emergent forces (Digital Society, Zeitgeist, Mind) challenge these established norms. These forces thrive on fluidity, the free flow of (and sometimes weaponized) information, and the unpredictable power of collective or individual consciousness.

Navigating and exploiting this dynamic are clandestine networks and transformative systems (Shadow Ops., Tech, Applied Sci-Fi). These entities wield sophisticated tools of intelligence, counter-intelligence, disruption, and innovation. They may serve various masters, subvert existing powers, or pursue their own distinct agendas within this complex web of influence.

Primary Goal: Outsmart opponents by:

Gathering info on opponent's hidden plans.

Denying info to the opponent.

Controlling the global 'Location' effect (currently prototyped as 'Weather,' but will encompass various environmental or zone-wide states that fulfill the same role).

Creating/manipulating status effects on creatures and slots. (See 'Status Effects Compendium' and 'Slot Effects Compendium').

Strategically moving creatures (friendly or enemy) for advantage.

Key Interaction: Tactical Positioning

Battlefield: Each player controls one row of 5 creature slots, facing each other symmetrically.

Slot Occupation: A maximum of one creature can occupy each slot. Placement is therefore key. **A player's slots can only be occupied by creatures they control. If an opponent's creature moves to a player's slot via an effect like *Recruitment* or *Conversion*, control of that creature changes to that player (see Section IV.D for *Recruitment* / *Conversion*). Thus, any creature occupying a player's slot is considered friendly to that player and under their control.**

Adjacency: Non-edge slots have two adjacent friendly slots (left and right) and one opposing enemy slot. Edge slots have one adjacent friendly slot and one opposing enemy slot.

Movement & Blocking: Placement dictates movement paths and potential targets (based on adjacency or position). It also triggers passive effects and generally controls the board. Creatures inherently block the slot they occupy; other creatures cannot move into or through an occupied slot unless an ability specifically allows for crossing, swapping, or pushing/pulling. Crucially, creatures cannot move freely. All movement requires specific Spells or creature abilities. If a creature changes sides (for instance, due to ***Recruitment*** or ***Conversion*** effects - see Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary and Section IV.D), it moves to an available slot on the new controller's side of the battlefield. A creature cannot be voluntarily moved into an opponent's slot by its current controller. Friendly creatures cannot move into enemy slots unless explicitly recruited or converted by an enemy effect. At that point, they effectively become enemy creatures under the opponent's control.

Key Mechanic: Fog of War

The "Fog of War" in this game revolves around the fact that players do not have a real-time preview of the specific actions their opponents are queuing during their planning phase. Opponents cannot see what Spells are being prepared or which abilities are being activated until those actions resolve and are recorded. Information about an opponent's specific plans for the current turn is primarily gleaned *after the fact* through the Turn History, or by using specialized Intelligence abilities that might offer limited insights under specific conditions.

End of Turn Action Revelation & Turn History: At the End of each Turn, after all queued actions for that turn have resolved, they are recorded in the **Turn History**. This history, envisioned with an "old newspaper" flavor for the UI, serves as a log.
    *   Standard resolved actions become fully visible to both players in the Turn History.
    *   Resolved ***Concealed Actions*** (actions hidden from the opponent until resolution) will appear as obscured entries (e.g., '???') in the Turn History for the opponent. The player who performed the ***Concealed Action*** sees it normally. (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary for `Concealed Action`; see 'Status Effects Compendium' for `Concealed`, `Revealed`, `Compromised` statuses and Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary for the `Undercover` ability which also generates `Concealed Actions`).

Intelligence & Investigating the Past: Players can use "Intelligence" actions. A primary use of these is to target specific '???' entries in the opponent's Turn History from previous turns or earlier in the current turn.
    *   Successfully revealing a '???' entry via an Intelligence action makes that past action's details permanently visible to the investigating player.
    *   This act of revelation is an action itself that is queued. When it resolves, if the original concealed action had any "On Being Revealed by Intelligence" trigger effects (like ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)*** or ***Strategic Disclosures*** - see Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary and Section IV.A), those effects are immediately formulated as new actions and added to the current Action Queue, resolving in sequence after the Intelligence reveal action.

Counter-Intelligence: These are actions or effects that might further obscure information, or more commonly, represent the risks of prying too deep (e.g., ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)*** triggered by Intelligence reveals). The `Compromised` status (which leaks information from an enemy agent - see 'Status Effects Compendium') is also a key Intelligence/Counter-Intelligence tool. Pre-set ***Interference*** effects (actions that disrupt the opponent's queue - see Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary and Section III.D.4) also form a core part of counter-intelligence strategies.

The `Revealed` Status Effect (see 'Status Effects Compendium') actively counters concealment. Applying it to a creature that is otherwise "Undercover" or `Concealed` (and thus producing ***Concealed Actions***) will cause its actions to no longer be concealed. This application can itself trigger any "On Being Revealed" effects (defined in Section IV.C).

The `Compromised` Status Effect (see 'Status Effects Compendium') makes an opponent's otherwise concealed creature's actions (past, present, and future) visible to the player who applied `Compromised`, without initially triggering "On Being Revealed" effects. The handler can then choose to use a separate Intelligence action to trigger those effects if desired.

C. Action Economy & Tempo
No resource cost (mana/energy) is required to play cards from hand. Instead, play is limited by hand size, timing considerations, and potential additional costs (see Section II.E.4.a for how all additional costs are handled).
Tempo is managed primarily by ***Deployment Time*** (X), an initial period where a creature is inactive after entering play. Its duration can be manipulated by game effects. (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary for keyword summary and 'Status Effects Compendium' for full status definition).

D. Action Types & Triggering
1. Creature Stats: Attack, Health, and Speed (which influences action order in conjunction with ***Priority (Action)*** - see Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary). Stats can be modified.
2. Creature Abilities (Passive): These abilities are inherently passive. **Crucially, whenever their specified game event conditions are met (as defined in Section IV.C Trigger Events), they ALWAYS trigger automatically. This triggering requires no player decision or intervention at the moment it occurs.** This automatic triggering happens unless the creature is under an effect (such as `Exhausted`, `Muted`, `Ostracised`, or `Brainwashed` - see 'Status Effects Compendium') or affected by ***Deployment Time*** that explicitly prevents its passive abilities from functioning. When a passive ability triggers, its effect is formulated as an action. This action is then added to the Action Queue to be resolved in sequence, as detailed in Section II.G (Universal Action Queuing). Targeting for the effects of these queued actions is predetermined by the card's text (e.g., "target the attacker," "adjacent creatures," "the creature that triggered this") or is resolved randomly if specified by the card. Players do not choose targets for passive abilities when they trigger. **Furthermore, when a player's queued passive ability resolves and affects an opponent, the opponent does not get to make choices about how that specific effect resolves against them; the effect's outcome must be fixed or conditional on pre-determined game state factors, not opponent choice at that moment of resolution.**
3. Special Creature Archetypes:
Support/Specialists: These may lack a standard Attack. They might possess an Active Ability (e.g., deal damage, heal, apply status) that the player queues during their planning phase each turn. If these abilities have additional costs, they are handled as per Section II.E.4.a. The player selects any required targets at the time of queuing, according to the ability's rules. These abilities are subject to ***Deployment Time*** and queue mechanics.
Automated Units: These may lack an Attack. They typically have powerful passive abilities that trigger automatically each turn (e.g., "OnTurnStart: deal 1 damage randomly"; "OnTurnEnd: grant 1 ***Armor*** adjacently"). The target for such abilities is predetermined by the ability itself.
4. Creature Attacks: Players actively queue these during their planning phase. **These attacks are added as actions to the Action Queue.** Target selection for attacks might be restricted by rules (e.g., can only target the slot directly in front, cannot target a creature whose actions are ***Concealed*** or a creature with `Ghosted` - see 'Status Effects Compendium'). Targets are chosen at the time the attack is queued. If a specific creature's Attack action has an additional cost defined on its card, this is handled as per Section II.E.4.a.
5. Spells: These are active actions. The player plays them from hand (e.g., by dragging and dropping onto a target, if applicable). When any card (including a Spell or Creature) is played from hand, or an ability is activated that has additional costs, these costs (including ***Requisites*** - see Section II.E.8 and Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary) are handled as per Section II.E.4.a. Any targets are chosen by the player at that time. The Spell's effect is then formulated as an action and added to the action queue. This action resolves according to standard queue mechanics (***Priority (Action)***, Speed, etc.). Spells can cause various game state changes, including movement, status effects, damage, healing, or card draw modification.
6. Determinism: Game effects are deterministic. There is no percentage-based randomness for success or failure (except in cases of explicitly random targeting). Outcomes are predictable based on the current game state and the interactions of active effects. **Once an action is in the queue and begins to resolve, its outcome is determined by its text and the game state, without further player or opponent choices influencing its core effect resolution (unless explicitly defined by a pre-set interaction like Interference).**

E. Card Management
1. Drawing Cards (***Scheduled Draw*** - see Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary):
Players draw a base number of cards (e.g., 1) only at the Start of their Turn. This is the sole occasion when cards naturally enter a player's hand from their deck.
The number of cards drawn is calculated as: Base Draw + Sum of all positive/negative draw modifiers accumulated during the previous turn.
No effect allows a player to draw cards immediately from their deck mid-turn.
2. Draw Manipulation (Next Turn Draw Modification): This affects future card draws.
Card advantage effects state: "Schedule X additional card draws for your next turn." This increments a counter that applies to the next turn's draw.
Card disadvantage effects state: "Draw X fewer cards next turn" or "Scheduled draw reduced by X." This decrements the counter (to a minimum of 0 cards drawn).
These modifiers accumulate additively during a turn. They are resolved together for the calculation of the next turn's Start of Turn draw.
3. Discard Pile: This is the zone for used Spells and destroyed Creatures.
4. Discarding from Hand:
    This refers to the act of a player moving cards from their hand to their Discard Pile.
    a.  **As an Additional Cost (Universal Rule):** The primary way cards are discarded from a player's hand is as an additional cost to play another card (e.g., Spell, Creature) or activate certain abilities. This rule applies universally to any card play or ability activation that specifies such a cost. "Additional costs" (including those specified by `Requisites` or explicitly phrased as "As an additional cost...") are *always* handled during the player's planning phase when they attempt to play a card or queue an ability with such costs. These costs are *never* paid as part of a resolving effect from the action queue.
        *   **Preemptive Check:** The game system must perform a preemptive check to determine if all mandatory additional costs *can* be paid by the player at the moment they attempt to play the card/queue the ability.
        *   **Payment:** If the preemptive check confirms the costs can be paid, the player then pays them immediately as part of the action of playing the card/queuing the ability. For discard costs, these selected cards are moved to the Discard Pile immediately. The card being played, along with its effect, is then added to the action queue.
        *   **Failure to Pay:** If a mandatory additional cost *cannot* be paid (as determined by the preemptive check), the attempt to play the card or queue the ability is cancelled. The card (if played from hand) is returned to the owner's hand, and the player receives feedback explaining why the action could not be taken (e.g., "Insufficient cards to discard," "No valid creature to sacrifice").
        *   **Cost Fulfillment Nuance:** Some costs might be framed as achieving a certain game state (e.g., "ensure you have fewer than X cards in hand," "control a creature of X faction") rather than a direct payment of specific resources. The player must meet these state-based conditions during the planning phase as part of successfully playing the card or queuing the ability. The preemptive check also applies to these state-based conditions.
    b.  **Discard as a Resolving Effect - Prohibition and Alternative:** Game effects (originating from Spells, creature abilities, traps, etc.) that resolve from the action queue *cannot* cause a player to discard cards directly from their hand. Consider an effect traditionally designed to force an opponent (or oneself) to discard a card from hand as part of its resolution (e.g., an effect that might read "Target player discards 1 card"). In this game, such an effect must instead be implemented differently: it becomes an effect that reduces the number of cards that player will draw on their next turn. This is further detailed in Section II.E.2 (Draw Manipulation) with phrasing like "Target player draws 1 fewer card next turn." This design ensures that all hand manipulation related to card effects focuses on future resources (next turn's draw) rather than immediate hand reduction during the action resolution phase. This, in turn, maintains the uninterrupted flow of action resolution.
    c.  **Distinction from Draw Manipulation:** Discarding from hand as an additional cost is an immediate action taken by the player during their planning phase. It affects the current hand size by player choice to enable another action. In contrast, Draw Manipulation, which includes effects that reduce the next turn's draw (now serving as the mechanical replacement for forced discard effects), is a delayed consequence that impacts the future resources available to a player.
5.  ***Banish***: This action permanently removes a card (from hand, board, discard pile, or deck) from the game. Banished cards are outside all game zones and are irretrievable. A creature that is Banished is not considered "Destroyed" for the purpose of triggering abilities that specifically react to a creature being Destroyed (e.g., `OnDied` triggers as defined in the 'Trigger Events List', or other abilities worded as "when an enemy creature is Destroyed..."). While `OnDied` does not trigger, effects that trigger `OnLeaveBattlefield` (see 'Trigger Events List') will still trigger if their conditions are met, with 'Banish' being the destination zone. (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary for the standalone keyword definition).
6. Card Lifecycle & Reshuffling:
A Spell is played -> its effect resolves -> the Spell card goes to the Discard Pile.
A Creature is destroyed (due to reaching 0 HP or by a "destroy" effect) -> the Creature card goes from the battlefield to the Discard Pile.
Reshuffle Trigger: This activates only when a player is required to draw cards at the Start of their Turn, but their Draw Deck contains fewer cards than the number scheduled to be drawn.
Reshuffle Process:
    The player attempts to draw the scheduled number of cards. Any cards remaining in the Draw Deck are drawn first (this could be 0 cards).
    If the Draw Deck becomes empty and the draw requirement has not yet been met, the player's entire Discard Pile is shuffled. This shuffled pile becomes their new Draw Deck.
    Immediately following the reshuffle, the player continues drawing cards from this new Draw Deck until the total number of cards drawn matches the number scheduled for that Start of Turn phase.
7. Creature Reset: When creature cards are reshuffled from the Discard Pile back into the Deck, any temporary modifications (such as current HP changes, temporary stat alterations, and some status effects) are reset to their printed base values.
8. ***Requisites*** (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary): Some powerful cards have conditions that must be met before they can be played or their abilities activated. These are treated as additional costs and follow the universal rules outlined in II.E.4.a, including preemptive checks and payment during the planning phase. Failure to meet a Requisite prevents the card from being played/ability from being queued, returning the card to hand if applicable, with feedback provided.
9. Opponent Hand Size Checks: Due to the simultaneous nature of player turns and action resolution, any game effects or abilities that need to check the number of cards in an opponent's hand (e.g., for conditional effects, targeting, or information gathering) will perform this check at the End of the current Turn. This check occurs after all other actions for the turn have resolved, ensuring a stable and definitive hand state for such evaluations.
10. **Revealing Cards from Hand**
    Some effects make a player show ("reveal") cards from their hand to their opponent.

    a.  **Instant & Temporary View:** When an effect reveals a card, your opponent instantly sees all its details (text, stats, etc.). This is a one-time look when the effect resolves; the card isn't continuously visible.
    b.  **Stays in Hand:** The revealed card remains in its owner's hand.
    c.  **Choosing Which Card(s) (from Opponent's Hand):**
        i.  **Filtering by Type:** An effect might specify a card type (e.g., "reveal a Creature"). Only cards of that type are eligible to be revealed.
        ii. **Random Pick:** If multiple cards match the filter (or if there's no filter and multiple cards are in hand), the game randomly selects which card(s) to reveal. The effect also states how many cards will be revealed.

F. Action Queue & Turn Flow Mechanics
**Turn Structure Overview:**
1.  Start of Turn Phase (***Scheduled Draw***, OnTurnStart triggers resolve from queue).
2.  Planning Phase (Players queue their Spells, Creature Attacks, and Active Abilities for the turn, paying any additional costs as per Section II.E.4.a).
3.  **Pre-Resolution Interference Phase:** Before the main Action Queue resolves, the game checks for any active "Set-up" ***Interference*** effects (`Nullify`, `Reorder`, `Redirect` - see Section III.D.4 and Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary). If an action in the pending queue meets the trigger conditions of such an effect, that action is modified directly within the pending queue. This phase ensures these specific modifications occur before standard resolution.
4.  Action Resolution Phase (The (potentially modified) Action Queue resolves action by action based on ***Priority (Action)***, Speed, and tie-breakers. Passive abilities triggering during resolution add their effects to this queue. **Crucially, once an action begins to resolve, its effects are applied as written without further player or opponent choices influencing its core outcome, unless such interaction is explicitly part of a pre-set mechanism like Interference.**).
5.  End of Turn Phase (Turn History updated, OnTurnEnd triggers resolve from queue, Hand Size checks for effects occur).

1. Queue Manipulation (General): Beyond the specific Pre-Resolution ***Interference*** effects, other actions might interact with queue visibility (e.g., Intelligence revealing details of pending actions) or apply effects that modify how opponents perceive or plan around the queue. Direct destruction, unsolicited removal, or direct nullification of actions from the queue by card effects during the Action Resolution Phase is not a feature, unless handled by the pre-set `Nullify (Interference)` mechanism.
2. ***Priority (Action)*** Enhancement (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary): Granting actions a higher ***Priority (Action)*** value causes them to resolve earlier, regardless of creature Speed. This is similar to priority mechanics in Pokémon. ***Priority (Action)*** enhancement can be an innate quality of a creature's action (e.g., "Priority Attack: +1 Priority"). Pre-Resolution ***Interference*** effects can also modify ***Priority (Action)***.
3.  Global ***Priority (Action)*** Modifiers: These are effects that impact the ***Priority (Action)*** of all actions for a specified duration.
    Example ("Coordinated Advance"): Friendly actions this turn and for the next 3 turns get +1 ***Priority (Action)***.
    Example ("Temporal Distortion"): For X turns, the normal action resolution order based on Speed is inverted.
    *   **Priority Resolution during Temporal Distortion:** Actions are first grouped and resolved by ***Priority (Action)*** level.
    *   **Speed Resolution during Temporal Distortion:** Within each ***Priority (Action)*** level, actions from creatures with lower Speed will act before creatures with higher Speed. If Speed is tied, use **Standard Tie-Breaking Protocol**.

4.  **Standard Tie-Breaking Protocol:** Used when actions are tied in both ***Priority (Action)*** and creature Speed (or Speed during Temporal Distortion).
    *   Lowest current (Health + ***Armor***) total resolves first.
    *   If tied, lowest base Attack resolves first.
    *   If still tied, resolve randomly (potential Karma System influence).

5.  Tie-Breaking During Temporal Distortion: If actions subject to inverted Speed order (same ***Priority (Action)*** level, Temporal Distortion active) have tied Speed, use **Standard Tie-Breaking Protocol**.

6.  ***Priority (Action)*** Reduction: Actions or effects can assign a negative modifier to an action's base ***Priority (Action)*** (e.g., Priority -1), causing later resolution. Pre-Resolution ***Interference*** effects can also modify ***Priority (Action)***.

G. Universal Action Queuing (New Section)
A fundamental principle of gameplay is that no card effect resolves instantaneously upon the card being played or its ability conditions being met. This principle means that every effect generated by a Spell or a creature ability (whether active or passive) becomes an action for the queue. Similarly, every player-initiated creature attack is treated as such an action. Furthermore, every triggered trap effect, which includes ***Strategic Disclosure*** effects as detailed in Section IV.A.1, is also formulated as an action and placed into the Action Queue. These actions are then resolved in sequence during the Action Resolution phase of the turn, according to the rules outlined in Section II.F (Action Queue & Turn Flow Mechanics). **Once an action begins to resolve from this queue, its effects are determined by its text and the current game state, without further intervention or choices from either player, particularly the opponent, unless such interaction is explicitly part of a pre-set mechanism like Interference.**
It's important to distinguish these card-generated queued actions from other player-initiated processes. For example, paying costs (such as discarding a card from hand as an additional cost, defined universally in Section II.E.4.a) is handled as described in that section during the planning phase. The mechanical process of drawing cards at the start of a turn (***Scheduled Draw***, per II.E.1) also follows its specific rules. Likewise, moving cards between zones as a direct consequence of a game rule – for instance, a creature being destroyed due to 0 Health and thus sent to the Discard Pile – is managed as detailed elsewhere. These types of processes are not "queued actions" generated by cards in the specific sense meant by this Universal Action Queuing principle.

III. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics

A. Information Warfare
This involves constant interaction between revealing an opponent's hidden plans (Intelligence) and concealing your own plans or setting traps (Counter-Intelligence). This also includes using Intelligence actions to investigate obscured '???' entries in the Turn History, potentially revealing past enemy plans or triggering residual effects, such as ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)*** or ***Strategic Disclosure*** benefits. Setting up ***Interference*** effects is also a key part of this loop.

B. Board Manipulation & Control
1. Logistics (Movement): Using abilities or Spells to reposition creatures (self, allies, or enemies) or to swap their positions. This optimizes attacks, blocks, and adjacency benefits, while also disrupting opponents' formations. Tactics include Post-Action Repositioning ("Hit and Run") and Positional Relays (buffs triggered by moving into a vacated slot).
2. Movement Restriction: Using the `Heavy` status (see 'Status Effects Compendium'), specific abilities, or Slot Effects (see 'Slot Effects Compendium') to lock down enemy creatures or protect allied creatures.
3.  Slot Effects / Environmental Hazards: These are persistent or triggered conditions applied to specific battlefield slots that affect creatures or actions related to those slots.

    *   **Single Source of Truth:** For detailed definitions of individual Slot Effects (e.g., Landmine, Scorched Earth), refer to the external companion document: **'Slot Effects Compendium'**.
    *   **Exclusivity & Replacement:** Only one Slot Effect can be active per slot at any given time. If a new Slot Effect is applied to a slot that already has an active Slot Effect, the **new Slot Effect replaces the old one**. The old effect is immediately removed, and its ongoing influences cease.
    *   **Visibility:**
        *   **Visible Slot Effects:** These are openly displayed on the slot, and both players are aware of their presence and type.
        *   **Hidden Slot Effects:** These are not visible to the opponent on the slot. The opponent is unaware of their presence or type until the Slot Effect is triggered by a game event (e.g., a creature entering a slot with a hidden 'Landmine') or revealed by a specific game mechanic (e.g., an "Inspect Slot" Intelligence action, if such a mechanic is implemented). Once triggered, a Hidden Slot Effect typically reveals itself as part of its resolution.
    *   **Triggers:** Slot Effects are activated by specific game events, as defined by the `Trigger Events List` and detailed in the effect's description in the 'Slot Effects Compendium'. Common triggers include `OnEnterSlot`, `OnBeforeLeaveSlot`, `OnTurnStart` (when occupied), `OnTurnEnd` (when occupied), or continuous effects while a creature occupies the slot. The specific trigger logic (e.g., checking if the slot is occupied by an enemy/friendly creature) is part of the individual Slot Effect's definition.
    *   **Functionality:** Slot Effects can cause a wide range of outcomes, including:
        *   Dealing damage to creatures.
        *   Applying creature status effects.
        *   Modifying creature stats while on the slot.
        *   Altering movement rules or costs.
        *   Restricting or enabling certain actions (e.g., a "Jamming Field" might prevent Spells from targeting the slot).
        *   Affecting the controller of the slot (e.g., dealing damage if the slot is empty and targeted).
    *   **Damage to Slots & Occupants (General Rule):** When a slot is the target of damage, or when damage is redirected to a slot (e.g., from a creature-targeted effect whose target died), that damage is applied to any creature currently occupying that slot. If the slot is empty, or if the creature occupying the slot is destroyed and there is excess damage, such damage is dealt to the player who controls that slot.
    *   **Cleansing Slot Effects:** Specific game actions or abilities, often denoted by the `Cleanse (SlotEffect)` keyword (see Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary), can remove Slot Effects from a slot. If a Hidden Slot Effect is cleansed before it triggers, it is removed without its effect occurring and without being revealed to the opponent (unless the cleanse action itself reveals it).
4. Hazard Removal / Decontamination: Actions or abilities designed to ***Cleanse*** negative Slot Effects (see Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary).
5. Location (Weather): Cards that impose global modifiers on the entire battlefield. These effects can last for a set duration or until changed by another Location card.
6. Flooding: Overwhelming the board with many weak or token creatures ("junk").
7. ***Recruitment*** / ***Conversion***: Taking control of enemy creatures. (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary and Section IV.D).

C. Creature Enhancement & Protection
1. Setup: Actions or abilities that increase the core stats (Attack, Health, Speed) of a player's own creatures or allied creatures.
2. Survivability: Actions like Heal (restoring HP), Add ***Armor***, or abilities that redirect attacks.
3. Immunity/Protection: Granular immunity that negates specific negative effects.
4. ***Cleanse***-ing (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary): Actively removing negative status effects from friendly creatures or slots.

D. Disruption & Control
1. Status Effects Application: A core mechanic involving the application of defined status effects (see 'Status Effects Compendium') to enemy creatures or slots.
2. Red Tape: Effects that delay an opponent's actions, lower their ***Priority (Action)***, or add turns to a creature's ***Deployment Time***.
3. Disable Archetype: Strategies focused on disabling key enemy functions, potentially via the `Exhausted` status (see 'Status Effects Compendium').
4.  **Interference (Triggered Queue Modification):** Certain abilities or effects allow players to set up ***Interference*** conditions (pre-set queue modifications like `Nullify`, `Reorder`, `Redirect` - see Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary) in advance. These trigger during the **Pre-Resolution Interference Phase** to modify opponent's queued actions. This is the *exclusive* mechanism for redirecting an opponent's queued actions and for nullifying an opponent's queued actions directly by another effect.
    *   **UI Notification:** When an ***Interference*** effect modifies an action, the UI should clearly indicate this.
    *   Types of ***Interference*** include: `Nullify (Interference)`, `Reorder (Interference)`, `Redirect (Interference)`.
    The trigger conditions for these ***Interference*** effects are defined on the cards that create them.

5.  **Attack/Effect Redirection:** All redirection of an opponent's queued actions is handled *exclusively* by pre-set `Redirect (Interference)` effects, as per Section III.D.4.
6. ***Recruitment*** / ***Conversion*** / Mind Control: Effects that allow a player to take control of an opponent's creature. (See Section IV.D).

E. Recursion & Recovery
1. ***Revive*** (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary): An ability or Spell that returns a creature from Discard to battlefield, subject to ***Deployment Time***.
2. Return to Hand (from Battlefield): An ability or Spell that removes a creature from its slot and puts the card into its owner's hand.
3. Return to Hand (from Discard): An ability or Spell that selects a card from a player's Discard Pile and puts it into that player's hand.

F. Combat, Damage & Targeting
0.  **Active Engagement for Combat Damage:** Creatures only deal combat damage as a result of performing an Attack action. No inherent "retaliation" damage unless by a specific ability.
1.  Direct Damage: Reducing a target's Health.
2.  Chain/Spread Damage: Damage that hits multiple distinct targets.
3.  ***Armor*** (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary): A temporary HP pool. Damage hits ***Armor*** first. Recoil damage also hits ***Armor*** first.
4.  Life Drain / Siphoning: Dealing damage also heals the source or controller.
5.  Targeting Nuances:
    *   **Targeting Types:** Effects and actions utilize two primary forms: ***Creature-Targeting*** (homing on a creature) and ***Slot-Targeting*** (targets the slot itself). (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary).
        *   **If Targeted Creature Dies (under ***Creature-Targeting***):** The effect redirects to target the slot the creature last occupied.
    *   **Damage to Slots & Occupants (General Rule):** Damage to a slot hits its occupant. If empty or occupant is overkilled, damage goes to the player controlling the slot.
    *   **Targeting Restrictions & Patterns:** Many effects have specific restrictions (e.g., only front slot, only `Exhausted` creatures).

IV. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems

A. Traps & Strategic Disclosures
These are effects tied to specific actions or creatures that trigger under particular conditions, often related to information warfare. When a trap's or strategic disclosure's trigger condition is met (as defined by the `OnRevealedByIntelligence` trigger in Section IV.C), its effect is formulated as an action and added to the Action Queue. This includes ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)*** which are detrimental to the revealer, and ***Strategic Disclosures*** which are beneficial. (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary for definitions, and IV.A.1 for Strategic Disclosure mechanics).

**Triggering Conditions for "On Being Revealed" Effects (Traps/Strategic Disclosures):**
Defined by the `OnRevealedByIntelligence` trigger (Section IV.C). Primarily:
1.  Intelligence Action on Turn History successfully reveals a '???' entry.
2.  Application of `Revealed` status (see 'Status Effects Compendium') to an "Undercover" / `Concealed` creature.

Type 1: ***Trap (Counter-Intelligence)*** ("On Being Revealed"): A passive ability that triggers a detrimental effect for the opponent who caused the reveal.
Type 2: ***Trap (Counter-Intelligence)*** (Rider on a Queued Action): A hidden rider on a queued action. If that action is modified by an opponent's ***Interference*** effect, the rider triggers.

Slot-based traps (defined in 'Slot Effects Compendium') also queue their effect as an action when triggered.

A.1. Strategic Disclosure Effects ("On Being Revealed" - Beneficial for Investigator/Revealer)
Distinct from detrimental ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)***, '***Strategic Disclosure***' effects are beneficial outcomes for the player who successfully causes the reveal of an opponent's concealed information.
*   Flavor: Represents the investigating player gaining an advantage from exposing secrets.
*   Mechanism: A player's reveal action (Intelligence on '???' or applying `Revealed` status) on an opponent's concealed information (that has a 'Strategic Disclosure' characteristic) triggers a beneficial effect for the revealing player, queued as an action.
*   Purpose: Incentivizes Intelligence capabilities and adds risk for players using concealment.
*   Triggering: Automatic when conditions are met.

B. Status Effects
Status effects are temporary or persistent conditions that modify creature behavior or stats. They are a core mechanic for creature enhancement, disruption, and control.
**For a comprehensive list and detailed definitions of all status effects, please refer to the 'Status Effects Compendium' document.** All status effects used in card design **must** be from this Compendium, using precise notation. New status effects can be proposed if a fundamentally new condition is needed, but require design approval and addition to the Compendium before use.
The interaction of these status effects with game rules, abilities, and other mechanics is detailed throughout this GDD. Key statuses frequently referenced include `Exhausted`, `Muted`, `Ostracised`, `Brainwashed`, `Heavy`, `Doomed (X turns)`, `Concealed`, `Revealed`, `Compromised`, and `Deployment Time`.

C. Trigger Events
This section outlines common game events that can trigger passive abilities or other effects. These events are defined in the external document **'Trigger Events List'**. Many triggers provide contextual information (e.g., source of damage, specific status applied) that can be used by an ability's logic.

D. Recruitment / Conversion Effects (See Section 0.A.1. Keyword Glossary)
This section details mechanics for taking control of an opponent's creature via ***Recruitment*** (temporary) or ***Conversion*** (permanent).
1.  Definition: Effects allowing a player to gain control of an opponent's creature.
2.  Targeting: Targets an enemy creature, chosen when the Spell is played or ability queued.
3.  Control Change: Creature comes under the new player's control; original card ownership doesn't change. (Destroyed creatures go to owner's Discard; returned-to-hand creatures go to owner's hand).
4.  Positioning & Movement: Creature moves to an empty friendly slot on the new controller's side. Card effect specifies behavior if no empty slot. (See Section I - Slot Occupation for rules on slot control).
5.  Duration:
    *   Temporary ***Recruitment***: Lasts X turns. Creature attempts to return to owner's side (or hand if no slot, or discard if hand full). Does not re-trigger 'OnEnterBattlefield' or reapply ***Deployment Time***.
    *   Permanent ***Conversion***: Lasts until creature leaves play or is re-converted.
6.  State Preservation: Creature retains current Health, ***Armor***, status effects (unless `Doomed (X turns)` is involved), base stats, and current ***Deployment Time***.
7.  Action Queue & Fog of War:
    *   **Cancellation of Previous Controller's Queued Actions:** When Recruited/Converted, previous controller's queued actions from that creature are cancelled.
    *   **New Controller's Actions:** New controller can queue actions from next planning phase.
    *   **"Time Bomb" Archetype:** Achieved through persistent negative status effects (e.g., `Earworm`, `Doomed (X turns)`, `Pandora` - see 'Status Effects Compendium') or detrimental passive abilities, not lingering actions.
    *   **Concealment Status After Control Change:** `Concealed` status or `Undercover` ability benefits the new controller.
    *   **Important Note on Action Persistence:** Actions resolve within the current turn.

V. Game Design Philosophy & Balancing

A. Game Tone
Intentionally cynical, funny, lighthearted, with satire of modern absurdities, bureaucracy, internet culture, myth tropes.

B. Balancing Focus
Paramount goal, requiring ongoing playtesting, data analysis, iteration.
1. Numerical Balance: Conservative initial numbers for stats, damage, healing, ***Armor***, durations.
2. ***Priority (Action)*** Modifier Rarity: Cards granting direct ***Priority (Action)*** enhancements are less common or have higher costs due to their impact.
3. ***Deployment Time*** Manipulation: Abilities reducing/bypassing ***Deployment Time*** are rare and carefully balanced.
4. Status Effect Design Principle: Prioritize using existing, defined status effects from the 'Status Effects Compendium'. If a new status effect concept arises, it must be for a fundamentally new type of condition and undergo design approval before being added to the Compendium and used.
5. Creature Ability Design Principle: Prioritize reusing established core mechanics and defined trigger types (from 'Trigger Events List'). Trigger conditions are flexible. **Effects that give an opponent a choice during resolution are to be avoided; outcomes should be deterministic or conditional on game state.**
6. Faction Synergy & Strategic Versatility Principle: Optimal decks often blend factions to cover weaknesses.
7. Underdog Affinity & Comeback Potential: Game aims for possible comebacks; tie-breaking can subtly favor weaker board states.
8. Embracing and Leveraging Chaos for Strategic Depth: Winning involves creating complex, challenging scenarios for opponents.
9. Rewarding Emergent and 'Game-Breaking' Synergies: Design embraces discovery of powerful, unconventional strategies, rewarding system knowledge and creativity, if balanced and thematic.
10. ***Recruitment***/***Conversion*** Power Level: Permanent ***Conversion*** is rare/high-cost. Temporary ***Recruitment*** balanced by duration/impact.
11. Balancing Levers: Opportunity cost, additional resource costs (handled universally as per Section II.E.4.a), space limitations, domino effects.
12. Development Goals & Player Experience: Reward strategic planning, creative deckbuilding, adaptation, comebacks. Avoid complicated math, unfair luck.
13. Prototype Karma System (Future Consideration): Subtle background mechanic to mitigate extreme "bad luck" streaks.
14. Core Design Tenet - Double-Edged Sword: Significant positive effects often have drawbacks; significant negatives ideally offer an upside. (e.g., `Doomed (X turns)` gives +Attack before death - see 'Status Effects Compendium').
