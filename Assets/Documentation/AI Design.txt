AI DESIGN
How the computer players are built (Scripts/AI). Last updated 2026-10-13.


1. THE PIECES

• AIView (what one seat sees). Built at the start of each plan; the only way the AI looks at the game.
    - Its own side in full: hand, board, the plans it already made, its creatures queued to arrive.
    - The other side as their cards show it: SeenSlot (who's there, the Slot Effect if this seat can see it) and
      SeenCreature (stats, ability, statuses, Heat, Counterattack from its card text). A creature this seat doesn't know
      (Fog of War) is a card back: guessed at 2 Attack / 3 Health, no ability, no statuses.
    - Both players' Health.
    - Never: the other hand or deck, their plans (even the ones the screen shows), hidden traps, Watchdog conditions,
      what an unknown creature really is.
• AIPlanner (the decisions). PlanDay(player) queues the Day's plays, attacks, moves and abilities through the same calls
  dragging cards makes (PlayCardAction, the combat handler, MoveCreatureAction, UseAbilityAction), so the rules can't tell
  it from a person. One class, four levels (AILevel: Easy, Medium, Hard, Very Hard), and for Hard a style (AIStyle).
• AIStyle (a way to play). Weights on what Hard values: damage to the player, its own Health, removal, its board,
  statuses, cards, information, protection, and how eager it is to race.
• AIEntrant (an AI that can take a seat): a level, and for Hard a style. AIEntrant.All lists them all.
• AIController (the plug in a game). Made at startup; at each Dawn it plans every seat the AI plays (MatchSetup.AI) with
  the AI chosen (MatchSetup.AIPlayer). With both seats it also ends the turn after a 1 second pause. F7 hands seats over.
• AITournament (AI against AI, no screen). Plays whole combats with the real GameManager and TurnManager
  (TurnManager.EndTurnImmediately skips the playback), about 15 to 20 games a second.
    - Debug > AI Tournament: the levels ('AI Tournament.txt').
    - Debug > AI Style Tournament: the Hard styles and Medium ('AI Styles.txt').
    - The same runs as on-demand tests: AITournamentTests.
• GameAudit (the bug hunter). Watches every tournament game; see 6.
• Runs in parallel: each game is a GameSession (the rules without a scene) and its TurnFlow, with its own random numbers
  (GameRandom is per thread), so games play side by side on worker threads and replay exactly by seed. AITournament.Workers
  sets how many at once: 5 by default. Measured on a 32-thread CPU (games a second): 1 thread 20, 3 27, 5 33, 8 32, 16 13,
  31 10. The editor's .NET runtime takes one global lock to allocate memory, and the games allocate a lot, so past about 5
  the threads wait for each other. GPUs can't help: they run the same arithmetic on big arrays, while a game's rules branch
  at every step. Every core at once would take separate processes (a headless build of the game, one process per core).


2. HOW A DAY IS PLANNED

The AI plans at Dawn, before the other seat (GDD 3.6: its intents are on the table before yours). Steps, in order:
1. Creatures: each creature in hand to a free slot.
2. Spells: each spell where it does the most good.
3. Abilities: Active abilities (Bodyguard, Wannabe, Identity Theft, Credit Grab) when worth more than attacking.
4. Attacks.
5. Moves (Hard only).
Planning again the same Day only adds what's left.

Scoring a spell ("Value"): the planner tries every place the card can be dropped (every slot for an aimed spell, the side
it does something to otherwise) and adds up what each of its effects does to each target it would land on, as
EffectResolver would pick them:
    + for us on our side, - on theirs; a random target counts as a share of each one it may pick.
    Damage: the damage, or the creature's whole worth (Attack + Health + Armor) plus its Health if it dies.
    A player brought to 0: 100 (nothing counts more; for our own Health, -100).
    Healing, Armor, stat changes, statuses (Burn by its Days, Exhausted by the Attack it stops, Doomed half the creature),
    Slot Effects (a Landmine waits for the next creature, a Snare for one leaving), draws and summons.
    Watchdogs and Trends count as good for whoever plays them; effects that are hard to weigh (copy, swap, grant) count as
    good on the side the card names.
    Each kind of good is multiplied by the style's weight for it (see 4).
It plays the card where the total is highest, and keeps it if nothing comes out above 0. No card has a rule of its own,
so new cards made of existing effects are played without any AI work.


3. THE THREE LEVELS

Easy: plays everything, without judging.
    • Creatures to random free slots.
    • Spells at a random creature on the side the card names.
    • Each creature attacks the slot in front of it.
    • Bodyguard guards someone at random; no other abilities.
Medium: the scoring above.
    • Creatures (the strongest first) to the slot that suits them; a much better one replaces a weak one.
    • Spells in hand order, each at its best aim.
    • Attacks, strongest first, each where it's worth most: a kill, else the player through an empty slot.
    • It counts planned damage, so two hits don't kill the same creature, and it sees lethal.
Hard: Medium, plus:
    • It reads what the cards it knows say:
        - Abilities that bend effects: Contrarian reverses (it will weaken its own Contrarian on purpose), Extremist
          doubles, Unbothered and Cold Storage block, No U and Return to Sender bounce back to us, Too Mainstream ignores
          areas, Spite gains Attack.
        - Statuses: Blessed negates (and loses a stack), Doomed blocks others, Untouchable takes no damage.
        - Heat: random damage goes to the hottest creature it can see, on either side.
        - A Shield stops combat damage; Counterattacks hit back; Hive Mind steals abilities.
        - Its own attacks: Recoil, Penny Stock, Double Tap, Insider Job.
    • Combinations: a forecast of what today's spells will have done when the attacks land (spells resolve before
      attacks, GDD 3.7): stat changes, damage, stuns (a stunned enemy is no threat), Exposed (+1 a hit), Heat, Untouchable.
        - Attacks are planned against the forecast: buff, then hit harder; stun or debuff, then kill.
        - Spells are picked one at a time, best first, each weighed knowing the ones before. Among themselves spells
          resolve in random order, so one spell counts on another only half the time (Heat, then random damage).
        - Combos across Days need nothing special: Heat, Doomed, Burn and Blessed stay, and it reads them.
        - Instant spells resolve as they're played: Revealed from Tapped Communications counts at once, so a "destroy it if
          it's Revealed" spell (Perfectly Timed Camera Malfunction) can follow the same Day. Holding one, Hard values
          revealing a big enemy creature as nearly killing it.
    • Gifts and assassins: it plants Gifts in empty enemy slots and never attacks its own planted Gift; given one, it
      summons over it first. Wet Work (Extreme Prejudice) is kept for the biggest threat nothing else can kill.
    • Black Budget (2026-10-13): requisites as far as it can see (a free slot beside the target, outnumbered, Attack N or
      less); "the strongest" as it sees them; taking control is worth the creature twice (they lose it, we gain it); its own
      Last Wills count when it sacrifices a creature (False Flag Operation on Dead Man's Switch); Kill Order and Brainwashed
      on theirs; buffs and statuses moved between two creatures (Funnel, Frame Job, Devil's Breath); back to the hand,
      fake deaths and going dark save a creature that would die; reveals cost it what When Revealed strikes back with, less
      what Allergic to Light burns; a Fall Guy takes its Destroys; decoys look like their fake card; Freak Weather is
      worth its lightning. Cards with a discard cost go last, paid with what it wasn't going to play.
    • Hidden from it: a Kill Order on its own creatures (tested: FogOfWarTests).
    • Protection: Armor, healing, Untouchable and Shields are worth the whole creature when they save one the
      other side could kill next Day; Bodyguard guards the one most at risk.
    • Attacks: lethal if it has it. Otherwise the race: if it would bring them to 0 in fewer Days than they would it, it
      goes for the player and only stops real threats. If not, it kills the biggest threats, each with the smallest
      attacker (or pair) that does it, and sends the rest at the player.
    • Moves: a creature with nothing to do leaves a Scorched Earth slot, or takes cover on a free Shield.

Very Hard: Hard, plus the long game (2026-10-14), the cards that pay off on a later Day. Only this level does it:
    • Lost creatures: one of its own that's Doomed, Brainwashed, or on Borrowed Time at 0 Health counts as lost even
      if nothing kills it today, so Extraction, Witness Protection Program and He Didn't Kill Himself rescue it (a fresh
      card, its statuses gone), and Frame Job passes the Doom or the brainwashing to the creature facing it.
    • Slow kills (Kill Order, Brainwashed) go where its attacks can't reach: worth the whole creature on one too tough
      to kill today, a third of it on one it could kill anyway, nothing on one already on its way out.
    • Condemned enemies: a creature of theirs it gave a Kill Order, or Brainwashed to come over, isn't attacked or hit
      by its damage spells (unless it must defend): it's dead, or its own, already.
    Very Hard beats Hard 65 - 51 (130 games, every deck against itself, both seats; 14 draws). The balance study plays
    Very Hard on both sides; the tournaments keep their levels and styles.


4. THE HARD STYLES (weights; 1 is the plain Hard AI)

    Balanced    1 everywhere.
    Rusher      Aggro: player damage 1.5, own Health 0.8 (spent), removal 0.9, protection 0.8, races 1.5x as eagerly.
    Controller  Control: removal 1.6, statuses 1.3, player damage 0.7, races late (0.6).
    Swarm       Wide: its board 1.6, cards 1.2, removal 0.8, races a little early (1.2).
    Hexer       Statuses and debuffs 2, removal 1.2, player damage 0.9.
    Bastion     Defence: protection 2, own Health 2, its board 1.3, player damage 0.8, races late (0.7).
    Spymaster   Intelligence 3 (Reveals, Watchdogs, Concealed, Encrypted), cards 1.3, statuses 1.2.
    Engine      Cards 2, its board 1.2, own Health 0.5 (pays Health for cards).
The same planner does it all: a new style is a new set of numbers. Rusher started at player damage 2 and races 2x; it
tied Medium, so it was toned down.


5. HOW STRONG THEY ARE
The levels (660 games: each faction deck against itself, both seats):
    Hard beats Medium 112 - 66, Easy 176 - 27; Medium beats Easy 155 - 39.
    Overall: Hard wins 65% of its games, Medium 50%, Easy 15%.
The Hard styles and Medium (1584 games, every pairing):
    Spymaster 50%, Hexer 47%, Controller 42%, Balanced 41%, Rusher 40%, Engine 40%, Bastion 39%, Swarm 37%, Medium 24%.
    Every style beats Medium; Spymaster beats Engine 27 - 6, Hexer beats Spymaster 20 - 14.
    Reading for balance: Watchdogs, Reveals and statuses win the most games when an AI values them highly, so they may be
    strong for what they cost; Swarm (going wide) and Bastion (turtling) are the weakest plans.
    Best AI per deck: Zeitgeist, Mind and Corporate with Spymaster; Digital Society with Hexer; Myth with Bastion;
    Utopia and Shadow Ops. with Controller; Panopticon with Swarm (see the report).
A draw is a combat called off after 80 Days (40 until 2026-10-17, when about 1 game in 5 between Hard styles was one), or both at 0 at once.


6. BUGS FOUND BY THE AI GAMES (GameAudit)
After every Day of every tournament game the audit checks:
    • No card is lost, gained, or in two places.
    • Slots and creatures agree on where each creature is, and no creature is in two slots.
    • No dead or 0-Health creature is left on the board, and no creature has negative Attack.
    • Health stays within bounds, and hands within the maximum.
    • The queue is empty and the Day moved on.
    • The AI only planned cards from its own hand.
It also collects every error, exception and warning the game logs. Found so far:
    • A card duplicated (fixed). A creature replaced by a summon left play while an effect that had already picked it
      (Football Hooligans' death damage) was still waiting; the effect then destroyed it again and it was discarded twice.
      Now a replaced creature's plans leave with it, and damage or destruction aimed at a creature no longer in play fizzles.
      Covered in GameplayTests (Board_PositionsDecideMovesReplacementsAndChains).
    • Warnings on almost every game (fixed). A status or stat change landing on a creature that died earlier in the same
      action (Underdog's death buff on itself, Burn after the damage that killed) now fizzles quietly, as the GDD says.
    • "0 damage to a player" warnings (fixed): an attacker brought to 0 Attack is a normal no-op, now a plain log.
The latest runs: no problems in 2244 games.


7. IMPERFECT INFORMATION: HOW IT'S KEPT
• Built in: the planner only gets an AIView. Of the other side it gets SeenCreature/SeenSlot objects with nothing filled in
  that the seat can't see. (They keep the real creature and slot only to aim at them; the planner reads nothing else.)
• The forecast only holds what this side's own spells will do.
• Tested (FogOfWarTests: TheAI_AtEveryLevel_PlansTheSame_WhateverItCantSee): two games look the same to player 1, but in
  one, player 2 has a different hand and plans, hidden Landmines, and a 9/9 where the other has a 2/3, both unknown.
  Every level must plan the same Day in both. Once the 9/9 is revealed, Medium and Hard must plan differently, so it
  does use what it sees. Planting a leak on purpose (letting it see unknown creatures) makes this test fail.
• Random choices (Easy) use the planner's own seeded random numbers, never the game's, so the shuffles stay the same.
