Adventure Map

How the adventure's map is built, after Slay the Spire 2's (researched 2026-10-15 from the fan references Spire Codex and
the Slay the Spire wiki, which read the game's data; the path drawing itself isn't published for the sequel, so it follows
the first game's, well documented). Code: Assets/Scripts/Adventure/AdventureMap.cs.


1. WHAT SLAY THE SPIRE 2 DOES

• Size. A 7-column grid. Act 1 has 15 rooms to choose (17 floors with the Ancient at the start and the boss), Act 2 has 14,
  Act 3 13: the run speeds up.
• Set counts, not percentages (the first game rolled each room's type; the sequel counts them, so no map is starved of
  elites or shops): 5 elites (8 from Ascension 1), 3 shops, 10-14 unknown rooms in Act 1 (9-13 later), 6-7 rest sites in
  Acts 1-2 (5-6 in Act 3); the rest are fights.
• Fixed rows. The first is all fights (the easy pool: the first 3 fights of Act 1, 2 of the others). The row 7 from the end
  is all treasure. The last is all rest sites, so there's always a rest before the boss. No elite or rest site in the first
  5 rows.
• Unknown rooms decide when you walk in: a fight 10% (+10% each time it doesn't come up), a shop 3% (+3%), a treasure 2%
  (+2%), otherwise an event. What comes up drops back to its base; it all resets each act. Never an elite unless a modifier
  says so.
• Every act opens with an Ancient: a full heal and a choice of special relics.

From the first game (likely still the basis):
• Six paths drawn from the bottom up, each step to one of the 3 nearest rooms above; the first two start in different
  rooms; paths never cross; rooms no path reaches are removed.
• An elite, shop or rest site never leads straight into another of its kind.
• Where a path forks, the branches hold different kinds of room.
• No rest site in the row before the last (it's all rest sites).


2. THE IDEAS BEHIND IT

• The whole map is shown at once: the game is in planning a route, risk against reward.
• Every fork is a real choice (the branches differ).
• Safe rooms never stack (no rest after rest, no shop after shop) and danger is dosed (no elite after elite).
• A gentle start (easy fights only), anchors at set points (treasure at the middle, rest before the boss).
• Unknown rooms correct themselves: their odds rise until something other than an event comes up.
• Counts over percentages: maps vary in shape, not in quality.


3. WHAT OURS DOES NOW (2026-10-15)

• One act of 15 rows and the boss above them; no Ascension, no Ancient yet.
• The paths, the fixed rows and the placement rules as above; counts: 5 elites, 3 shops, 6 rest sites, 10-14 unknown rooms,
  and fights for every other room, in one bag the size of the map (as Slay the Spire does); each room draws one at random
  among those it may hold. Fixed 2026-10-15 (the user: rest sites didn't follow Slay the Spire): the bag held only the
  special rooms and each room took the first allowed, so the first rows used them up, elites and rest sites piled onto
  row 5 and the top of the map was all fights. Now they spread evenly over rows 5-13 (200 maps, no rule broken).
• Fights, elites and the boss are matches against the AI (Easy for the fights of the first rows, then Medium; Hard for an
  elite, Very Hard for the boss). Winning one brings you back to the map a room further; losing one ends the run;
  beating the boss wins it.
• Rest sites, shops, treasure and unknown rooms are walked through: nothing in them yet. (Since then: shops and events
  2026-10-16; rest sites, treasure, relics, potions and card upgrades 2026-10-16, see 6.)
• Not carried between fights yet: your Health (each match starts at full).


4. THE DECKS (the user, 2026-10-15; AdventureDecks.cs)

• Your starter deck, Slay the Spire style: 12 plain cards of your faction, two simple creatures (4 and 3 copies), a
  spell that hits (3) and one that helps (2). No big strategy to learn.
• Tiers: every card gets a rough power score from its stats, effects and costs, and is ranked against the other cards
  of its kind (creatures and spells apart): D the bottom fifth, C, B, A, S the top tenth.
• The enemy's deck is made for its room, of another faction. Strength 0 to 1 by row (an elite more, the boss 1):
    - The first fights: Tier C/D cards, and clumsy on top: some bloated (30 to 40 cards, a little of everything, nothing
      to build momentum on), the mix of creatures and spells off (a quarter, or nine tenths, creatures).
    - Further up: better tiers, about 3 creatures to 2 spells, the best cards in several copies, 18 to 22 cards.
• After a fight won (not the boss), Slay the Spire's rewards screen: a card pack ("Add a card to your deck"; an elite drops
  two, with different cards). Open one to see three cards of the deck you beat; click one to take it, Skip to leave them,
  or Back to think and open it again later. View deck shows your whole deck, from the rewards and from the map. Proceed
  goes on to the map; packs not opened are left behind. Your deck grows up to 40 cards.
• Decks everywhere: at most 40 cards, copies no longer limited.


6. RELICS, POTIONS, REST SITES AND UPGRADES (the user, 2026-10-16: "implement all of your ideas", from Slay the Spire)

• Rest sites: Sleep (+20 Health) or Train (upgrade a card of your deck). One or the other.
• Card upgrades (CardUpgrades.cs): the card's "+" version, one better number: a creature +1 Attack and +1 Health; a spell 1
  more on each of its numbers (damage, healing, Armor, draws, stat changes, stacks). Once per card; it lives in the run only.
• Treasure rooms: a relic you don't have yet and 20 EuroYuans.
• Elites drop a relic on top of their two packs.
• Relics (Relics.cs), each one rule that's always on, named after the Brainstorm List's Neutral Resources:
    First Aid Kit (heal 6 after each fight won), Double Headed Coin (each fight pays 5 more), Credit Card (shop prices a
    quarter off), Backpack (packs show 4 cards), Air Fryer (each Dawn your most hurt creature heals 2), Taser (a creature that
    attacks one of yours takes 1), Coffee (your first creature each fight arrives ready), Kevlar (your creatures enter with
    2 Armor), Protein Shake (your creatures enter with +1 Health), Memo Note (one more spell in your opening hand).
• Potions: Single Use spells, 3 at most, in your opening hand each fight, not counted among your spells; one not used stays.
  Fights drop one 40% of the time; shops sell two at 12. Nicotine (draw 2), Stimulant Drug (+2 Attack this Day), Superglue
  (+5 Armor), Soylent (heal 6), Flamethrower (4 damage), Eject Button (a creature of yours back to your hand), Stealth Suit
  (Conceal 2 on your creatures), Creatine (+1/+1).
• The map's side panel lists your relics and potions; the rewards screen says what a fight gave besides cards.
• The AI adventure simulation does all of this too: it sleeps below 40 Health and trains above, buys a potion at shops.
