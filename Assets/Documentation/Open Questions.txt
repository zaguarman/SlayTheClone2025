45. The Kübler-Ross model as statuses (the user, 2026-10-15)
    • The ask: all five stages of grief as status effects. Proposed, nothing built yet; each lasts N Days unless it says so.
        - Denial: the damage it takes is held back, not felt; it all lands at once when Denial ends ("It's fine.").
          Collides a little with Borrowed Time (can't die while it lasts): Denial delays the damage, it doesn't stop dying.
        - Anger: +2 Attack, but it can't be healed or given Armor.
        - Bargaining: a hit that would kill it leaves it at 1 Health, and its controller discards a random card for the deal
          (each time). Collides with Survivor (once, from full Health, free): Bargaining works any time, and costs a card.
        - Depression: its Attack and Speed are halved (rounded down), and it can't get buffs.
        - Acceptance: no other status can land on it; landing, it ends the other four stages.
    • A chain to tie them together: one card ("Five Stages of Grief") gives Denial, and each stage, ending, gives the next
      (Denial, Anger, Bargaining, Depression, Acceptance), so a creature goes through all of them.
    • Faction: Mind (the psyche) fits best; Zeitgeist (moods of modern life) for a meme take.
    • To decide: the five effects, whether the chain is in, and the faction.

44. Corporate: hustle, leveraging losses, tempo, and the hierarchy (the user, 2026-10-15)
    • The direction: we're in a rush, be a hustler, "work hard and you can become a billionaire". Corporate turns its losses
      into gains and builds tempo; the hierarchy is the other half, and how to play it is still to decide.
    • Done so far: C-Suite, the top of the ladder, with Collateral Damage (its attack's leftover hits the player); Laissez-Faire
      and The Invisible Hand move Attack up to the strongest (the fake free market). Corporate already pays with discards.
    • Leveraging losses, ideas: a Write-Off (when a friendly creature dies, draw or +Attack to the strongest: the loss is
      deductible); Unpaid Intern and Inhuman Resources already pay out on death or damage; Dead Cat Bounce, Margin Call.
    • The hierarchy, options to choose from:
        a) Ranks by Attack: your strongest creature is the Boss, read live (like Strongest). Cards say "your Boss" and
           "your staff" (the others): staff effects feed the Boss (the Invisible Hand already does), the Boss's effects
           pay the staff a little. Nothing new to track, nothing to place: it reads off the board.
        b) Printed ranks (Intern, Worker, Manager, Executive) as a line on the card; a Manager buffs the Workers, an
           Executive takes credit (Credit Grab) from the Managers. Richer, but a new stat to read and balance.
        c) The chain of command by slot: the leftmost is the top; effects run down the row. Positional, but it fights with
           moving and pushing.
      Recommended: a), the Boss as "your strongest", since it's streamlined and already half built.
    • To decide: which hierarchy, and which loss-leveraging cards to make first.
    • The formation (the user, 2026-10-15, after thinking it over): five slots a side in three rows, as in a real war. Ours
      a Λ (the enemy's a V): 1 at the front, 2 in the middle, 2 at the back.
        - Front (1): the defensive wall, takes the melee. Name to pick (Frontline is the user's working name).
        - Flanks (2, the middle): the attackers, ranged positions. +1 Attack and +1 Speed while there.
        - Headquarters (2, the back): the important people (presidents, CEOs, the Pope), hard to reach. Bonus: to decide.
      Reach: distance = how deep each slot sits (front 0, Flanks 1, Headquarters 2), added, plus 1. Melee: 1 (front to front).
      Ranged: 2-3. Artillery: exactly 4 (Flanks to their Headquarters). Attacking an empty slot hits the player.
      Moving: one row forward or back, or along the same row; the back rows aren't blocked by the ones ahead.
      The hierarchy (Chain of Command for Corporate and Myth, rank by slot) waits for the slot names: to discuss later.
      Front slot bonus suggested: +1 Armor each Dawn (at most 2); Headquarters: none (being hard to reach is its guard).
    • Decided (the user, 2026-10-15): the names are Vanguard (front, 1), Flanks (middle, 2), Headquarters (back, 2). The slot
      decides the reach: the Vanguard melees (distance 1: their Vanguard); the Flanks and Headquarters shoot (distance 2-3:
      the Flanks reach their Vanguard and Flanks, Headquarters their Vanguard). Artillery, a creature keyword, overrides it:
      exactly distance 4, never less (Flanks to their Headquarters, Headquarters to their Flanks). Bonuses: Vanguard +1
      Armor each Dawn (at most 2), Flanks +1 Attack and +1 Speed while there, Headquarters none. Built 2026-10-17 (GDD 2.4).

43. Speed ties: what the order numbers show, and who wins a tie (the user, 2026-10-15)
    • Now (GDD 3.7, ActionsQueue.CompareInLayer): in a layer, higher Speed first (Rush Hour: lower). At the same Speed, one
      side's actions go in the order its player planned them; between the two sides, the lower Health + Armor first, then
      the lower printed Attack, then a coin flip drawn once when it's queued. Moves go in planned order, spells at random.
      The screen numbers attacks by Speed, each side counting its own ties in planned order, the two sides sharing numbers
      (your two Knights 2 and 3, their two Basilisks 2 and 3): it shows the enemy's planned order.
    • The user's proposal: number by Speed alone (all four "2"), the game settles the tie.
        A) Display only: same Speed, same number; the rules stay (your own ties still go as planned, unseen).
        B) Display and rules: drop "your ties as planned"; every tie goes Health + Armor, then Attack, then the coin.
      Recommended: B, one tie rule for everyone, and the number says exactly what you know. Keep Health + Armor (stable and
      readable) or go straight to the coin flip: the user's call.
    • Decided (the user, 2026-10-15): B. Every tie, your own included, goes Health + Armor, then printed Attack, then the
      coin flip; the planned order plays no part. The arrows number by Speed alone: all attacks at the same Speed share a
      number, both sides. Health + Armor stays the first tie-breaker. Built.

42. Reach: a cone by default, Ranged and Artillery (an idea, 2026-10-14, not built)
    • Now: a creature can attack any enemy slot. The idea: by default it only reaches a cone, the slot facing it and the two
      beside that one (columns i-1, i, i+1). Ranged reaches any slot. Artillery (short range) reaches only the slots two
      columns away (i-2 and i+2), never what's in front of it:
          S A S S S          A S S S A
          S S S C S          S S C S S
    • What it would change: positioning starts to matter for attacks too (today it matters for moves, Bodyguard, Heat,
      neighbours). A creature at the end of the row covers 2 slots, one in the middle 3. Moving becomes a way to reach a
      target, and the empty slot in front of a creature is a hole it can't cover from afar. Artillery wants a wall in front:
      it can't hit what faces it, so it pairs with a sturdy creature that can.
    • Creatures that would fit Ranged: Lone Gunman (a sniper), Keyboard Warrior (reaches anyone online), Man o' War (naval
      guns), Golden Dome (missile defence), Particle Accelerator (a beam), Omniscient Space-Time Bending Bearded Wizard,
      Surveillance Giraffe (sees over everything). Artillery: Sherman Tank, Man o' War (a broadside, if not Ranged),
      Mercenaries (mortars). The Chosen One's Spray and Pray already hits at random.
    • Decided (the user, 2026-10-15): yes. Melee (the default): the slot in front and its sides. Ranged: 1 to 2 slots away.
      Artillery: only 3 slots away. To build with the new formation (see 44).
    • Was to decide: whether the cone becomes the default for every creature (it changes every faction's balance, the AI's
      attack choice and the drag hints: the slots out of reach would grey out while you drag), or only some creatures get
      a limited reach. Recommended: the cone for everyone, Ranged and Artillery as keywords, built together, then a round
      robin to rebalance by faction.

40. The balance reports' credit, and Discard (2026-10-14)
    • Built as asked: the AI games now credit the cards that set things up, not only the ones that dealt the blow. Assists:
      damage and kills by a creature carrying another card's effect (a Licence to Kill, a power-up, Die Another Day), or on a
      victim weakened by your side's cards (GoldenEye's Blackout, Generational Trauma). Opened: player damage through a slot a
      card emptied, in the two Days after. Logistics and support: cards drawn, sent for, denied to the enemy (Supply Chain
      Attack), Health healed. One impact score weighs it all in damage, logistics weighed up (a card drawn, sent for or
      denied counts as 2 damage, a kill as 4). RunMatchup ranks a deck's cards by it; the Balance Report has a support table.
    • Answered (Discard): a discard cost is a payment, paid in full or not at all (with fewer cards the card isn't played
      and stays in the hand). Chaotic cards pay at random (Social Engineering, Bilderberg Membership); calculated ones let
      you pick the cards (Headhunter, NDA, Dead Drop: a picker opens when you play it, the AI gives up what it values least).
      Which way a card pays is also a balancing lever. A card whose effect is to discard (not to pay) discards as many as you
      have, up to X: in the rules and the keyword, no card does it yet.
    • Answered: no third way to pay; random and picked by hand stay. Fixed rules (neighbours in the hand, newest first)
      would add steps and juggling: play should stay streamlined. Kept as an option for later: a card can ask for a kind
      of card as its payment (creatures, spells, or any card).
