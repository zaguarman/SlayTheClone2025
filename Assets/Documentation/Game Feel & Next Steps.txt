GAME FEEL & NEXT STEPS (brainstorm, 2026-10-13)

Where we are: the rules engine is deep (layers, Speed, Watchdogs, Fog of War, Slot Effects, statuses, abilities, keywords),
there are 8 decks and 128 cards with art, a menu, a gallery and a queue panel. What's missing is mostly what makes it FEEL
like a game: an opponent that plays back, feedback when things happen, and a reason to play again (a run).
Ideas are marked [S] small, [M] medium, [L] large.


1. GAME FEEL: MAKE EVERY ACTION LAND

The Day resolves as a list of actions. That's the show: each one should read, hit and leave a mark.

Resolution playback
• [M] Pace by importance: moves and buffs quick (0.2s), attacks and deaths slow (0.5s), a beat of silence before Last Rites. A "fast forward" button and hold-to-skip.
• [S] Spotlight the actor: dim the table, light the creature acting and its target, then return. One thing on screen at a time.
• [M] Impact frames: a 2-3 frame freeze on hits, the card squashes and shakes in proportion to the damage, a small screen shake on big hits and player damage only.
• [S] Floating numbers: red damage, green heals, blue Armor absorbed, grey "0" when Shield or Armor eats it all. Crits... we don't have crits, so make Exposed's +1 a visible "+1".
• [S] The queue panel as a timeline: a strip along the top showing the next 5 actions as icons, the current one highlighted, so the player sees "what's next" without opening anything.
• [M] Watchdogs as a reveal moment: when one triggers, the action freezes, the Watchdog card flips up from the side ("NULLIFIED" stamp in red), then the action shatters or bends to its new target.

Planning (the half of the game the player spends the most time in)
• [M] Outcome preview: while dragging an attack, show predicted damage on the target ("-3, dies") and the counter-damage from Counterattack. Slay the Spire's intent numbers are the gold standard.
• [S] Intent pips on enemy creatures (sword, boot, sparkle) with their numbers, and "???" for Concealed ones. The arrows are there, but pips are faster to read.
• [S] Hover a queued action in the queue panel to highlight its arrow and cards on the board, and the other way round.
• [M] Undo the last planning action (Ctrl+Z) until End Turn.
• [S] End Turn warns when a creature has no plan ("2 creatures are idle. End anyway?") and can be turned off.

Card handling
• [S] Hand fan and lift: cards fan out, the hovered one rises and the neighbours slide aside (Hearthstone, Slay the Spire). Drag tilts the card toward the movement.
• [S] Draw and discard animations at Dawn and Dusk: cards fly from the deck pile, the hand fans out one by one.
• [S] Summon arrival: the creature drops into the slot with a dust puff; Deployment Time shown as a ticking clock that pops when ready.
• [M] Deaths: Last Will effects flash their icon as the card burns away; Doomed creatures get a cracked frame that spreads each Dawn.

Time of day
• [M] Dawn and Dusk as lighting: the table warms at Dawn and turns blue-violet at Dusk, the river line glows. A banner "DAY 3 — DAWN" like Inscryption's chapter cards. It teaches the Dawn/Dusk keywords for free.

Sound (none yet; cheap to add, huge effect)
• [M] A small set: card pick/drop/hover, attack hit (light/heavy), heal chime, armor clank, status apply (one per status family), Watchdog alarm, death, Dawn rooster/Dusk bell, End Turn stamp.
• [S] Faction stingers: a 2-second motif when a faction's creature arrives (Corporate cash register, Statecraft trumpet, Zeitgeist vaporwave chord, Myth choir, Tech modem...).

Humour as feel (the satire is our identity: make it talk)
• [M] Opponent barks: each menu opponent gets a few lines on events (Your Manager: "Per my last email." when it nullifies; Your Gaslighting Ex: "That never happened." on a Redirect; The Megachurch: "Your donation is appreciated." when it draws).
• [S] Headlines: at Dusk, a newspaper-style ticker summarising the Day ("INTERN DIES, COMPANY DRAWS 1 CARD", "LOCAL GIRAFFE SEES EVERYTHING").
• [S] Death quips: a one-liner from the card's flavour when it dies.
• [S] The Card Gallery could show each card's art description and design notes on click (they're already on the assets).


2. NEXT STEPS (roughly in order)

A. An opponent that plays back [L] — the biggest blocker
• DONE (2026-10-13): three levels, Easy, Medium and Hard (Scripts/AI; how they work in 'AI Design'). They play under Fog of War through AIView, tested against leaks. They plug into either seat (match setup: You vs AI / Hot seat / AI vs AI, and the AI level; F7 in game). Debug > AI Tournament plays them against each other with every deck ('AI Tournament.txt'): Hard beats Medium about 2 to 1 and Easy 9 to 1. Hard has eight styles (Rusher, Controller, Swarm, Hexer, Bastion, Spymaster, Engine, Balanced) and plays combinations (Debug > AI Style Tournament, 'AI Styles.txt'); every tournament game is audited for broken rules, which found and fixed a card-duplication bug.
• Not yet: see 'AI Design' 6.
• GDD 6 says the AI has no hand: it plays a script of intents. Start there: each opponent gets a deck + a simple scripted policy (play creatures in empty slots, aim spells at the biggest threat, attack the slot in front, keep Watchdogs for when it has 2+ creatures).
• It must play under Fog of War (Intel already enforces this for Watchdogs): it reacts only to what it can see.
• Then: personalities per opponent (aggressive, defensive, trap-heavy) via weights, not new code.

B. Balance from simulation [M]
• We already have a test that plays every deck for 3 turns. Turn it into an AI vs AI tournament (100 games per pair) that writes win rates, average game length and the cards that were played but never mattered.
• Use it to tune the new two-faction decks and Utopia, which nobody has played yet.

C. A run (the roguelike part) [L]
• A map of encounters like Slay the Spire's: fights against faction groups (GDD 6: mostly one faction plus a splash), elites, a boss per act.
• Rewards: pick 1 of 3 cards (biased to the factions you meet, so "steal their tools to beat the next fight" works), Health, gold.
• The content docs still missing from the GDD review (#24): Enemy, Player Character, Relic / Stock / Subscription compendiums. Relics as "Stocks" (they gain or lose value with the Day) and "Subscriptions" (pay each act) fit the satire.
• Events: short text encounters with choices, written in the same cynical voice (a TED talk, a team-building retreat, a crypto bro).

D. The faction mechanics still waiting [M each]
• Investments and Contracts (Corporate), Laws as Locations (Statecraft), Recruitment and Brainwashed (needs Owner vs Controller), Revive and Banish (Myth / Shadow Ops.), Evolution (Utopia), Prophecy (Myth), Trends and Strikes (Zeitgeist), Doxxing (Digital Society).
• Each one turns a dozen "Future:" design notes into real cards. Pick by which decks feel flattest after B.

E. Onboarding [M]
• A first match against a scripted tutorial opponent that teaches: plan, the queue order, Speed, a Watchdog, Fog of War. One concept per Day.
• Keywords already explain themselves; add a "?" on the queue panel and on the Dawn/Dusk banner.

F. Polish and production [M]
• Settings (volume, speed of playback, fog debug off for players), a save of the last run, a build that runs outside the editor.
• Art pass: the SVG pipeline works; next is a consistent frame per faction and a few hand-drawn hero illustrations for bosses.

Open decisions that block some of this (see 'Open Questions'): own decks vs pairs for the small factions, aimed spells limited to a side, keyword names, and Encrypted's faction.


3. SUGGESTED NEXT 3 STEPS
1. Sound + resolution playback pacing + floating numbers (one session; the biggest feel improvement for the effort).
2. A scripted AI opponent good enough to lose to: a first version is in (see A); next, moves and personalities.
3. The AI vs AI balance tournament: the harness is in (AITournament); next, a deck-against-deck table with Hard on both sides, then fix the worst decks.


NOTES FROM THE USER (2026-10-15)

• Card visual effects like the Pokemon cards in the mobile games (Pokemon TCG Pocket): crisp, with glitter, holographic
  sheen and reflections that move with the card as it tilts. To try for rare cards first, then for every card.
• Creatures are subjects: something you could point at. Schrodinger's Cat is a cat, the Ship of Theseus is a ship.
  Abstract things (the Scientific Method, a theory, a fallacy) are spells, Watchdogs, Trends or Slot Effects instead.
  To review: the creatures that are abstract today, to turn into spells or rename.
• Planning ahead for the next draw (Lucid Dreaming, the Send For cards, delayed draws): two of them played the same Day can
  pick the same card. A card picked is set aside at once (stashed) so the next one can't pick it too.

  and a queued spell glowing on the bottom side. Not reproduced in an AI-vs-AI game. Not a slot highlight (those draw
