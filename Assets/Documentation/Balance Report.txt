WHY THE BOTTOM DECKS LOSE
Three causes, the same as in the first report, and they stack:
  a) No board-wide damage. The decks on top win with one or two cards that hit every creature on the other side:
     Mutually Assured Destruction (30.5 damage a game), Clickbait (32.3), Margin Call (20.5), This Is Fine (26.9), Black
     Hole (27.2). Myth, Prosperity Gospel and Plausible Deniability have none, so they trade one creature for one creature
     against decks that wipe their whole row.
  b) Slow creatures. Utopia's creatures need 1.9 Days on average before they act, Myth's 1.1, Prosperity Gospel's 1.3:
     they're killed by those sweepers before they do anything. The best decks either deploy at 0 (the three Shadow Ops.
     decks on top, Digital Society) or are sturdy enough to survive the wait (Statecraft: 4.5 Health on average).
  c) Cards that don't touch the board. Plausible Deniability plays 12 to 14 information cards a game (Smoke And Mirrors,
     Burn Notice, Eavesdrop) that change what's seen, not what's alive. Myth's spells (Exorcism, Memento Mori, Monkey Hand,
     The Holy Grail, Excess of Bread and Wine) protect or heal, and deal no damage at all.

1. THE SHORT VERSION

• The decks are far apart. Statecraft (95% of its games won) and Corporate (90%) beat everything except each other; Digital
  Society (80%) beats everyone else and loses every game to those two. Shadow Ops. wins 7%, Prosperity Gospel 22%, Myth 23%.
• The decks that win are the ones with the most board-wide damage. With no costs every card in hand is played each Day,
  so a card is worth its raw effect, and "damage to every creature" cards outclass everything else.
• The winning strategies value statuses, debuffs and information (Spymaster 50%, Hexer 47% of their games); aggro (Rusher
  40%), going wide (Swarm 37%) and turtling (Bastion 39%) do worst.
• The cards that carry the strongest decks: Mutually Assured Destruction, Margin Call, Hostile Takeover, Clickbait.
  The cards that win mirror matches: Dogpile, Clickbait, Trolley Problem, Red Herring, Taxpayer Funds Venture Capital
  Profits, This Is Fine.
• Deployment Time is very expensive right now: Fat Tony (Deployment Time 2) died before acting in every game traced.


2. THE MOST WINNING STRATEGIES

By style (each deck against itself, so only the way of playing differs):
    Spymaster 50%, Hexer 47%, Controller 42%, plain Hard 41%, Rusher 40%, Engine 40%, Bastion 39%, Swarm 37%, Medium 24%.
• Spymaster and Hexer weigh statuses, debuffs and information two to three times as much as the plain AI. They win more,
  so those effects are worth more than their face value suggests: a stun or -2 Attack at the right creature swings a Day.
• Rusher (go face) loses: with the whole hand played every Day, the board refills fast, and ignoring it lets the other
  side's creatures and removal take over. Swarm (go wide) loses to board-wide damage. Bastion (protect) can't stop it either.
• The styles that do best play the "everyone gets it" spells (Climate Change, Kakistocracy, Absolute Zero) two to four
  times as often as the plain AI. Those cards don't win by themselves (low edge, below); the styles win through their
  targeted debuffs.

By deck (deck against deck, Hard on both sides):
    Statecraft 95%   Corporate 90%   Digital Society 80%   Tech 57%   Mind 52%   Zeitgeist 48%   Utopia 37%
    Panopticon 27%   Myth 23%   Prosperity Gospel 22%   Shadow Ops. 7%
• A rock-paper-scissors at the top:
    - Statecraft and Corporate beat Digital Society 6 - 0: their 2 and 3 damage to every creature wipe its 2.7-Health board.
    - Digital Society beats everyone else 6 - 0: Clickbait and Dogpile ping every enemy creature, every Day.
    - Statecraft and Corporate split their games 3 - 3.
• What the top decks are made of:
    - Statecraft has the toughest creatures (2.1 Attack / 4.5 Health on average), and Mutually Assured Destruction
      (3 to every creature and 3 to each player) kills the other side's board while its own survives.
    - Corporate: Margin Call (2 to every creature, and draw 2) and Hostile Takeover (4 damage) remove a board a Day.
• Why the bottom decks lose:
    - Shadow Ops.: weak bodies (1.3/3.8) and spells that only show or hide things (Eavesdrop, Tapped Communications, Smoke
      And Mirrors) or set traps; its best damage dealer does 5.7 a game, against 37 for Clickbait.
    - Myth and Prosperity Gospel: slow creatures (Deployment Time 1.1 to 1.3 on average) that deal little (Mummy, Martyr:
      about 6 a game).
    - Utopia: the slowest deck (Deployment Time 1.9), saved somewhat by Black Hole.
    - Panopticon wins less than Digital Society alone (27% against 80%): its Shadow Ops. half dilutes the pings.
    - Caveat: the AI values information modestly. A player who uses Shadow Ops.' intel well will do better than 7%, but
      not enough to close a gap this wide.

4. SUGGESTIONS (nothing changed yet: your call)

The biggest lever: costs. With no mana every card in the hand is played every Day, so the only knob is each card's raw
effect. The GDD's Faction Costs and additional costs (discards, sacrifices, Requisites) would give the strong cards a
price. Until then, by card:
• Clickbait: 1 to EACH enemy creature, every Day, is the best card in the game. Make it 1 damage to a random enemy creature
  and the ones beside it, or 1 to each enemy creature with 2 Health or less.
• Mutually Assured Destruction: the biggest swing (3 to every creature and each player), it also causes the double-KO draws.
  Make it 2, or creatures only.
• Margin Call: 2 to every creature AND draw 2. Drop the draw, or 1 damage.
• This Is Fine: +2 Health to yours before 2 to everyone makes it one-sided. Swap the order, or +1.
• Taxpayer Funds Venture Capital Profits: draw 2 and 2 to the enemy, no drawback. Give it one (2 damage to you, or the
  enemy draws 1).
• Statecraft's bodies (4.5 Health on average) are what make Mutually Assured Destruction one-sided: trim a few by 1.
• Shadow Ops.: give its intel cards a direct effect (Eavesdrop draws 1, Tapped Communications also Exposes), and its spies
  more teeth (Mole, Sleeper Agent: +1 Attack, or damage when revealed).
• Deployment Time: Fat Tony (2) and the slow Utopia and Myth creatures die before acting. Either lower it, or protect
  deploying creatures (Armor or Untouchable while they wait).
• 0-Attack creatures (Political Decoy, Meatproxy As A Service, Roko's Basilisk) and Day Trader need a reason to be played:
  more Health, or an effect that pays off.


5. HOW SURE THESE NUMBERS ARE
• The decks gap is certain: 6 - 0 results repeat across seeds and seats.
• Card edges over about 65% or under 35% are clear; between those, treat them as hints (60 to 130 games each, about +/-6%).
• The "what each style wins with" table in the data file is mostly noise (too few games per style and deck).
• It's how the Hard AI plays. A human will play some cards better (Watchdogs, Reveals, traps) and some worse.
• Player 1 won 171 games, Player 2 151: no meaningful seat advantage.
