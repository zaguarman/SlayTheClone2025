**I. INTRODUCTION AND CORE DEFINITIONS**

**I.A. General Gameplay Keywords:**

*   **Abilities Disabled:** A state where a creature's passive abilities do not trigger, and its active abilities cannot be activated by its controller. Effects that cause this state will specify its duration. **If a creature gains `Abilities Disabled` (e.g., via the `Muted` status) at any point during a Day's Action Resolution Phase, this status takes immediate effect. Any unresolved actions in the Action Queue originating from that creature (whether from passive abilities triggered earlier or active abilities queued during Planning) are nullified and will not resolve.** Furthermore, the creature cannot have new passive abilities trigger nor activate active abilities while this status is in effect. *(GDD Context: Section IV.F.3. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Combat, Damage & Targeting - Armor)*
*   **Armor:** A temporary Health point pool that takes damage before a creature's Health. Recoil damage also hits Armor first. Armor can be removed by specific effects. *(GDD Context: Section IV.F.3. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Combat, Damage & Targeting - Armor)*
*   **Banish:** Permanently removes a card (from hand, board, discard pile, or deck) from the game. Banished cards are outside all game zones and irretrievable. *(GDD Context: Section III.E.5. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Banish)*
*   **Cannot Act:** A state where a creature cannot have Attack actions or Active Abilities queued for it by its controller, nor can it perform them. Effects that cause this state will specify its duration. **If a creature gains `Cannot Act` during a Day's Action Resolution Phase, any unresolved Attack or Active Ability actions from that creature in the Action Queue are nullified and will not resolve.**
*   **Cleanse:** An action or effect that actively removes one or more status effects from a target. Specific cleanse effects will define which types of statuses they can remove. By default, Cleanse can remove both positive and negative cleansable status effects, unless the Cleanse effect itself specifies otherwise (e.g., "Cleanse 1 negative status"), or the status effect's definition in the 'Status Effects Compendium' details a special interaction with being targeted by a Cleanse action (e.g., `Burnout`). *(GDD Context: Section IV.C.4. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Creature Enhancement & Protection - Cleanse-ing; see individual Status Effect definitions in the 'Status Effects Compendium' for cleansability)*
*   **Concealed Action:** An action that is not visible to the opponent in any pre-resolution preview and appears as '???' in the opponent's Turn History upon resolution. Generated by the `Concealed` status effect (see 'Status Effects Compendium') or an `Undercover` ability. *(GDD Context: Section II. Core Concept & Vision - Key Mechanic: Fog of War)*
*   **Conversion:** An effect that allows a player to gain permanent control of an opponent's creature. *(GDD Context: Section V.D. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Recruitment / Conversion Effects)*
*   **Creature-Targeting:** An effect or action that targets a specific creature. If the targeted creature moves to a different slot before the action resolves, the action will still affect that same creature in its new location (homing). If the creature dies or otherwise Leaves Play before resolution, the effect redirects to the slot it last occupied. *(GDD Context: Section IV.F.5. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Combat, Damage & Targeting - Targeting Nuances)*
*   **Day:** A global period of play, consisting of a Dawn Phase (Start of Day), Planning Phase, Pre-Resolution Interference Phase, Action Resolution Phase, and Dusk Phase (End of Day). All players experience these phases simultaneously. Durations listed in "Days" (e.g., "lasts X Days") refer to these global Days. Thematically, a Day represents one cycle of game activity.
*   **Dawn (Start of Day):** The first phase of a global **Day**. Effects described as occurring "Each Dawn" or "at Dawn" resolve during this phase, after scheduled card draws for all players. Corresponds to the "Start of Turn" concept in traditional card games.
*   **Dusk (End of Day):** The final phase of a global **Day**. Effects described as occurring "Each Dusk" or "at Dusk" resolve during this phase, after the Action Resolution Phase and before the next **Day** begins. Corresponds to the "End of Turn" concept in traditional card games. Natural decay of status effect stacks typically occurs at Dusk (see Section V.B. for precise timing).
*   **Interference:** A category of pre-set effects that trigger during the Pre-Resolution Interference Phase to modify an opponent's action that is already pending in the Action Queue. Types include `Nullify (Interference)`, `Reorder (Interference)`, and `Redirect (Interference)`. *(GDD Context: Section IV.D.4. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Disruption & Control - Interference (Triggered Queue Modification))*
*   **Leaves Play:** A creature is removed from the battlefield through any means (e.g., destroyed by damage, direct destroy effect, Banished, returned to hand).
*   **Nullify (Interference):** An `Interference` effect that causes a specific opponent's pending action to be marked as "Nullified," resolving with no game effect. A Nullified action is considered to have been "Cancelled" for the purposes of triggering `OnActionCancelled`. *(GDD Context: Section IV.D.4. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Disruption & Control - Interference (Triggered Queue Modification); refer to 'Trigger Events List' for `OnActionCancelled`)*
*   **Priority (Action):** A value assigned to an action that determines its resolution order within the Action Queue, superseding creature Speed. Higher `Priority (Action)` actions resolve before lower `Priority (Action)` actions. *(GDD Context: Section III.F. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Action Queue & Turn Flow Mechanics)*
*   **Recruitment:** An effect that allows a player to gain temporary control of an opponent's creature. *(GDD Context: Section V.D. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Recruitment / Conversion Effects)*
*   **Reorder (Interference):** An `Interference` effect that alters a pending action's `Priority (Action)` or other properties influencing its resolution order.
*   **Redirect (Interference):** An `Interference` effect that changes the target of a specific opponent's pending action.
*   **Requisite:** A condition or set of conditions that must be met (e.g., sacrificing a creature, discarding cards) before a card can be played or an ability activated. Meeting Requisites is a Meta-Action handled during the planning phase. (See rules for Requisites in Section III.E.8. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Requisites and cost payment in Section III.H.3. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing - Payment of Additional Costs).
*   **Revive:** An ability or Spell that returns a creature card from a player's Discard Pile to an empty battlefield slot. *(GDD Context: Section IV.E.1. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Recursion & Recovery - Revive)*
*   **Scheduled Draw:** The primary mechanism for drawing cards, occurring at **Dawn (Start of Day)**. The number of cards drawn is based on a base amount plus modifiers. *(GDD Context: Section III.E.1. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Drawing Cards)*
*   **Slot-Targeting:** An effect or action that targets a specific battlefield slot. If a creature in that slot moves, the effect impacts whatever is in the slot at resolution. *(GDD Context: Section IV.F.5. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Combat, Damage & Targeting - Targeting Nuances)*
*   **Strategic Disclosure:** A beneficial effect that triggers for the player who successfully reveals an opponent's concealed information (via an Intelligence action or applying the `Revealed` status effect - see 'Status Effects Compendium'). *(GDD Context: Section V.A.1. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Traps & Strategic Disclosures - Strategic Disclosure Effects)*
*   **Trap (Counter-Intelligence):** A detrimental effect that triggers against an opponent when they reveal specific concealed information or interact with a trapped action/slot. *(GDD Context: Section V.A. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Traps & Strategic Disclosures)*
*   **Undercover (Ability):** A passive creature ability causing all actions queued by that creature to be `Concealed Actions`. *(GDD Context: see example in Section VII.B. Illustrative Content Examples - Example Passive Ability Concepts; also refer to `Concealed Action` in this section, Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords, and the `Concealed` status in 'Status Effects Compendium')*

**II. Core Concept & Vision**

**Premise:** A card game focused on outsmarting opponents via strategic planning, information control, and tactical positioning on a constrained battlefield.

**Genre:** Roguelite Deckbuilder. Players build and modify their deck during a run. Consequently, card choices and adapting to challenges are crucial.

**Central Conflict:** The core tension revolves around Intelligence versus Counter-Intelligence. Thematically, this is mirrored by a multifaceted conflict:

Established pyramidal powers (Statecraft, Myth, Corporate) strive to impose and maintain their specific paradigms of order and control. They often achieve this by manipulating information, enforcing dogma, and preserving hierarchical structures.

In contrast, decentralized, emergent forces (Digital Society, Zeitgeist, Mind) challenge these established norms. These forces thrive on fluidity, the free flow of (and sometimes weaponized) information, and the unpredictable power of collective or individual consciousness.

Navigating and exploiting this dynamic are clandestine networks and transformative systems (Shadow Ops., Tech, Utopia). These entities wield sophisticated tools of intelligence, counter-intelligence, disruption, and innovation. They may serve various masters, subvert existing powers, or pursue their own distinct agendas within this complex web of influence.

**Primary Goal:** Outsmart opponents by:

*   Gathering info on opponent's hidden plans.
*   Denying info to the opponent.
*   Controlling the global 'Location' effect (currently prototyped as 'Weather,' but will encompass various environmental or zone-wide states that fulfill the same role).
*   Creating/manipulating status effects on creatures and slots. (See 'Status Effects Compendium' for Status Effect definitions and 'Slot Effects Compendium').
*   Strategically moving creatures (friendly or enemy) for advantage.

**Key Interaction: Tactical Positioning**

*   **Battlefield:** Each player controls one row of 5 creature slots, facing each other symmetrically. Each slot has a controller, which is the player whose side of the board it is on.
*   **Slot Occupation:** A maximum of one creature can occupy each slot. Placement is therefore key. **A player's slots can only be occupied by creatures they control. If an opponent's creature moves to a player's slot via an effect like *Recruitment* or *Conversion*, control of
that creature changes to that player (see Section V.D. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Recruitment / Conversion Effects for *Recruitment* / *Conversion*). Thus, any creature occupying a player's slot is considered friendly to that player and under their control.**
*   **Adjacency:** Non-edge slots have two adjacent friendly slots (left and right) and one opposing enemy slot. Edge slots have one adjacent friendly slot and one opposing enemy slot.
*   **Movement & Blocking:** Placement dictates movement paths and potential targets (based on adjacency or position). It also triggers passive effects and generally controls the board. Creatures inherently block the slot they occupy; other creatures cannot move into or through an occupied slot unless an ability specifically allows for crossing, swapping, or pushing/pulling. Crucially, creatures cannot move freely. All movement requires specific Spells or creature abilities. If a creature changes sides (for instance, due to ***Recruitment*** or ***Conversion*** effects - defined in Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords and detailed in Section V.D. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Recruitment / Conversion Effects), it moves to an available slot on the new controller's side of the battlefield. A creature cannot be voluntarily moved into an opponent's slot by its current controller. Friendly creatures cannot move into enemy slots unless explicitly recruited or converted by an enemy effect. At that point, they effectively become enemy creatures under the opponent's control.

**Key Mechanic: Fog of War**

The "Fog of War" in this game revolves around the fact that players do not have a real-time preview of the specific actions their opponents are queuing during their Planning Phase of a global **Day**. Opponents cannot see what Spells are being prepared or which abilities are being activated until those actions resolve and are recorded. Information about an opponent's specific plans for the current **Day** is primarily gleaned *after the fact* through the Turn History, or by using specialized Intelligence abilities that might offer limited insights under specific conditions.

*   **End of Day Action Revelation & Turn History:** At the **Dusk (End of Day)**, after all queued actions for that **Day** have resolved, they are recorded in the **Turn History**. This history, envisioned with an "old newspaper" flavor for the UI, serves as a log.
    *   Standard resolved actions become fully visible to both players in the Turn History.
    *   Resolved ***Concealed Actions*** (actions hidden from the opponent until resolution) will appear as obscured entries (e.g., '???') in the Turn History for the opponent. The player who performed the ***Concealed Action*** sees it normally. (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords for `Concealed Action` and `Undercover` ability definitions; see 'Status Effects Compendium' for `Concealed`, `Revealed`, `Compromised` Status Effect definitions).
*   **Intelligence & Investigating the Past:** Players can use "Intelligence" actions. A primary use of these is to target specific '???' entries in the opponent's Turn History from previous **Days** or earlier in the current **Day**.
    *   Successfully revealing a '???' entry via an Intelligence action makes that past action's details permanently visible to the investigating player.
    *   This act of revelation is an action itself that is queued. When it resolves, if the original concealed action had any "On Being Revealed by Intelligence" trigger effects (like ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)*** or ***Strategic Disclosures***, which are defined in Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords and further detailed in Section V.A. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Traps & Strategic Disclosures), those effects are immediately formulated as new actions and added to the current Action Queue, resolving in sequence after the Intelligence reveal action.
*   **Counter-Intelligence:** These are actions or effects that might further obscure information, or more commonly, represent the risks of prying too deep (e.g., ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)*** triggered by Intelligence reveals). The `Compromised` Status Effect (which leaks information from an enemy agent - see 'Status Effects Compendium') is also a key Intelligence/Counter-Intelligence tool. Pre-set ***Interference*** effects (actions that disrupt the opponent's queue - defined in Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords and detailed in Section IV.D.4. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Disruption & Control - Interference (Triggered Queue Modification)) also form a core part of counter-intelligence strategies.
*   The `Revealed` Status Effect (see 'Status Effects Compendium') actively counters concealment. Applying it to a creature that is otherwise "Undercover" or `Concealed` (and thus producing ***Concealed Actions***) will cause its actions to no longer be concealed. This application can itself trigger any "On Being Revealed" effects (defined in Section V.C. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Trigger Events, specifically the 'OnRevealedByIntelligence' trigger).
*   The `Compromised` Status Effect (see 'Status Effects Compendium') makes an opponent's otherwise concealed creature's actions (past, present, and future) visible to the player who applied `Compromised`, without initially triggering "On Being Revealed" effects. The handler can then choose to use a separate Intelligence action to trigger those effects if desired.
*   **Targeting `Concealed` Creatures:** Unless a spell or ability specifically states it can target `Concealed` creatures directly (e.g., an Area of Effect spell, or an ability that says 'target a random Concealed creature'), an opponent attempting to target a known `Concealed` creature with a single-target spell or ability must instead target a specific **slot** they believe the `Concealed` creature occupies. If the `Concealed` creature is in that slot when the effect resolves, it is affected. If the slot is empty or occupied by a different (valid) target, the effect resolves accordingly for that slot/other target. If the slot is occupied by an invalid target or the effect cannot apply to a slot, the effect may fizzle for that specific targeting aspect. *(This rule should also be noted in the `Concealed` status definition in its Compendium for discoverability).*

**III. Foundational Game Systems & Rules**

**III.A. Faction System**
All cards are categorized into distinct factions. The definitive primary factions are: Shadow Ops., Statecraft, Myth, Corporate, Digital Society, Zeitgeist, Mind, Tech, and Utopia. Each card belongs to strictly one faction.
Deckbuilding allows and encourages combinations of factions within a single deck, similar to how color pairs or shards function in Magic: The Gathering. This system promotes diverse deck archetypes, which are built around combined faction strategies and identities.

**III.C. Action Economy & Tempo**
No resource cost (mana/energy) is required to play cards from hand. Instead, play is limited by hand size, timing considerations, and potential additional costs (see Section III.E.4.a. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Discarding from Hand (As an Additional Cost) for how all additional costs are handled).
Tempo is managed primarily by `Deployment Time` (X **Days**), an initial period where a creature is inactive after entering play. Its duration can be manipulated by game effects. (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords for keyword summary and 'Status Effects Compendium' for full definition of the `Deployment Time` Status Effect).

**III.D. Action Types & Triggering**
1.  **Creature Stats:** Attack, Health (representing maximum Health), and Speed (which influences action order in conjunction with ***Priority (Action)*** - see Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords). Stats can be modified. Current Health (HP) is tracked separately from maximum Health.
2.  **Creature Abilities (Passive):** These abilities are inherently passive. **Crucially, whenever their specified game event conditions are met (as defined in Section V.C. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Trigger Events), they ALWAYS trigger automatically. This triggering requires no player decision or intervention at the moment it occurs.** This automatic triggering happens unless the creature is under an effect (such as `Exhausted`, `Muted`, `Ostracised`, or `Brainwashed` - see 'Status Effects Compendium' and Section I.A for `Abilities Disabled`/`Muted` clarification regarding retroactive nullification) or affected by `Deployment Time` (see 'Status Effects Compendium') that explicitly prevents its passive abilities from functioning. When a passive ability triggers, its effect is formulated as an action. This action is then added to the Action Queue to be resolved in sequence, as detailed in Section III.G. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Universal Action Queuing. Targeting for the effects of these queued actions is predetermined by the card's text (e.g., "target the attacker," "adjacent creatures," "the creature that triggered this") or is resolved randomly if specified by the card. Players do not choose targets for passive abilities when they trigger. **Furthermore, when a player's queued passive ability resolves and affects an opponent, the opponent does not get to make choices about how that specific effect resolves against them; the effect's outcome must be fixed or conditional on pre-determined game state factors, not opponent choice at that moment of resolution.**
3.  **Special Creature Archetypes:**
    *   Support/Specialists: These may lack a standard Attack. They might possess an Active Ability (e.g., deal damage, heal, apply status) that the player queues during their Planning Phase each **Day**. If these abilities have additional costs, they are handled as per Section III.E.4.a. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Discarding from Hand (As an Additional Cost). The player selects any required targets at the time of queuing, according to the ability's rules. These abilities are subject to `Deployment Time` (see 'Status Effects Compendium') and queue mechanics.
    *   Automated Units: These may lack an Attack. They typically have powerful passive abilities that trigger automatically each **Day** (e.g., "Each Dawn: deal 1 damage randomly"; "Each Dusk: grant 1 ***Armor*** adjacently"). The target for such abilities is predetermined by the ability itself.
    *   "Must Act" Creatures: Some creatures may have abilities or be affected by Status Effects (e.g., `Bloodthirst` - see 'Status Effects Compendium') that impose penalties if certain actions (like Attack) are not queued by the player. (Future Design Space: A "Compelled Action" mechanic might auto-queue a default action if the player fails to meet a "MUST queue" requirement, ensuring game state consistency. This would be explicitly defined if implemented.)
4.  **Creature Attacks:** Players actively queue these during their Planning Phase. **These attacks are added as actions to the Action Queue.** Target selection for attacks might be restricted by rules (e.g., can only target the slot directly in front, cannot target a creature with `Ghosted` - see 'Status Effects Compendium'). Targets are chosen at the time the attack is queued. If a specific creature's Attack action has an additional cost defined on its card, this is handled as per Section III.E.4.a. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Discarding from Hand (As an Additional Cost).
5.  **Spells:** These are active actions. The player plays them from hand (e.g., by dragging and dropping onto a target, if applicable). When any card (including a Spell or Creature) is played from hand, or an ability is activated that has additional costs, these costs (including ***Requisites*** - defined in Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords and detailed in Section III.E.8. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Requisites) are handled as per Section III.E.4.a. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Discarding from Hand (As an Additional Cost). Any targets are chosen by the player at that time. The Spell's effect is then formulated as an action and added to the action queue. This action resolves according to standard queue mechanics (***Priority (Action)***, Speed, etc.). Spells can cause various game state changes, including movement, status effects, damage, healing, or card draw modification.
6.  **Determinism:** Game effects are deterministic. There is no percentage-based randomness for success or failure (except in cases of explicitly random targeting). Outcomes are predictable based on the current game state and the interactions of active effects. **Once an action is in the queue and begins to resolve, its outcome is determined by its text and the game state, without further player or opponent choices influencing its core effect resolution (unless explicitly defined by a pre-set interaction like Interference or a Meta-Action modifying resolution as per Section III.F.X).**

**III.E. Card Management**
1.  **Drawing Cards (***Scheduled Draw*** - see Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords):**
    *   Players draw a base number of **3 cards** only at **Dawn (Start of each global Day)**. This is the sole occasion when cards naturally enter a player's hand from their deck.
    *   The number of cards drawn is calculated as: Base Draw (3) + Sum of all positive/negative draw modifiers accumulated during the previous **Day**.
    *   **Minimum Draw:** The total number of cards drawn after all modifiers cannot be less than **1 card**.
    *   No effect allows a player to draw cards immediately from their deck mid-**Day**.
2.  **Draw Manipulation (Next **Day's** Draw Modification):** This affects future card draws.
    *   Card advantage effects state: "Schedule X additional card draws for your next **Day**." This increments a counter that applies to the next **Day's** draw.
    *   Card disadvantage effects state: "Draw X fewer cards next **Day**" or "Scheduled draw reduced by X." This decrements the counter (to achieve a minimum draw of 1 card as per III.E.1).
    *   These modifiers accumulate additively during a **Day**. They are resolved together for the calculation of the next **Day's** Dawn draw.
3.  **Discard Pile:** This is the zone for used Spells and destroyed Creatures.
4.  **Discarding from Hand:**
    *   This refers to the act of a player moving cards from their hand to their Discard Pile.
    *   a.  **As an Additional Cost (Universal Rule):** The primary way cards are discarded from a player's hand is as an additional cost to play another card (e.g., Spell, Creature) or activate certain abilities. This rule applies universally to any card play or ability activation that specifies such a cost. "Additional costs" (including those specified by `Requisites` or explicitly phrased as "As an additional cost...") are *always* handled during the player's Planning Phase when they attempt to play the card or queue the ability with such costs. This is a Meta-Action (see Section III.H.3. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing - Payment of Additional Costs). These costs are *never* paid as part of a resolving effect from the action queue.
        *   **Preemptive Check:** The game system must perform a preemptive check to determine if all mandatory additional costs *can* be paid by the player at the moment they attempt to play the card/queue the ability.
        *   **Payment:** If the preemptive check confirms the costs can be paid, the player then pays them immediately as part of the action of playing the card/queuing the ability. For discard costs, these selected cards are moved to the Discard Pile immediately. The card being played, along with its effect, is then added to the action queue.
        *   **Failure to Pay:** If a mandatory additional cost *cannot* be paid (as determined by the preemptive check), the attempt to play the card or queue the ability is cancelled. The card (if played from hand) is returned to the owner's hand, and the player receives feedback explaining why the action could not be taken (e.g., "Insufficient cards to discard," "No valid creature to sacrifice").
        *   **Cost Fulfillment Nuance:** Some costs might be framed as achieving a certain game state (e.g., "ensure you have fewer than X cards in hand," "control a creature of X faction") rather than a direct payment of specific resources. The player must meet these state-based conditions during the Planning Phase as part of successfully playing the card or queuing the ability. The preemptive check also applies to these state-based conditions.
    *   b.  **Discard as a Resolving Effect - Prohibition and Alternative:** Game effects (originating from Spells, creature abilities, traps, etc.) that resolve from the action queue *cannot* cause a player to discard cards directly from their hand. Consider an effect traditionally designed to force an opponent (or oneself) to discard a card from hand as part of its resolution (e.g., an effect that might read "Target player discards 1 card"). In this game, such an effect must instead be implemented differently: it becomes an effect that reduces the number of cards that player will draw on their next **Day**. This is further detailed in Section III.E.2. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Draw Manipulation with phrasing like "Target player draws 1 fewer card next **Day**." This design ensures that all hand manipulation related to card effects focuses on future resources (next **Day's** draw) rather than immediate hand reduction during the action resolution phase. This, in turn, maintains the uninterrupted flow of action resolution.
    *   c.  **Distinction from Draw Manipulation:** Discarding from hand as an additional cost is an immediate action taken by the player during their Planning Phase. It affects the current hand size by player choice to enable another action. In contrast, Draw Manipulation, which includes effects that reduce the next **Day's** draw (now serving as the mechanical replacement for forced discard effects), is a delayed consequence that impacts the future resources available to a player.
5.  ***Banish***: This action permanently removes a card (from hand, board, discard pile, or deck) from the game. Banished cards are outside all game zones and are irretrievable. A creature that is Banished is not considered "Destroyed" for the purpose of triggering abilities that specifically react to a creature being Destroyed (e.g., `OnDied` triggers as defined in the 'Trigger Events List', or other abilities worded as "when an enemy creature is Destroyed..."). While `OnDied` does not trigger, effects that trigger `OnLeaveBattlefield` (see 'Trigger Events List') will still trigger if their conditions are met, with 'Banish' being the destination zone. (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords for the standalone keyword definition).
6.  **Card Lifecycle & Reshuffling:**
    *   A Spell is played -> its effect resolves -> the Spell card goes to the Discard Pile.
    *   A Creature is destroyed (due to reaching 0 HP or by a "destroy" effect) -> the Creature card goes from the battlefield to the Discard Pile.
    *   Reshuffle Trigger: This activates only when a player is required to draw cards at **Dawn (Start of their Day)**, but their Draw Deck contains fewer cards than the number scheduled to be drawn.
    *   Reshuffle Process:
        *   The player attempts to draw the scheduled number of cards. Any cards remaining in the Draw Deck are drawn first (this could be 0 cards).
        *   If the Draw Deck becomes empty and the draw requirement has not yet been met, the player's entire Discard Pile is shuffled. This shuffled pile becomes their new Draw Deck.
        *   Immediately following the reshuffle, the player continues drawing cards from this new Draw Deck until the total number of cards drawn matches the number scheduled for that **Dawn (Start of Day)** phase. If, after reshuffling the Discard Pile, the new Draw Deck still contains fewer cards than the number scheduled to be drawn (even considering the minimum draw of 1 as per III.E.1), the player draws all available cards from their deck. The 'minimum draw 1' rule ensures that a player always draws at least one card *if one is available to be drawn or if modifiers would have otherwise reduced their draw to zero from a sufficiently stocked deck*. It does not compel the creation of cards if the deck and discard pile are exhausted.
7.  **Creature Reset:** When creature cards are reshuffled from the Discard Pile back into the Deck, any temporary modifications (such as current HP changes, temporary stat alterations, and some status effects) are reset to their printed base values.
8.  ***Requisites*** (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords): Some powerful cards have conditions that must be met before they can be played or their abilities activated. These are treated as additional costs and follow the universal rules outlined in Section III.E.4.a. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Discarding from Hand (As an Additional Cost) (and Section III.H.3. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing - Payment of Additional Costs), including preemptive checks and payment during the Planning Phase. Failure to meet a Requisite prevents the card from being played/ability from being queued, returning the card to hand if applicable, with feedback provided.
9.  **Opponent Hand Size Checks:** Due to the simultaneous nature of player actions within a global **Day** and action resolution, any game effects or abilities that need to check the number of cards in an opponent's hand (e.g., for conditional effects, targeting, or information gathering) will perform this check at **Dusk (End of the current Day)**. This check occurs after all other actions for the **Day** have resolved, ensuring a stable and definitive hand state for such evaluations.
10. **Revealing Cards from Hand**
    *   Some effects make a player show ("reveal") cards from their hand to their opponent.
    *   a.  **Instant & Temporary View:** When an effect reveals a card, your opponent instantly sees all its details (text, stats, etc.). This is a one-time look when the effect resolves; the card isn't continuously visible.
    *   b.  **Stays in Hand:** The revealed card remains in its owner's hand.
    *   c.  **Choosing Which Card(s) (from Opponent's Hand):**
        *   i.  **Filtering by Type:** An effect might specify a card type (e.g., "reveal a Creature"). Only cards of that type are eligible to be revealed.
        *   ii. **Random Pick:** If multiple cards match the filter (or if there's no filter and multiple cards are in hand), the game randomly selects which card(s) to reveal. The effect also states how many cards will be revealed.
11. **Other Deck/Card Interactions:** Effects on cards may allow for other forms of card manipulation, such as looking at cards in a deck, reordering them, or moving them within the deck. The specifics of these interactions are defined on the cards themselves and are distinct from Scheduled Draw or mandatory discard actions.

**III.F. Action Queue & Turn Flow Mechanics**
**Day Structure Overview (Global Day for all players):**
1.  **Dawn Phase (Start of Day)** (***Scheduled Draw*** for all players, `OnTurnStart` triggers resolve from the global Action Queue, corresponding to "Each Dawn" effects).
2.  **Planning Phase** (All players simultaneously queue their Spells, Creature Attacks, and Active Abilities for the **Day**, paying any additional costs as per Section III.E.4.a. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Discarding from Hand (As an Additional Cost) and Section III.H.3. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing - Payment of Additional Costs).
3.  **Pre-Resolution Interference Phase:** Before the main global Action Queue resolves, the game checks for any active "Set-up" ***Interference*** effects (`Nullify`, `Reorder`, `Redirect` - see Section IV.D.4. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Disruption & Control - Interference (Triggered Queue Modification) and Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords). If an action in the pending global queue meets the trigger conditions of such an effect, that action is modified directly within the pending queue. This phase ensures these specific modifications occur before standard resolution.
4.  **Action Resolution Phase** (The (potentially modified) global Action Queue resolves action by action based on ***Priority (Action)***, Speed, and tie-breakers. "Watchdog" Meta-Actions (see Section III.H.5) may inspect and modify actions *as they are about to execute*. Passive abilities triggering during resolution add their effects to this queue. "Linked Meta-Actions" (see Section III.H.6) may resolve as part of or immediately following a primary action. See Section III.F.X for the Principle of Dynamic State Application. **Crucially, once an action begins to resolve, its effects are applied as written without further player or opponent choices influencing its core outcome, unless such interaction is explicitly part of a pre-set mechanism like Interference or a Watchdog/Linked Meta-Action modifying resolution, or due to dynamic state changes as per III.F.X.**).
    *   ***CRITICAL PRINCIPLE: EFFECT DELAY VIA QUEUE.*** *No creature appears on the battlefield, no status effect is applied, no damage is dealt, and no spell takes effect *merely because its card was played or its ability conditions were met during the Planning Phase.* All such outcomes are contingent upon the corresponding action (e.g., `Play Creature Action`, `Spell Action`, `AbilityEffect Action`) successfully resolving from the global Action Queue during this Action Resolution Phase, according to priority, speed, and other ordering rules. Costs are paid upfront in Planning, but the *benefits/effects* are delayed until queue resolution.*
5.  **Dusk Phase (End of Day)** (Turn History updated for all players, `OnTurnEnd` triggers resolve from the global Action Queue, corresponding to "Each Dusk" effects. Hand Size checks for effects occur. Duration of timed effects and Status Effects - see 'Status Effects Compendium' and Section V.B. for decay timing - are processed. Natural decay of status effect stacks also occurs at Dusk, as per Section V.B.).

**III.F.X. Principle of Dynamic State Application within Current Turn Resolution:**
During the Action Resolution Phase, if a creature's status or applicable modifiers change (e.g., gaining `Muted`, `Cannot Act`, becoming subject to a new Watchdog), these changes can have an immediate impact on that creature's other unresolved actions already in the queue for the current Day, or on how other pending actions interact with it.
*   **Impactful Status Application:** For statuses like `Muted`, `Cannot Act`, or `Abilities Disabled`, their application may cause previously queued actions from that creature (or actions targeting that creature, if the status grants untargetability like `Ostracised`) to be nullified or re-evaluated just before their potential resolution. Specific interactions are detailed under individual status definitions (e.g., `Muted` in Section I.A. and the 'Status Effects Compendium').
*   **Modifier Application (Watchdogs):** As detailed in III.H.5, Watchdogs, once active, will affect relevant actions as they come up for execution from the current Action Queue.
*   **System Check:** The game system must be prepared to check the validity, targeting, and potential nullification of an action about to resolve against the current, potentially just-changed, state of the entities involved due to effects resolved earlier in the same Action Resolution Phase.

1.  **Queue Manipulation (General):** Beyond the specific Pre-Resolution ***Interference*** effects and "Watchdog" Meta-Actions, other actions might interact with queue visibility (e.g., Intelligence revealing details of pending actions) or apply effects that modify how opponents perceive or plan around the queue.
2.  ***Priority (Action)*** **Enhancement (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords):** Granting actions a higher ***Priority (Action)*** value causes them to resolve earlier, regardless of creature Speed. This is similar to priority mechanics in Pokémon. ***Priority (Action)*** enhancement can be an innate quality of a creature's action (e.g., "Priority Attack: +1 Priority"). Pre-Resolution ***Interference*** effects or "Watchdog" Meta-Actions can also modify ***Priority (Action)***.
3.  **Global ***Priority (Action)*** Modifiers:** These are effects that impact the ***Priority (Action)*** of all actions for a specified duration.
    *   Example ("Coordinated Advance"): Actions by creatures you control this **Day** and for the next 3 **Days** get +1 ***Priority (Action)***.
    *   Example ("Temporal Distortion"): For X **Days**, the normal action resolution order based on Speed is inverted.
        *   **Priority Resolution during Temporal Distortion:** Actions are first grouped and resolved by ***Priority (Action)*** level.
        *   **Speed Resolution during Temporal Distortion:** Within each ***Priority (Action)*** level, actions from creatures with lower Speed will act before creatures with higher Speed. If Speed is tied, use **Standard Tie-Breaking Protocol**.
4.  **Standard Tie-Breaking Protocol:** Used when actions are tied in both ***Priority (Action)*** and creature Speed (or Speed during Temporal Distortion).
    *   Lowest current (Health + ***Armor***) total resolves first.
    *   If tied, lowest base Attack resolves first.
    *   If still tied, resolve randomly (potential Karma System influence).
5.  **Tie-Breaking During Temporal Distortion:** If actions subject to inverted Speed order (same ***Priority (Action)*** level, Temporal Distortion active) have tied Speed, use **Standard Tie-Breaking Protocol**.
6.  ***Priority (Action)*** **Reduction:** Actions or effects can assign a negative modifier to an action's base ***Priority (Action)*** (e.g., Priority -1), causing later resolution. Pre-Resolution ***Interference*** effects or "Watchdog" Meta-Actions can also modify ***Priority (Action)***.

**III.G. Universal Action Queuing**
A fundamental principle of gameplay is that no card effect resolves instantaneously upon the card being played or its ability conditions being met *unless explicitly defined as a Meta-Action (see Section III.H. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing)*. This principle means that every effect generated by a Spell or a creature ability (whether active or passive) becomes an action for the global Action Queue. Similarly, every player-initiated creature attack is treated as such an action. Furthermore, every triggered trap effect, which includes ***Strategic Disclosure*** effects as detailed in Section V.A.1. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Traps & Strategic Disclosures - Strategic Disclosure Effects, is also formulated as an action and placed into the global Action Queue. These actions are then resolved in sequence during the Action Resolution phase of the **Day**, according to the rules outlined in Section III.F. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Action Queue & Turn Flow Mechanics. **Once an action begins to resolve from this queue, its effects are determined by its text and the current game state, without further intervention or choices from either player, particularly the opponent, unless such interaction is explicitly part of a pre-set mechanism like Interference or specific Meta-Actions influencing resolution (like Watchdogs or Linked Meta-Actions), or due to dynamic state changes as per Section III.F.X.**
It's important to distinguish these card-generated queued actions from Meta-Actions (see Section III.H. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing) such as paying costs, immediate slot effect consumption/replacement, or the mechanical process of drawing cards at **Dawn (Start of a Day)**. These types of processes are not "queued actions" generated by cards in the specific sense meant by this Universal Action Queuing principle.
*(See also the CRITICAL PRINCIPLE note in Section III.F.4 regarding delayed effects via queue).*

**III.H. Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing**
While the Universal Action Queuing principle (see Section III.G. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Universal Action Queuing) governs most game effects, certain processes occur immediately or outside the standard Action Queue resolution order. These "Meta-Actions" are foundational to the game's flow and how specific effects are processed.
1.  **Definition:** Meta-Actions are game state changes or processes that occur instantaneously, during the Planning Phase before actions are queued, or that modify actions at the point of queuing or resolution, rather than resolving through the standard Action Queue sequence as a primary queued action.
2.  **Slot Effect Replacement:** (As per Section IV.B.3. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics - Board Manipulation & Control - Slot Effects / Environmental Hazards): When a new Slot Effect is applied to a slot that already has an active Slot Effect, the old Slot Effect is **immediately** removed. The new effect takes its place. This is a direct, immediate state change.
3.  **Payment of Additional Costs:** (As per Section III.E.4.a. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Discarding from Hand (As an Additional Cost)): Costs such as discarding cards, sacrificing creatures (e.g., for ***Requisites***), or meeting other conditions to play a card or activate an ability are handled **during the player's Planning Phase** before the primary card effect or ability is added to the Action Queue. This is a player-initiated Meta-Action.
4.  **"1 Trigger" Slot Effect Consumption:** (As per individual entries in 'Slot Effects Compendium'): When a Slot Effect designated as "1 Trigger" has its trigger condition met, its game effect is formulated and added to the Action Queue. The Slot Effect itself is then **immediately** consumed (removed from the slot). This prevents the same "1 Trigger" Slot Effect from triggering multiple times before its effect resolves.
5.  **"Watchdog" System for Modifying Queued Actions:**
    *   **Mechanism:** Certain game effects (e.g., from Spells like "Mental Breakdown") can establish a temporary "Watchdog" that is linked to a specific creature, condition, or action type.
    *   **Function:** A 'Watchdog' effect, once active (i.e., after the spell or ability establishing it has resolved), monitors the Action Queue for specific actions that are **about to be executed**. Watchdogs effectively have a high priority to inspect and potentially modify actions *just before* those actions resolve.
        *   **Activation:** A Watchdog becomes active when the effect that creates it (e.g., a Spell like 'Mental Breakdown') resolves from the Action Queue.
        *   **Monitoring Scope:** From the moment it becomes active, a Watchdog observes actions (as defined by its trigger conditions, e.g., 'any Attack action by Creature X') that are next in line to be executed from the global Action Queue *for the remainder of the Watchdog's own duration (e.g., 'for the rest of this Day')*.
        *   **Modification Timing:** If an action currently at the front of the Action Queue (i.e., about to resolve) meets the Watchdog's trigger conditions, the Watchdog **immediately modifies** that action's properties (e.g., its target, Priority (Action), or even potentially nullifies it if the Watchdog is designed to do so) *before* the original action proceeds to resolve in its (potentially modified) form.
        *   **Impact on Already Queued Actions:** This means a Watchdog **can and will** affect actions that were placed into the Action Queue *before* the Watchdog itself became active, provided those actions have not yet resolved and meet the Watchdog's criteria when they reach the front of the queue for execution. This aligns with the Principle of Dynamic State Application (III.F.X).
    *   **Distinction from `Interference`:** `Interference` effects (see Section IV.D.4) are typically pre-set conditions that modify actions *already pending* in the Action Queue during a specific **Pre-Resolution Interference Phase** (which occurs before the main Action Resolution Phase begins). Watchdogs, under this revised definition, act more dynamically *during* the Action Resolution Phase, intervening just as an action is about to execute.
    *   **Example:**
        1.  During the Planning Phase, Player queues an `Attack Action` from Creature C targeting Opponent's Creature O.
        2.  Opponent, during the same Planning Phase, queues a Spell S that applies a 'Redirect Attack Watchdog' to Player's Creature C, lasting for this Day.
        3.  During the Action Resolution Phase:
            *   Spell S resolves first (due to higher priority or winning a speed tie-break for spells). The 'Redirect Attack Watchdog' is now active on Creature C.
            *   Later in the Action Resolution Phase, Creature C's `Attack Action` (which was queued earlier) comes up for execution.
            *   The active Watchdog on Creature C inspects this `Attack Action`. If it meets the Watchdog's criteria (e.g., 'any Attack by Creature C'), the Watchdog modifies its target according to the Watchdog's rules (e.g., redirects to a random creature).
            *   Creature C's `Attack Action` then resolves against the new, modified target.
6.  **"Linked Meta-Actions" for Dependent Follow-up Effects:**
    *   **Mechanism:** Some resolving actions from the Action Queue can have subsequent effects or Meta-Actions "linked" to their successful resolution. This is often used for effects phrased as "immediately gains [Status X]" or "then [do Y]."
    *   **Function:** If the primary action resolves successfully from the queue, the linked subsequent effect (e.g., "Apply Status X to Target Creature") is processed as part of the same conceptual event chain. This could mean the linked effect is injected into the current resolving block of the primary action or added to the Action Queue with very high priority to resolve next.
    *   **Dependency:** Crucially, if the primary action fails to resolve successfully (e.g., it is Nullified, or its target becomes invalid in a way that prevents its core effect), these linked subsequent effects typically do not occur.
    *   **Example:** A card effect states, "Deal 2 damage to target creature. If this destroys the creature, its controller immediately discards a card (becomes: draws 1 fewer card next **Day**)." The draw reduction is a linked meta-effect, dependent on the creature being destroyed by the damage. Phrasing like "immediately gains [Status X]" following a control change (e.g., Taqiyya Infiltrator) indicates the status application is a linked, high-priority follow-up part of the control change resolution.

**IV. Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics**

**IV.A. Information Warfare**
This involves constant interaction between revealing an opponent's hidden plans (Intelligence) and concealing your own plans or setting traps (Counter-Intelligence). This also includes using Intelligence actions to investigate obscured '???' entries in the Turn History, potentially revealing past enemy plans or triggering residual effects, such as ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)*** or ***Strategic Disclosure*** benefits (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords for definitions and Section V.A. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Traps & Strategic Disclosures for mechanics). Setting up ***Interference*** effects is also a key part of this loop.

**IV.B. Board Manipulation & Control**
1.  **Logistics (Movement):** Using abilities or Spells to reposition creatures (self, allies, or enemies) or to swap their positions. This optimizes attacks, blocks, and adjacency benefits, while also disrupting opponents' formations. Tactics include Post-Action Repositioning ("Hit and Run") and Positional Relays (buffs triggered by moving into a vacated slot).
2.  **Movement Restriction:** Using the `Heavy` Status Effect (see 'Status Effects Compendium'), specific abilities, or Slot Effects (see 'Slot Effects Compendium') to lock down enemy creatures or protect allied creatures.
3.  **Slot Effects / Environmental Hazards:** These are persistent or triggered conditions applied to specific battlefield slots that affect creatures or actions related to those slots.
    *   **Single Source of Truth:** For detailed definitions of individual Slot Effects (e.g., Landmine, Scorched Earth), refer to the external companion document: **'Slot Effects Compendium'**. Each Slot Effect has a controller, which is the player who applied the effect.
    *   **Exclusivity & Replacement:** Only one Slot Effect can be active per slot at any given time. If a new Slot Effect is applied to a slot that already has an active Slot Effect, the new Slot Effect replaces the old one. The old effect is **immediately** removed (this is a Meta-Action, see Section III.H.2. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing - Slot Effect Replacement), and its ongoing influences cease.
    *   **Visibility:**
        *   **Visible Slot Effects:** These are openly displayed on the slot, and both players are aware of their presence and type.
        *   **Hidden Slot Effects:** These are not visible to the opponent on the slot. The opponent is unaware of their presence or type until the Slot Effect is triggered by a game event (e.g., a creature entering a slot with a hidden 'Landmine') or revealed by a specific game mechanic (e.g., an "Inspect Slot" Intelligence action, if such a mechanic is implemented). Once triggered, a Hidden Slot Effect typically reveals itself as part of its resolution.
    *   **Triggers:** Slot Effects are activated by specific game events, as defined by the `Trigger Events List` and detailed in the effect's description in the 'Slot Effects Compendium'. Common triggers include `OnEnterSlot`, `OnBeforeLeaveSlot`, `OnTurnStart` (when occupied, corresponding to "Each Dawn"), `OnTurnEnd` (when occupied, corresponding to "Each Dusk"), or continuous effects while a creature occupies the slot. The specific trigger logic (e.g., checking if the slot is occupied by an enemy/friendly creature) is part of the individual Slot Effect's definition.
    *   **Consumption of "1 Trigger" Effects:** Slot Effects designated as "1 Trigger" are consumed (removed from the slot) **immediately** when their effect is formulated and added to the Action Queue (this is a Meta-Action, see Section III.H.4. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing - "1 Trigger" Slot Effect Consumption). This prevents them from triggering multiple times.
    *   **Functionality:** Slot Effects can cause a wide range of outcomes, including:
        *   Dealing damage to creatures.
        *   Applying creature Status Effects (see 'Status Effects Compendium').
        *   Modifying creature stats while on the slot.
        *   Altering movement rules or costs.
        *   Restricting or enabling certain actions (e.g., a "Jamming Field" might prevent Spells from targeting the slot).
        *   Affecting the controller of the slot (e.g., dealing damage if the slot is empty and targeted).
    *   **Damage to Slots & Occupants (General Rule):** When a slot is the target of damage, or when damage is redirected to a slot (e.g., from a creature-targeted effect whose target died or Left Play), that damage is applied to any creature currently occupying that slot. If the slot is empty, or if the creature occupying the slot is destroyed and there is excess damage, such damage is dealt to the player who controls that slot.
    *   **Cleansing Slot Effects:** Specific game actions or abilities, often denoted by the `Cleanse (SlotEffect)` keyword can remove Slot Effects from a slot. If a Hidden Slot Effect is cleansed before it triggers, it is removed without its effect occurring and without being revealed to the opponent (unless the cleanse action itself reveals it). Individual Slot Effects specify if they are cleansable in the 'Slot Effects Compendium'.
4.  **Hazard Removal / Decontamination:** Actions or abilities designed to ***Cleanse*** negative Slot Effects.
5.  **Location (Weather):** Cards that impose global modifiers on the entire battlefield. These effects can last for a set duration (in **Days**) or until changed by another Location card.
6.  **Flooding:** Overwhelming the board with many weak or token creatures ("junk").
7.  ***Recruitment*** / ***Conversion***: Taking control of enemy creatures. (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords for definitions and Section V.D. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Recruitment / Conversion Effects for mechanics).

**IV.C. Creature Enhancement & Protection**
1.  **Setup:** Actions or abilities that increase the core stats (Attack, Health, Speed) of a player's own creatures or allied creatures.
2.  **Survivability:** Actions like Heal (restoring HP), Add ***Armor***, or abilities that redirect attacks.
3.  **Immunity/Protection:** Granular immunity that negates specific negative effects.
4.  ***Cleanse***-ing (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords): Actively removing negative Status Effects (see 'Status Effects Compendium') from friendly creatures or slots.

**IV.D. Disruption & Control**
1.  **Status Effects Application:** A core mechanic involving the application of defined Status Effects (see 'Status Effects Compendium') to enemy creatures or slots.
2.  **Red Tape:** Effects that delay an opponent's actions, lower their ***Priority (Action)***, or add **Days** to a creature's `Deployment Time` (see 'Status Effects Compendium').
3.  **Disable Archetype:** Strategies focused on disabling key enemy functions, potentially via the `Exhausted` Status Effect (see 'Status Effects Compendium').
4.  **Interference (Triggered Queue Modification):** Certain abilities or effects allow players to set up ***Interference*** conditions (pre-set queue modifications like `Nullify`, `Reorder`, `Redirect` - see Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords) in advance. These trigger during the **Pre-Resolution Interference Phase** to modify opponent's actions that are *already pending in the Action Queue*. This is the *exclusive* mechanism for directly modifying an opponent's *already queued* actions by another effect in this manner before the main Action Resolution Phase.
    *   **UI Notification:** When an ***Interference*** effect modifies an action, the UI should clearly indicate this.
    *   Types of ***Interference*** include: `Nullify (Interference)`, `Reorder (Interference)`, `Redirect (Interference)`.
    The trigger conditions for these ***Interference*** effects are defined on the cards that create them.
5.  **Attack/Effect Redirection:** Direct redirection of an opponent's *already queued* actions is handled *exclusively* by pre-set `Redirect (Interference)` effects, as per Section IV.D.4. Effects like "Mental Breakdown" (which uses a "Watchdog" Meta-Action as per III.H.5) modify actions *as they are about to execute from the queue*, which can include actions queued earlier in the turn if the Watchdog becomes active before they resolve.
6.  ***Recruitment*** / ***Conversion*** / **Mind Control:** Effects that allow a player to take control of an opponent's creature. (See Section V.D. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Recruitment / Conversion Effects).

**IV.E. Recursion & Recovery**
1.  ***Revive*** (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords): An ability or Spell that returns a creature from Discard to battlefield, subject to `Deployment Time` (see 'Status Effects Compendium').
2.  **Return to Hand (from Battlefield):** An ability or Spell that removes a creature from its slot and puts the card into its owner's hand.
3.  **Return to Hand (from Discard):** An ability or Spell that selects a card from a player's Discard Pile and puts it into that player's hand.

**IV.F. Combat, Damage & Targeting**
0.  **Active Engagement for Combat Damage:** Creatures only deal combat damage as a result of performing an Attack action. No inherent "retaliation" damage unless by a specific ability.
1.  **Direct Damage:** Reducing a target's Health.
2.  **Chain/Spread Damage:** Damage that hits multiple distinct targets.
3.  ***Armor*** (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords): A temporary HP pool. Damage hits ***Armor*** first. Recoil damage also hits ***Armor*** first.
4.  **Life Drain / Siphoning:** Dealing damage also heals the source or controller.
5.  **Targeting Nuances:**
    *   **Targeting Types:** Effects and actions utilize two primary forms: ***Creature-Targeting*** (homing on a creature) and ***Slot-Targeting*** (targets the slot itself). (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords).
        *   **If Targeted Creature Dies or Leaves Play (under ***Creature-Targeting***):** The effect redirects to target the slot the creature last occupied.
    *   **Damage to Slots & Occupants (General Rule):** Damage to a slot hits its occupant. If empty or occupant is overkilled, damage goes to the player controlling the slot.
    *   **Targeting Restrictions & Patterns:** Many effects have specific restrictions (e.g., only front slot, only `Exhausted` creatures - see 'Status Effects Compendium').

**V. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems**

**V.A. Traps & Strategic Disclosures**
These are effects tied to specific actions or creatures that trigger under particular conditions, often related to information warfare. When a trap's or strategic disclosure's trigger condition is met (as defined by the `OnRevealedByIntelligence` trigger in Section V.C. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Trigger Events), its effect is formulated as an action and added to the Action Queue. This includes ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)*** which are detrimental to the revealer, and ***Strategic Disclosures*** which are beneficial. (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords for definitions, and Section V.A.1. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Traps & Strategic Disclosures - Strategic Disclosure Effects for Strategic Disclosure mechanics).

**Triggering Conditions for "On Being Revealed" Effects (Traps/Strategic Disclosures):**
Defined by the `OnRevealedByIntelligence` trigger (see Section V.C. Detailed Mechanics & Specific Systems - Trigger Events). Primarily:
1.  Intelligence Action on Turn History successfully reveals a '???' entry.
2.  Application of `Revealed` Status Effect (see 'Status Effects Compendium') to an "Undercover" / `Concealed` (see 'Status Effects Compendium') creature.

*   Type 1: ***Trap (Counter-Intelligence)*** ("On Being Revealed"): A passive ability that triggers a detrimental effect for the opponent who caused the reveal.
*   Type 2: ***Trap (Counter-Intelligence)*** (Rider on a Queued Action): A hidden rider on a queued action. If that action is modified by an opponent's ***Interference*** effect, the rider triggers.

Slot-based traps (defined in 'Slot Effects Compendium') queue their effect as an action when triggered.

**V.A.1. Strategic Disclosure Effects ("On Being Revealed" - Beneficial for Investigator/Revealer)**
Distinct from detrimental ***Traps (Counter-Intelligence)***, '***Strategic Disclosure***' effects are beneficial outcomes for the player who successfully causes the reveal of an opponent's concealed information.
*   Flavor: Represents the investigating player gaining an advantage from exposing secrets.
*   Mechanism: A player's reveal action (Intelligence on '???' or applying `Revealed` Status Effect - see 'Status Effects Compendium') on an opponent's concealed information (that has a 'Strategic Disclosure' characteristic) triggers a beneficial effect for the revealing player, queued as an action.
*   Purpose: Incentivizes Intelligence capabilities and adds risk for players using concealment.
*   Triggering: Automatic when conditions are met.

**V.B. Status Effects**
Status effects are temporary or persistent conditions that modify creature behavior or stats. They are a core mechanic for creature enhancement, disruption, and control.
**All status effects are defined as "Status Effects" in the external 'Status Effects Compendium' (see Section I.B. Other External References).** This GDD uses precise notation for these keywords when referencing them. New status effects can be proposed if a fundamentally new condition is needed, but require design approval and addition to the 'Status Effects Compendium' before use. Each Status Effect definition in the compendium specifies its cleansability, interaction with the global **Day** structure (e.g., effects "Each Dawn" or "Each Dusk"), and its Decay Rule.

**Core Status Effect Principles:**
*   **Natural Stack Decay (Revised Timing):** By default, all status effects with stacks lose 1 stack **Each Dusk (End of Day)**. **A status effect applied or gained during Day X will have its first stack/duration decay occur at the Dusk phase of Day X+1**, unless its specific definition in the 'Status Effects Compendium' or the applying effect states an immediate decay or a different timing (e.g., 'Each Dawn'). This ensures the effect is active for the entirety of the Day it is applied following its resolution.
*   **`Fixed_Stacks` Property:** Some status effects may be designated with the `Fixed_Stacks` property within the 'Status Effects Compendium'. A status with `Fixed_Stacks` has a stack count that can only be set at application (or refresh) and changed by its defined natural decay or specific internal mechanics (like evolution/consumption). Player actions or other card effects cannot directly add to or subtract from its current stacks, unless an effect explicitly states it can modify `Fixed_Stacks` statuses (which would be very rare and specific).
*   **Implicit "Upon Application" in Descriptions:** The `Description` field of a status effect in the 'Status Effects Compendium' implicitly details its immediate effects upon application (i.e., when the action applying it resolves), followed by any ongoing or triggered behaviors. Phrases like "Upon application" are generally omitted from the `Description` for brevity and player-focused clarity.
*   **Impact of Application on Queued Actions:** As per the Principle of Dynamic State Application (III.F.X), the application of certain statuses (like `Muted`, `Cannot Act`) can immediately affect or nullify unresolved actions from that creature already in the Action Queue for the current Day. This should be explicitly noted in the definition of such impactful statuses in the 'Status Effects Compendium'.

The interaction of these Status Effects with game rules, abilities, and other mechanics is detailed throughout this GDD. Key Status Effects frequently referenced include `Exhausted`, `Muted` (noting its I.A. clarification & III.F.X impact), `Ostracised`, `Brainwashed`, `Heavy`, `Doomed` (X **Days**), `Concealed` (noting its II. targeting clarification), `Revealed`, `Compromised`, and `Deployment Time`. (All defined in 'Status Effects Compendium').
The `Exposed` status applied by `Exhausted` has a duration that is either specified by the source applying `Exhausted` or defaults to an independent duration (e.g., Indefinite), not necessarily linked to `Exhausted`'s own duration (see 'Status Effects Compendium' for `Exposed` and `Exhausted` definitions).
The `Brainwashed` status effect changes ownership at the **Dusk of the 2nd Day** after application.

**V.C. Trigger Events**
This section outlines common game events that can trigger passive abilities or other effects. These events are defined in the external document **'Trigger Events List'**. Many triggers provide contextual information (e.g., source of damage, specific status applied) that can be used by an ability's logic. When describing ability triggers based on `OnTurnStart` or `OnTurnEnd` from this list, game text and this GDD will use the thematic terms 'Each Dawn' or 'Each Dusk' respectively. For example, an ability triggering "OnTurnStart" would be described as triggering "Each Dawn."

**V.D. Recruitment / Conversion Effects (See Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords)**
This section details mechanics for taking control of an opponent's creature via ***Recruitment*** (temporary) or ***Conversion*** (permanent).
1.  **Definition:** Effects allowing a player to gain control of an opponent's creature.
2.  **Targeting:** Targets an enemy creature, chosen when the Spell is played or ability queued.
3.  **Control Change:** Creature comes under the new player's control; original card ownership doesn't change. (Destroyed creatures go to owner's Discard; returned-to-hand creatures go to owner's hand).
4.  **Positioning & Movement:** Creature moves to an empty friendly slot on the new controller's side. Card effect specifies behavior if no empty slot. (See Section II. Core Concept & Vision, subsection Key Interaction: Tactical Positioning - Slot Occupation for rules on slot control).
5.  **Duration:**
    *   Temporary ***Recruitment***: Lasts X **Days**. Creature attempts to return to owner's side (or hand if no slot, or discard if hand full). Does not re-trigger 'OnEnterBattlefield' or reapply `Deployment Time` (see 'Status Effects Compendium').
    *   Permanent ***Conversion***: Lasts until creature **Leaves Play** or is re-converted.
6.  **State Preservation:** Creature retains current Health, ***Armor***, status effects (unless `Doomed` (X **Days**) - see 'Status Effects Compendium' - is involved), base stats, and current `Deployment Time` (see 'Status Effects Compendium').
7.  **Action Queue & Fog of War:**
    *   **Cancellation of Previous Controller's Queued Actions:** When Recruited/Converted, previous controller's queued actions from that creature are cancelled.
    *   **New Controller's Actions:** New controller can queue actions from the next **Day's** Planning Phase.
    *   **"Time Bomb" Archetype:** Achieved through persistent negative Status Effects (e.g., `Earworm`, `Doomed` (X **Days**), `Pandora` - see 'Status Effects Compendium' for definitions) or detrimental passive abilities, not lingering actions.
    *   **Concealment Status After Control Change:** `Concealed` status (see 'Status Effects Compendium') or `Undercover` ability benefits the new controller.
    *   **Important Note on Action Persistence:** Actions resolve within the current global **Day**.

**VI. Game Design Philosophy & Balancing**

**VI.A. Game Tone**
Intentionally cynical, funny, lighthearted, with satire of modern absurdities, bureaucracy, internet culture, myth tropes.

**VI.B. Balancing Focus**
Paramount goal, requiring ongoing playtesting, data analysis, iteration.
1.  **Numerical Balance:** Conservative initial numbers for stats, damage, healing, ***Armor***, durations.
2.  ***Priority (Action)*** **Modifier Rarity:** Cards granting direct ***Priority (Action)*** enhancements are less common or have higher costs due to their impact.
3.  **`Deployment Time` Manipulation:** Abilities reducing/bypassing `Deployment Time` (see 'Status Effects Compendium') are rare and carefully balanced. (`Deployment Time` is measured in **Days**).
4.  **Status Effect Design Principle:** Prioritize using existing, defined Status Effects from the 'Status Effects Compendium'. If a new status effect concept arises, it must be for a fundamentally new type of condition and undergo design approval before being added to the 'Status Effects Compendium' and used. Reminder text for complex secondary effects (like `Ghosted` transforming to `Doomed`) can be considered for player clarity but the compendium remains the single source of truth for the full effect.
5.  **Creature Ability Design Principle:** Prioritize reusing established core mechanics and defined trigger types (from 'Trigger Events List'). Trigger conditions are flexible. **Effects that give an opponent a choice during resolution are to be avoided; outcomes should be deterministic or conditional on game state, potentially influenced by defined Meta-Actions and the Principle of Dynamic State Application (III.F.X).**
6.  **Faction Synergy & Strategic Versatility Principle:** Optimal decks often blend factions to cover weaknesses.
7.  **Underdog Affinity & Comeback Potential:** Game aims for possible comebacks; tie-breaking can subtly favor weaker board states.
8.  **Embracing and Leveraging Chaos for Strategic Depth:** Winning involves creating complex, challenging scenarios for opponents.
9.  **Rewarding Emergent and 'Game-Breaking' Synergies:** Design embraces discovery of powerful, unconventional strategies, rewarding system knowledge and creativity, if balanced and thematic.
10. ***Recruitment***/***Conversion*** **Power Level:** Permanent ***Conversion*** is rare/high-cost. Temporary ***Recruitment*** balanced by duration/impact.
11. **Balancing Levers:** Opportunity cost, additional resource costs (handled universally as per Section III.E.4.a. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Card Management - Discarding from Hand (As an Additional Cost) and Section III.H.3. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Meta-Actions & Pre-Queue/Immediate Processing - Payment of Additional Costs), space limitations, domino effects.
12. **Development Goals & Player Experience:** Reward strategic planning, creative deckbuilding, adaptation, comebacks. Avoid complicated math, unfair luck.
13. **Prototype Karma System (Future Consideration):** Subtle background mechanic to mitigate extreme "bad luck" streaks.
14. **Core Design Tenet - Double-Edged Sword:** Significant positive effects often have drawbacks; significant negatives ideally offer an upside. (e.g., the `Doomed` Status Effect gives +Attack before death - see 'Status Effects Compendium').

**VII.B. Example Passive Ability Concepts**
This section provides thematic groupings of passive ability concepts for creatures, drawing from the "Illustrative Card Examples List" and general GDD principles. These abilities trigger automatically when their conditions are met, as per Section III.D.2. Foundational Game Systems & Rules - Action Types & Triggering - Creature Abilities (Passive), unless the creature is prevented from doing so (e.g., by `Muted`, `Exhausted` - see 'Status Effects Compendium' and I.A `Abilities Disabled` clarification). The consistent use of the `Abilities Disabled` keyword for full scope (passive and active) is maintained. Events like `OnTurnStart` or `OnTurnEnd` are described using "Each Dawn" or "Each Dusk".

*   **`Undercover` (Ability):** (As per Section I.A. General Gameplay Keywords) All actions queued by this creature are `Concealed Actions`.
    *   *Example Reference: Schrödinger's Cat (initially possesses this).*

*   **Day-Based Triggers (Each Dawn / Each Dusk):**
    *   **Self-Modification/Benefit:** Creature gains stats, status effects, or triggers resource gain for its controller.
        *   *Example: `Wojak Living The Dream` (Each Dawn: Gains `Burnout` - see 'Status Effects Compendium'; ATK scales with `Burnout` stacks).*
        *   *Example: `Pastafarian God` (Each Dawn: Conditional buff to specific creature types or schedules card draw for its controller's next **Day**).*
        *   *Example: `Brainlet Watermill` (Each Dawn: Gains `Burnout` - see 'Status Effects Compendium'; schedules card draw for its controller's next **Day** if `Burnout` stacks are sufficient).*
        *   *Example: `DOGE` (Each Dusk: Attack stat randomly changes).*
    *   **Area of Effect / Global Impact:** Affects other creatures (friendly and/or hostile) or the general game state.
        *   *Example: `Existential Dread` (Each Dusk: All creatures with >1 Health lose 1 Health).*
    *   **Targeted Impact:** Affects specific (often random) targets.
        *   *Example: `Monkey Clicking` (Each Dawn: Deals 1 damage to a random enemy creature).*

*   **Event-Triggered Effects:**
    *   **OnDeploymentTimeComplete:** Creature undergoes a significant transformation, gains substantial buffs, or triggers a unique, often powerful, effect.
        *   *Example: `Schrödinger's Cat` (Gains stats, Speed, and loses `Undercover` if not previously banished).*
        *   *Example: `Roko's Basilisk` (Applies `Doomed (1 Day)` - see 'Status Effects Compendium' - to all other creatures).*
        *   *Example: `Taqiyya Infiltrator` (Moves to opponent's side, changes control, and gains multiple detrimental status effects via a Linked Meta-Action).*
    *   **OnDied (When this creature is destroyed):**
        *   **Beneficial for Controller:** Triggers positive outcomes for its controller.
            *   *Example: `Unicorn (Enterprise)` (Controller schedules additional card draws for their next **Day**).*
        *   **Retaliatory/Consequential:** Triggers detrimental effects for the opponent, the source of death, or applies lasting effects to the board.
            *   *Example: `Dead Man's Switch` (Applies `Burnout` and `Exhausted` - see 'Status Effects Compendium' - to enemy creature deathSource, or deals damage to player if Spell deathSource).*
            *   *Example: `Taqiyya Infiltrator` (Applies 'Scorched Earth' Slot Effect to its last occupied slot).*
    *   **OnDealDamage (When this creature deals damage with an Attack):** Applies an additional effect, typically a status effect, to the damaged target.
        *   *Example: `Sentient Dentures` (Damaged target gains `Earworm` - see 'Status Effects Compendium').*
    *   **OnAttack (When this creature performs an Attack action):** Triggers an additional effect beyond normal combat damage, often conditional.
        *   *Example: `Giant Laserthrowing Futuristic Titanium Lizard` (If fewer than three enemy creatures, deals damage to each available enemy).*
    *   **Reactive (To Opponent's Actions or Effects):** Triggers based on specific actions taken by the opponent or effects applied by them.
        *   *Example: `Schrödinger's Cat` (If targeted by an opponent's Intelligence action or gains `Revealed` - see 'Status Effects Compendium' - while `Deployment Time` - see 'Status Effects Compendium' - is active, it Banishes itself).*
        *   *Example: `Venus Flytrap` (If targeted by an opponent's Spell, or its slot is, it gains Attack).*

*   **Static / Inherent Properties:** These are continuous effects or characteristics of the creature.
    *   **Movement Restriction:** Creature inherently cannot perform Move actions.
        *   *Example: `Venus Flytrap`.*
    *   **Targeting Immunity/Restriction:** Creature possesses inherent immunities or restrictions regarding being targeted by specific types of Spells, abilities, or effects.
        *   *Example: `Giant Laserthrowing Futuristic Titanium Lizard` (Its passive ability specifies it cannot be targeted by Spell actions from 'Corporate' or 'Statecraft' Factions).*

*   **Set-up Interference (Conceptual):** A passive ability that establishes an Interference effect (e.g., `Nullify`, `Reorder`, `Redirect`) which will trigger under specified conditions during the Pre-Resolution Interference Phase to modify an opponent's queued action.
    *   *Example (Conceptual, from original GDD): "Pre-emptive Scramble: Set-up `Reorder (Interference)`: If an opponent queues 3 or more Spell actions this **Day**, the opponent's Spell action with the highest base ***Priority (Action)*** (or other defined criteria) is modified by this ***Interference*** effect." (Note: While "Reflexive Control" is a Spell that sets up Interference, this concept refers to a creature's *passive ability* doing so.)*
