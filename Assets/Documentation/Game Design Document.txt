1. KEY TERMS

1.1 Glossary

Abilities Disabled
    • What it is: A game state. A creature in this state can't use its passive abilities (the ones that trigger automatically from its card) or its active abilities (the ones its controller chooses to use from its card). Statuses such as Muted put a creature in this state.
    • Duration: The effect that causes this will say how long it lasts.
    • Immediate Effect: If a creature gets Abilities Disabled during resolution, it takes effect right away. Any of its pending actions that came from its abilities are cancelled and won't resolve. Its Attack Actions are not affected (that is what Cannot Act does). The creature also can't trigger new passive abilities or use active ones while this is on.
    • Exception: Deployment Time and its OnDeploymentTimeComplete trigger follow their own rules (see 3.2 Tempo & Deployment Time).

Action and Effect (the user, 2026-10-16)
    • Action: what is done, a step of the Day's queue: an attack, a move, a summon, a spell cast, an ability used, a Watchdog set. Actions are planned, queued, ordered by layer and Speed, watched by Watchdogs, and they resolve.
    • Effect: what an action did in the game when it resolved: the damage dealt, the status that landed, the stat changed, the creature that died. Example: the action "Ram attacks the Turtle" had the effects 3 damage and Exhausted on the Turtle.
    • In the code an action is a queued IGameAction; the effects are what its executor changes on the board (and the follow-up actions it queues, which are actions of their own). Cards print effects ("Gamble", "On Play: 2 damage..."), which become actions when they trigger. Say "action" for the step and "effect" for its result, in text, the queue and the docs.

Action Nullification (When a Creature is About to Die)
    • What it is: A game rule. When a creature is marked for destruction, its pending non-retaliatory actions are cancelled.
    • Exceptions: Retaliatory triggers (OnDamaged, OnAboutToDie, OnDied) still happen. The full order is in 3.9 Creature Death Sequence.

Armor
    • What it is: A temporary shield of Health points on a creature. Damage hits Armor before the creature's actual Health. Recoil damage (damage a creature deals to itself) also hits Armor first.
    • Cap: A creature's Armor can't exceed twice its maximum Health. Armor gained above the cap is lost. If maximum Health drops, Armor above the new cap is lost too.
    • Fading: Every creature's Armor fades by 1 at the start of each Dusk, before "Each Dusk" triggers. So Armor granted at Dusk lasts until the next Dusk.
    • Leaving Play: A creature loses all its Armor when it Leaves Play.
    • Not Health: Health Loss ignores Armor (see Health Loss).
    • Removal: Specific effects can remove Armor.

Banish
    • What it is: Permanently removes a card from the current run. This can happen to cards in hand, on the battlefield, in the discard pile, or in the deck.
    • Consequence: Banished cards are completely out of play and can't be brought back during this run.

Cannot Act
    • What it is: A game state. A creature in this state cannot be told to Attack, Move, or use its Active Abilities by its controller, nor can it perform them if they were already queued. Deployment Time and statuses such as Ostracised put a creature in this state.
    • Duration: The effect causing this will say how long it lasts.
    • Immediate Effect: If a creature gets Cannot Act during resolution, any of its pending Attack, Movement, or Active Ability actions are cancelled.

Cleanse
    • What it is: An action or effect that removes the harmful status effects or Temporary Stat Modifications (stat decreases) from a creature. Positive statuses (like Blessed) and buffs stay.
    • Purge: The stronger version. It removes everything of the kind it names, good and bad alike: positive statuses and buffs too. Use it to strip an enemy's protection. Everything below about Cleanse applies to Purge as well.
    • Specificity: The Cleanse effect will say what it removes: status effects, Temporary Stat Modifications, or both. Statuses and stat modifications are independent (see 3.3 Creatures & Action Types), so cleansing one never removes the other.
    • Cleansable by Default: Every status can be cleansed unless its compendium entry says "Cleansable: No".
    • Not Cleansable: Cleansing such a status has no effect, unless its entry defines what a cleanse attempt does instead (e.g., Burnout explodes).
    • Slots: Slot Effects are not status effects. They are removed by the separate Cleanse (SlotEffect) keyword.

Concealed Action
    • What it is: An action planned by a creature with the Undercover ability or the Concealed status. The opposing side can't see it before it resolves, and it shows up as '???' in their Turn History afterwards. This works the same for both sides: concealed enemy actions are part of the "???" you see.
    • Benefit: Enemy Homing effects aimed at a concealed creature behave like Fixed effects, and enemy Watchdogs can't match actions they can't see.

Controller
    • What it is: The player who controls a creature on the battlefield right now. It starts as the player who summoned it and changes with effects like Recruitment or Brainwashed.
    • In card text: Abilities that affect or benefit "the player" of a creature (e.g., "its controller schedules 1 additional card draw") always mean its current controller.
    • Difference from Owner: Control can change during a combat; ownership never does. See Owner.

Conversion
    • What it is: An effect (usually a rare/expensive Spell or ability) that targets an enemy creature.
    • Outcome (player use): The enemy creature is immediately removed from the fight (Banished from the enemy side), and its card is "Captured": a copy is added to your discard pile, ready to use in the current run.
    • Outcome (AI use): The AI takes control of your creature for the rest of the combat. Nothing is captured; your card stays in your deck. Full rules in 5.4 Recruitment & Conversion.

Damage
    • What it is: Harm calculated through the damage system. Card text says "takes X damage" or "deal X damage".
    • How it works: Damage modifiers apply (e.g., Exposed +1, Cognitive Dissonance -1), then Armor absorbs it before Health. It triggers OnDamaged and OnDealDamage. See 3.9 Creature Death Sequence and 4.6 Combat, Damage & Targeting.
    • Difference from Health Loss: Damage only lowers current Health, which healing can restore. Health Loss lowers the Health stat itself.

Day
    • What it is: The main unit of game time, shared by both sides. Each Day has five phases: Dawn, Planning, Briefing, Resolution, and Dusk.
    • Durations: When something says it lasts for "X Days," it means these Days.

Dawn (Start of Day)
    • What it is: The first phase of a Day.
    • Events: You draw your scheduled cards. Then "Each Dawn" effects trigger and resolve on the spot.

Dusk (End of Day)
    • What it is: The final phase of a Day.
    • Events: Armor fades by 1. "Each Dusk" effects trigger and resolve on the spot. Status effect durations and stacks decrease. You check whether you have too many cards in hand.

Encrypted (Counter-Intelligence)
    • What it is: Protection for a creature's actions. Watchdogs ignore actions from an Encrypted creature, and no charge is spent.
    • Who uses it: the factions that run on computers: Shadow Ops., Digital Society, Corporate, Statecraft and Zeitgeist. A good status: a Cleanse leaves it, a Purge removes it.

• Fixed Targeting / Fixed: (Targeting Keyword Prefix):
    • What it is: Aims an effect at whatever creature is in a chosen slot when the action resolves. The default for effects aimed at an enemy slot.
    • Behavior: It doesn't follow a creature that moves. If the creature there isn't a valid target, the effect fizzles (does nothing). If the slot is empty, a creature-only effect (stat changes, statuses, Armor) fizzles, but damage hits that side's player instead (see 4.6 Combat, Damage & Targeting).
    • Difference from Static: Fixed only ever affects a creature. It can't affect the slot itself.

Health Loss (-X Health)
    • What it is: A reduction of a creature's Health stat. Card text says "-X Health" or "gets -X Health". It's rarer and more expensive than dealing damage.
    • How it works: Maximum Health and current Health both drop by X. It isn't damage: Armor doesn't absorb it, damage modifiers don't apply, and OnDamaged doesn't trigger. A creature at 1 Health with 10 Armor that gets -1 Health dies.
    • Duration: Permanent unless the effect says otherwise (a Persistent Stat Modification). A temporary -X Health lasts as long as its effect says; when it ends, maximum and current Health both go back up by X.
    • Death: If it drops maximum or current Health to 0, the creature dies (see 3.3 Creatures & Action Types - Minimum Values).

• Homing Targeting / Homing: (Targeting Keyword Prefix):
    • What it is: Targeting that locks onto a specific creature. Effects aimed at your own creatures use Homing unless the card says otherwise: there's no fog of war on your side, so you know where they'll be. Effects aimed at an enemy slot use Fixed unless the card says Homing.
    • Behavior: The effect "locks on" to one creature and follows it if it moves. If the creature Leaves Play, the effect is aimed at the slot it was last in instead.
    • Creatures Being Summoned: Aiming at a slot where your creature is queued to be summoned locks on to that creature. If its summon is taken back, the effect is aimed at that slot instead.
    • Aimed at an Empty Slot: A Homing effect dropped on an empty slot has no creature to lock on to. Its target is that slot, so it behaves like Fixed.
    • Why It Matters: Movement resolves before Spells, Attacks and Abilities, so creatures can dodge anything aimed at a slot. Effects that target a specific creature (Homing) are the only reliable answers, which is why they're scarce.
    • Against Concealed creatures: If a Homing effect targets an enemy creature with Concealed (and the concealment isn't cancelled), it behaves like Fixed for that one time.

Intelligence
    • What it is: Everything about information and the action queue, in three layers: Scan (uncover hidden information, instantly), Alter (Watchdogs that act on the queue), and Secure (Counter-Intelligence). Full rules in 2.5 Fog of War & Intelligence.
    • The Past Is Off-Limits: Intelligence only affects the action queue and what each side can see. It never changes anything that already resolved.

Leaves Play
    • What it means: A creature is removed from the battlefield (e.g., destroyed, discarded, Banished, returned to hand).

Mandela Effect
    • What it is: A rare, one-time game effect. When it resolves, it changes the base stats of every creature (both sides) currently on the battlefield, for as long as each of those creatures stays on the battlefield.
    • Scope: Creatures summoned after this effect don't get changed. Full rules in 5.6 Mandela Effect.

Movement Action
    • What it is: The action created whenever a creature moves itself.
    • Restrictions: Can be stopped by effects like Cannot Act or Anchored.

Nullify (Watchdog effect)
    • What it is: A Watchdog effect that makes a pending action resolve with no game effect (it does nothing). It still counts as having happened, and the Turn History shows that it was nullified and by what.

Owner
    • What it is: The player whose deck a card was in at the start of the combat. (Captured copies you gain belong to you.)
    • Card Zones: A card that leaves the battlefield always goes to its owner's hand, discard pile, or deck, even if another player controlled it at that moment.
    • Never Changes: Effects change a creature's controller, never its owner.

Persistent Stat Modification
    • What it is: A change to a creature card's base Attack, Health, or Speed that lasts for the rest of the current combat.
    • How it works: These directly change the creature's base stats. Temporary Stat Modifications are then added on top of this new base.
    • Zones: The card keeps these mods while it moves between the battlefield, hand and discard pile.
    • Reset: Removed when the card is reshuffled from the discard pile into the deck, and at the end of combat.
    • Cleansing: Usually not removable by standard Cleanse effects.

Player Health
    • What it is: Each side (you and the enemy) has a Player Health total. Both start every combat at 20 (Relics, Stocks or specific encounters may change this).
    • Damage: Reduced by effects that specifically target a "Player," and by Attack Actions or damage that resolve into an empty slot on that player's side.
    • Defeat: The combat ends when either side's Player Health reaches 0, checked after every action. If yours reaches 0 (even in a tie), your run ends. (Other ways to win a combat may be added later.)

Prophecy
    • What it is: A Myth effect that is announced openly and happens a set number of Days later, at Dawn. Full rules in 5.7 Prophecy.

Recruitment
    • What it is: An effect (Spell or ability) that targets an enemy creature.
    • Outcome (player use): You control that creature for the rest of the current combat. If it survives the combat (and meets any extra conditions on the card), its card is "Captured": a copy is guaranteed to be one of your post-combat card reward options.
    • Outcome (AI use): The AI controls your creature for the rest of the combat. Nothing is captured; your card stays in your deck. Full rules in 5.4 Recruitment & Conversion.

Redirect (Watchdog effect)
    • What it is: A Watchdog effect that changes the target of a pending action: to a random creature on either side, other than the one acting and the one it was aimed at. Heat Signature draws it first: if any creature has Heat, it goes to the one with the most.

Reorder (Watchdog effect)
    • What it is: A Watchdog effect that moves a pending action within its layer. A Briefing Watchdog can move it earlier or later; an Monitored Watchdog can only move it later (it only sees the action when it's already up). Layers never change order.

Requisite
    • What it is: A condition (like discarding cards or sacrificing a creature) that you must meet during the Planning Phase to play certain cards or use certain abilities. It is paid instantly, as an additional cost.

Revive
    • What it is: An ability or Spell that brings a creature card back from your discard pile to an empty friendly slot on the battlefield.

Sacrifice
    • What it is: Destroying one of your own creatures, usually as an additional cost (Myth's main way to pay). It's paid instantly during Planning, like any additional cost.
    • A Real Death: A sacrificed creature is destroyed, not discarded. Everything that reacts to a death triggers: its own OnAboutToDie and OnDied, and other cards' "whenever a creature dies" or OnKill-style effects. OnAboutToDie resolves on the spot, during Planning; OnDied and other death effects are queued for the main resolution pass.
    • Saving It Is Fine: If an OnAboutToDie effect saves the creature, or it comes back after dying (e.g., "resurrects once"), the sacrifice still counts as paid.
    • Always a Price: A creature that can't die (e.g., an "immortal" effect) can't be chosen as a sacrifice. Effects that save or bring back a creature must be limited (e.g., once per combat, or consuming something), so no creature can be sacrificed again and again for free.
    • Death vs. Removal: Only killing counts as death (damage, destroy effects, Health reaching 0, Doomed, sacrifice). Returning a creature to hand, discarding it from the battlefield (e.g., summoning onto its slot) or Banishing it is not a death, and death triggers don't fire.

Scheduled Draw
    • What it is: The main way you draw cards, happening at Dawn.

• Static Targeting / Static: (Targeting Keyword Prefix):
    • What it is: Aims an effect at a slot itself.
    • Behavior: The effect can change the slot (e.g., place a Slot Effect) and/or affect whatever creature is in it when the action resolves. It doesn't follow creatures. A creature-only part of the effect (like a status) fizzles if the slot is empty; damage hits that side's player instead (see 4.6 Combat, Damage & Targeting).

Strategic Disclosure
    • What it is: A benefit you get when you use Intelligence to uncover important hidden enemy information (like "???" intents or secret weaknesses on enemy cards). The reveal is instant; the benefit is queued like any other effect.

Temporary Stat Modification
    • What it is: A short-term change to a creature's Attack, Health, or Speed.
    • Duration: Usually lasts for a set number of Days, until something specific happens, or until it's cleansed.
    • Tracking: Tracked separately from base stats and shown like BaseStat + TemporaryModifierValue.
    • Cleansing: Usually removable by Cleanse effects.
    • Reset: Removed when the creature Leaves Play (unless the effect moving it says otherwise) and at the end of combat.

Trap (Counter-Intelligence)
    • What it is: A hidden effect that punishes the opposing side. You can set traps (e.g., hidden Slot Effects). Enemy cards (especially bosses) may have traps that hurt you if you use Intelligence on certain sensitive information.
    • A Reaction: A trap that reacts to an action resolves before that action (see 3.11 Instant Effects - Reactions and Retaliation). A trap sprung by a reveal is discovered instantly; its punishment is queued.

Undercover (Ability)
    • What it is: A passive creature ability (printed on a card) that makes all actions planned by that creature Concealed Actions. It is an ability, not a status.

Watchdog
    • What it is: An Intelligence asset that watches the action queue and changes it when its condition is met. It isn't a creature and never attacks. Full rules in 3.11 Instant Effects - Watchdogs.

2. GAME OVERVIEW

2.1 Premise & Player Experience

• What's the Game About? A roguelite deckbuilder where you guide your character through a "path" that represents life's increasing challenges and weirdness in a chaotic, funny, modern world. You build a deck of creature and spell cards from different Factions (all cards come from one big shared pool). You need smart planning, information gathering (Intelligence), and good positioning to beat groups of AI-controlled enemy creatures.
• Genre: Roguelite Deckbuilder.
• How Should It Feel to Play? Like you're tactically outsmarting enemies (predicting and countering their scripted plans), uncovering risky secrets (using Intelligence to see hidden enemy moves and weaknesses), and solving combat puzzles created by unique enemy card combinations.
• The Story's Journey: The "path" is an allegory for a life journey (e.g., "getting older"), with fights representing social, corporate, tech, and life's curveballs. The tone is cynical, funny, and satirical.

2.2 Central Conflict: Your Brains vs. Scripted Mayhem

You use Intelligence to figure out and deal with threats from enemy Faction groups. These enemies follow pre-set scripts and use cards from the same shared pool you do. The challenge is to outsmart these rule-based systems and the built-in advantages of powerful enemies.

2.3 Goal of a Run

• Make it through the "path" by defeating enemy encounters and bosses.
• Build a flexible deck by getting new cards, Recruiting/Converting enemy creatures, and using Relics, Stocks, and Subscriptions.
• Master the game's mechanics to beat increasingly harder challenges.

2.4 The Battlefield

• Layout (the user, 2026-10-15): each side has 5 slots in a formation, as in a real war: the Vanguard alone in front (the
  wall that takes the melee), the two Flanks behind it (the shooters: +1 Attack and +1 Speed), and Headquarters at the back
  (the important people, hard to reach). Ours is a Λ, the enemy's a V, Vanguards facing at the middle of the table. A slot
  faces the one in the same column across the table. A step goes from the Vanguard to either Flank, Flank to Flank, a Flank
  to the Headquarters slot behind it, and Headquarters to Headquarters: "beside" means a step away. (Formation.cs)
  Reach: the distance of an attack is how deep each slot sits (Vanguard 0, Flanks 1, Headquarters 2), added, plus 1. The
  slot decides: the Vanguard melees (1, their Vanguard); the Flanks and Headquarters shoot (2-3); Artillery, a keyword,
  shoots exactly 4. Bonuses: Vanguard +1 Armor each Dawn (at most 2), Flanks +1 Attack/Speed, Headquarters none.
  (Reach and bonuses: being built.)
Slot Occupation & Movement
    • Creatures block the slot they're in; other creatures can't normally move into or through an occupied slot, unless an ability specifically says they can (like swapping, pushing, or pulling).
    • Default Movement: A creature moving itself walks step by step through its formation, any distance, along empty slots, to an empty slot. It can't enter or pass through an occupied slot. The back rows are never blocked by the ones ahead (Headquarters to Headquarters is a step). Exception: friendly Ghosted creatures don't block it.
    • Swapping in Melee Range (every creature; the user, 2026-10-16, replacing Push, archived in Archived/Push.txt): A moving creature swaps with the friendly creature one step away (melee range): the other one takes the slot the mover started from. Further away, it needs the Swap keyword. Being moved isn't the other creature's own movement. A Rooted creature can't be swapped, nor can Heavy and multi-slot ones.
    • Moving While Deploying (the user, 2026-10-16): A creature still deploying can't attack or use its ability yet, but it can move and swap, to take its place. Exhausted stops it; Rooted (a building going up, e.g. Ministry for Equality, Pyramids: Rooted while deploying) stops it, and nothing can move it.
    • Mobility Matters: Most attacks are aimed at a chosen slot or creature, and Homing is scarce, so repositioning is how you dodge threats and line up attacks. Moving past other creatures (Leapfrog) or swapping with them is a special ability only some creatures have.
    • Blocked Before It Resolves: If the path or destination gets blocked before the Movement Action resolves, the creature stops at the furthest empty slot it can still reach toward its destination. If it can't move at all, the move does nothing.
    • Entering Slots: Only the slot where a creature ends its move counts as entered (OnEnterSlot). Slots it passes don't trigger.
    • Automatic Move Order: Players choose where their creatures go, never in what order. Each side's Movement Actions resolve in an order the game picks so that every planned move works (see 3.7 Resolution Order). Moves on one side never affect the other side's row, so there's no race between sides.
        • Destinations, Not Steps: You drag each creature to the slot you want, in any order. The game moves first whoever needs to make room.
        • Example: A is in slot 3 and B in slot 1. Drag B to slot 3, then A to slot 5: the game moves A first, then B.
        • Swap Only When Needed: A move swaps with a creature only if that creature isn't leaving its slot. If it's planned to leave, the game lets it leave first.
        • Impossible Plans: If no order can make a move work (e.g., two creatures that would have to pass each other), that move shows as blocked while you plan, with a hint (e.g., "needs Swap or Leapfrog"), and can't be confirmed.
        • Live Preview: Ghosts and arrows always show the final formation. Editing or cancelling a move updates the whole preview at once.
        • Battle Arrows: planned actions show as campaign-map arrows, so the battle reads at a glance: translucent bands with a bright rim, faint at the tail and strongest at the head. Attacks are red, moves green, spells violet, other damage orange. Everything of one kind aimed at the same target is one arrow: creatures attacking the same slot flow into one trunk, wider for each of them, with one head. Arrows keep to the right of where they go, so two going opposite ways (two creatures attacking each other) pass side by side, and moves along a row bow aside so a swap's two arrows don't overlap. Heads stop at the target card's edge, clear of its stats.
        • Tie-Breaker: When several orders work, the game uses the order the moves were planned in.
        • The AI plans its moves the same way.
    Movement Keywords (abilities printed on specific creatures; each is still that creature's own Movement Action):
        • Leapfrog: Moves to any empty slot of its formation, jumping over occupied slots.
        • Swap: Trades places with a friendly creature at any distance, ignoring creatures in between. Being swapped isn't the partner's own movement, so it works on Anchored partners. Heavy and multi-slot creatures can't be swapped.
        • Swaps only affect friendly creatures. Creatures never change sides by moving (see 5.4 Recruitment & Conversion).
    Choosing the Move Type
        • Each destination has one meaning. The game always uses the best movement option the creature has: Leapfrog over walking for an empty slot; a swap for an occupied one (in melee range, or any distance with the Swap keyword).
        • The UI shows that meaning before you drop the creature (planned: color-coded reachable slots and a preview of where every creature ends up).
    • Any time a creature moves itself, it creates a Movement Action, which can be restricted.
    • Other effects can push, pull, or swap creatures without them using their own action.
    • You can't move your own creatures into enemy slots. A creature only changes sides when an effect changes who controls it (e.g., Recruitment, Conversion used by the AI, Brainwashed). It is then placed on its new controller's side as part of that effect. Changing sides is not movement (placement rules in 5.4 Recruitment & Conversion).

2.5 Fog of War & Intelligence

The "Fog" is all the mystery around enemy encounters:
• Unrevealed "???" intents (you can't see the full details of an enemy's planned action).
• Hidden passive abilities on enemy cards.
• Boss phase-change triggers.
• Full scripted attack patterns.

• Enemy "???" Intents: Some enemy actions have parts hidden as "???" until they resolve. Intelligence can reveal them.
• Turn History: A record of actions that have already happened, to help you spot patterns. Nullified actions stay in it, marked with what nullified them.
• Concealment vs. the AI: The AI only reacts to what it can see. It can't see through Concealed or Undercover unless a card ability lets it.
• Unknown Creatures: A side sees that the other is summoning, and where, but not what: the card shows as '???' until its summon finishes. Then the creature shows what it is, unless it's stealthy (Undercover or Ambush, or Concealed when it enters): a stealthy creature stays '???' (name, stats, art, text) until a Reveal or Revealed shows it. Once seen, it stays known until it leaves play.
• Each Player's View: The game resolves everything knowing everything; each player is only shown what they know: their own cards, and what their cards revealed. The screen shows the view of the side you play, and only that side's cards can be used. The enemy's hand shows card backs (an enemy spell queued from it shows where it's aimed, as a card back there, not what it is, until it resolves). A Concealed enemy creature's card shows '???' instead of its arrows and move ghosts; hidden enemy Slot Effects aren't shown; enemy Watchdogs show only what they watch.
• The Queue: A Queue button at the right edge of the table opens a list of every pending action in the order it will resolve, grouped by layer, with who does what to whom. It follows the same view: the enemy's Concealed plans read "plans ???", what it summons is "???" until the summon finishes, its spells show where they're aimed but not what they are, and its hidden traps and Watchdog conditions stay hidden.
• Debugging: Debug > Fog of War (F9 in play) shows everything and lets you use both sides' cards, for hot-seat testing; Debug > Play As Player 2 (F8) shows player 2's view and uses their cards: what's concealed flips with it, and the two halves of the table swap so their side sits at the bottom. Neither changes the rules.
• Reveal (implemented): aimed at a slot, it shows its caster the slot's hidden Slot Effect and what the creature there plans this Day; aimed at a player, their Watchdog conditions. A card that only reveals (Reveal, or applying Revealed) resolves the moment it's played.
• Bestiary (Meta-Progression): Using Intelligence fills out a Bestiary that keeps enemy card and mechanic info for future runs.

Intelligence: Scan, Alter, Secure
Intelligence is a cat-and-mouse game in three layers. Scanning is cheap and common, altering is rare and expensive, and securing costs resources. A deck that builds powerful creatures needs some protection; a deck that loves chaos picks out the enemy's key plays and turns them against them or makes them vanish.

• Golden Rule: Information is instant; consequences wait in line. Intelligence only affects the action queue and what each side can see. It never changes the past.

• 1. Scan (cheap, common)
    • Reveal: A one-time look at hidden information: a "???" intent, a creature's details, a hidden Slot Effect, a Watchdog's condition, or even the whole queue.
    • Surveillance: Ongoing observation of a slot or creature. Nothing there can be Concealed or Undercover, and you see everything about it: its actions, ability, stats and name.
    • Instant: Effects that only change what you can see resolve immediately during Planning, outside the queue (they're Meta-Actions, see 3.11). The enemy plans first, so a reveal shows you their plan before you commit yours.
    • A creature can scout instead of attacking: an active Intelligence ability resolves instantly and uses that creature's intent for the Day.
    • Consequences still queue: hitting a Honeypot or earning a Strategic Disclosure is discovered instantly, but the punishment or reward is an action that resolves in order.

• 2. Alter (rare, expensive): Watchdogs
    • Watchdogs are the only way to change the action queue: nullify, redirect, reorder or create actions, or reveal an action as it comes up. Full rules in 3.11 Instant Effects - Watchdogs.

• 3. Secure (Counter-Intelligence)
    • Against seeing: Concealed and Undercover show "???" until someone spends a reveal. Traps like Honeypot punish the snooper.
    • Against changing: Encrypted creatures' actions are ignored by Watchdogs.
    • Against reacting: reveal the enemy's Watchdog conditions (instant) and plan around them.

2.6 Shared Card Pool

All creature and spell cards come from one big shared pool, and abilities work the same no matter who controls the card. The strategy comes from how these shared tools are combined (your choices vs. enemy scripts). The only exceptions are bosses and some elites, which may use unique cards that can't be captured (listed in the 'Enemy Compendium').


3. CORE RULES

3.1 Factions

Every card belongs to exactly one faction: Shadow Ops., Statecraft, Myth, Corporate, Digital Society, Zeitgeist, Mind, Tech, or Utopia.
You can (and should!) mix cards from different factions when building your deck, similar to color combinations in Magic: The Gathering. Faction themes, mechanics and matchups are described in the 'Faction Compendium'.
• Deck Builder (until runs exist): the main menu's Decks screen makes decks from any cards on offer, mixing factions freely: 20 to 40 cards, at most 2 copies of each. The starter decks are listed there too, to look at and copy as a start. Decks are saved as they change. Once complete, the match setup offers a deck to either side, next to the starter decks. The Card Gallery shows the cards faction by faction.

• Mechanic Ownership: Every mechanic and status belongs to a faction. Its owner uses it cheaply and often; one or two other factions get it rarely and at a higher cost; nobody else gets it. A few statuses are Neutral and open to everyone.
• Faction Costs: Each faction pays for its power in its own way, on top of Deployment Time. Mixing factions means mixing cost types.
• Faction Holes: Each faction has one thing it can't do at all. You cover it by combining factions.

Myth · faith, death & prophecy
    Pays with: sacrificing its own creatures
    Can't: move creatures

Statecraft · order, law & war
    Pays with: bureaucracy (extra Deployment Time on its own creatures)
    Can't: hide anything

Shadow Ops. · secrets & sabotage
    Pays with: burning (Banishing its own cards from hand or discard pile)
    Can't: heal or gain Armor

Corporate · money, growth & exploitation
    Pays with: capital (discarding cards from hand)
    Can't: heal

Tech · systems & the queue
    Pays with: Technical Debt (fewer draws next Day)
    Can't: bring anything back from the discard pile

Digital Society · virality & the mob
    Pays with: exposure (its own creatures gain Revealed or Heat Signature)
    Can't: have big creatures, Heavy, or Persistent Stat Modifications

Zeitgeist · moods of modern life
    Pays with: self-harm (damage or statuses on its own creatures)
    Can't: use Intelligence

Mind · perception & paradox
    Pays with: double-edged effects that also hit its own side
    Can't: deal raw damage

Utopia · science & the future
    Pays with: side effects (a negative status on the creature it experiments on)
    Can't: conceal anything

3.2 Tempo & Deployment Time

There's no mana or energy cost to play cards. What you can play is limited by:
• Hand Size.
• Timing (when you can play things).
• Additional Costs (discards, sacrifices, Banishing your own cards, scheduling fewer draws, Requisites), paid instantly during Planning.
• Faction Costs (see 3.1 Factions). Some are additional costs paid during Planning (Myth, Shadow Ops., Corporate, Tech); the rest are drawbacks built into the card's effect.

The main tempo control is Deployment Time.
• Deployment Time (N Days): Printed on the creature's card. The default is 0, which isn't shown on the card. It is its own mechanic, not a status effect: status rules (Cleanse, Blessed, Doomed's status removal...) never touch it, and only effects that name Deployment Time can change it.
• While Deploying: A creature with Deployment Time 1 or more when it enters play Cannot Act and its passive abilities don't trigger until it's ready. Effects that set a new Deployment Time replace the current count.
• Countdown: Deployment Time counts down by 1 Each Dusk, but never at the Dusk of the Day the creature entered play. When it reaches 0, the creature is ready. So a creature with Deployment Time N sits out N full Days after the Day it arrived.
• Counterplay: The countdown is the opponent's window: kill or disrupt the creature before it's ready, since its "When Ready" effects wait too.
    • Example: Two creatures enter play on Day 3. The one with Deployment Time 0 never gets the status and can be given an intent from Day 4. The one with Deployment Time 1 skips the Dusk of Day 3, reaches 0 at the Dusk of Day 4, and can be given an intent from Day 5.
• "When Ready" (OnDeploymentTimeComplete): A creature's own "when this enters" abilities wait until it's ready.
    • Deployment Time 0: They resolve right away, as part of its Play Creature Action in the Summon layer, like Pokémon's Intimidate. The effect stays even if the creature dies to the next attack.
    • Deployment Time 1 or more: They trigger when the countdown reaches 0 at Dusk, and resolve in the Summon layer of the next Dawn resolution pass, before the first Planning in which the creature can act. Until then, the opponent has a window to answer it.
    • Other cards still see it arrive right away: OnEnterBattlefield and OnEnterSlot effects on other creatures and slots (e.g., a Landmine, or "whenever an enemy enters") trigger when it lands, whatever its Deployment Time. Only the entering creature's own abilities wait.
Deployment Time Is Special
    • Its countdown and its OnDeploymentTimeComplete trigger ignore general Abilities Disabled states (like Muted or Exhausted) and other normal status rules.
    • When Deployment Time runs out, OnDeploymentTimeComplete triggers and its effects are queued, even if the creature is in the Abilities Disabled state at that moment. This is the one exception to Abilities Disabled stopping passive abilities.
    • Only an effect that explicitly mentions Deployment Time (its countdown, its completion, the OnDeploymentTimeComplete trigger, or the resulting actions) can change or stop this process.

3.3 Creatures & Action Types

These rules apply to both sides.

• Creature Stats: Creatures have base Attack, Health (their maximum Health), and Speed, printed on their card.
    • Base Stats: The printed values. They can be changed for the rest of a combat by Persistent Stat Modifications, or for as long as that creature stays on the battlefield by a Mandela Effect.
    • Temporary Stat Modifications: Short-term changes added on top of the current base stats.
    • How Stats Are Shown: FinalValue (CurrentBase + SumOfTemporaryModifiers). Persistent mods change CurrentBase.
        • Example: A 3/3/3 creature gets a +1/+1/+1 Persistent Mod. Its base becomes 4/4/4 for the combat. If it then gets a +2/+2/+2 Temporary Mod, its stats are 6(4+2) / 6(4+2) / 6(4+2).
    • Current Health (HP): Tracked separately; shows how much damage the creature has taken from its current maximum Health.
    • Stacking: Multiple Temporary mods add together. Multiple Persistent mods add together. Both count toward the final stats.
    • Calculation Order: First add every flat change (Temporary mods, and flat stat changes from statuses like Addicted's +1 Speed) to CurrentBase. Then apply every multiplier (e.g., Heavy x2, Chill /2). Round down once, at the very end, never between steps.
        • Example: Attack 3 with a +2 Temporary Mod and Heavy: (3+2) x2 = 10. With Chill as well: (3+2) x2 /2 = 5. Attack 7 with only Chill: 7/2 = 3.5, rounded down to 3.
    • Minimum Values: Attack, Speed and maximum Health can drop to 0, never below. A creature with 0 Attack deals no damage; weakening a threat buys time to set up.
        • Health Changes Reverse: A change to the Health stat moves maximum and current Health together, and when it ends, it reverses exactly. A +X Health that ends lowers both by X; a temporary -X Health that ends raises both by X.
            • Example: A 5 Health creature gets +2 Health (5/7 -> 7/7), takes 3 damage (4/7), then the buff ends: 2/5. If that leaves it at 0 or less, it dies: the buff was all that kept it alive.
        • Health 0: When a stat change (e.g., Health Loss, or a +Health buff ending) drops a creature's maximum or current Health to 0, it is marked for destruction the moment that change is applied. It follows the Creature Death Sequence from the Lethal Check (3.9); no damage was dealt, so OnDamaged doesn't trigger.
    • Stats and Statuses Are Independent: Stat modifications and status effects are separate layers, like stat stages and status conditions in Pokémon. A creature can have a raised Attack and a status at the same time, and removing one never removes the other.
        • A status that changes a stat (e.g., Chill halving Attack) applies that change only while the status is on the creature. It isn't a Temporary Stat Modification, and it ends when the status ends.
    • Durations: Temporary Stat Modifications last for the number of Days the effect says, or until the creature Leaves Play. Persistent Stat Modifications stay on the card for the rest of the combat (see Creature Stat Reset in 3.5).

• Passive Abilities: Always "on" in the background.
    • Automatic Triggering: Whenever their conditions are met, they ALWAYS trigger automatically. You don't decide anything when they trigger.
    • When They Don't Trigger: When the creature is in the Abilities Disabled state (e.g., from Exhausted, Muted, Ostracised, or a consumed Brainwashed), or has Deployment Time (except OnDeploymentTimeComplete).
    • What Happens When They Trigger: The effect becomes a 'Creature Ability Effect Action' and is added to the Action Queue.
    • Targeting: Targets are set by the card's text (e.g., "Homing: the attacker," "Static: adjacent slots," "Homing: the creature that triggered this"). If the card says random, the game picks randomly. Nobody chooses targets for passive abilities when they trigger.
    • No Opponent Choices: When a passive ability affects an enemy, the enemy doesn't get to choose how it applies. The outcome is fixed by the card and the game state.
    • One Ability per Creature: A creature has at most one ability. Gaining a new one (copying, swapping, spreading) replaces the old one.
    • Ability Changes Last Until Reshuffle: A gained or lost ability stays on the card, even through leaving play, until the card is reshuffled into the deck or the combat ends, like Persistent Stat Modifications. Details in the 'Abilities Compendium'.

Creature Roles
    • Support/Specialists: May have no standard Attack. Instead they have an Active Ability (deal damage, heal, apply a status) that their controller queues during Planning.
    • Using an Active Ability: hold Shift and drag the creature onto the creature it's for; it replaces its attack or move for the Day. A creature that can't attack (Bodyguard) needs no Shift: dragged onto another friendly creature, it uses its ability. Abilities that only help a friendly creature land with the spells, before the attacks; the others in the Ability and Attack layer, by Speed. Cooldown X rests an Active Ability for X Days; "once per combat" spends it until the combat ends.
    • Building blocks: every mechanic works as an ability, a trigger or a spell. Protection is the status Untouchable; copying stats, stealing stat raises, swapping places and gaining an ability are effects any card can carry; an ability given by a card replaces the creature's own until its card is reshuffled. On a spell, the effects between two creatures work on the target and the creature facing it.
        • Additional costs are paid instantly during Planning, like any other additional cost.
        • Targets are chosen when the ability is queued, using Homing:, Fixed:, or Static:.
        • Using the ability is that creature's one primary intent for the Day.
        • They resolve as 'Creature Ability Effect Actions' in the Ability & Attack Layer, and are blocked by Deployment Time.
    • Automated Units: May have no Attack. They usually have strong passive abilities that trigger every Day (e.g., "Each Dawn: Static: deal 1 damage to a random enemy slot"; "Each Dusk: grant 1 Armor to adjacent creatures").
    • "Must Act" Creatures: Some creatures, or statuses like Bloodthirst, give penalties if their controller doesn't queue a certain action (like an Attack) for them.
        • (Future Idea: a "Compelled Action" mechanic that queues a default action automatically. Not part of the rules yet.)

• Attack Actions: Queued by the controller during Planning, as the creature's one primary intent.
    • They resolve in the Ability & Attack Layer.
    • Targets are chosen when the attack is queued, using Homing:, Fixed:, or Static:. Cards may restrict targets (e.g., only the slot directly in front, can't target Ghosted creatures).
    • Attacks may target an empty enemy slot. An attack that resolves into an empty enemy slot hits the defending player (see 4.6 Combat, Damage & Targeting).
    • Additional costs on an Attack are paid instantly during Planning.

• Spell Actions: Played from hand (or from the AI's script), e.g., by dragging onto a target.
    • Additional costs (including Requisites) are paid when the card is played.
    • Targets are chosen when the card is played.
    • Where It Lands: A spell aimed at a target is dropped on a slot, on either side. A spell that hits a whole side (its player, or every creature there) is dropped anywhere on that side's battlefield, and can't be played on a side it does nothing to. One that plays on either side alike (a Trend, or one that does something to both sides) can be dropped anywhere on the table between the hands, the river between the battlefields included. While a card is dragged, only where it can land lights up: the slot under it, the whole battlefield, or both battlefields. Nothing lights up under the pointer when no card is dragged.
    • Spell Ghosts: a queued spell leaves the hand and shows as a translucent ghost where it will land, so the board says what's coming: in its slot when that slot is empty, otherwise small on the slot's edge facing the other side (the creature there stays in sight); a spell for a whole side, small in the middle of that battlefield's edge, the battlefield glowing. Several at one place (up to about 4) share it: the newest in the middle, each older one a step to the left and partly under the next; any of them can still be hovered, and clicked to take it back. A lone spell in an empty slot sits in it; a second one there puts both on the edge. Like a queued creature's ghost, it's hovered for its enlarged view, clicked or right-clicked to take the play back (the card returns to the hand), and dragged to aim it elsewhere or away to take it back. When it's cast, the ghost flies to the middle and bursts. The enemy's are card backs.
    • The effect becomes a 'Spell Action' and resolves in the Spell Layer.
    • Spells can cause movement, apply status effects, deal damage, heal, or change card draw.
    • Ordering: Spells have no Speed, so Spell Actions resolve in random order (both sides mixed). Future cards might give Spells their own Speed.

• Movement Actions: Moving isn't a default action every creature has.
    • Whenever a creature moves itself (from its own Active or Passive Ability, or because a Spell or ability tells it to move), it creates a Movement Action.
    • Movement Actions resolve in the Movement Layer and are blocked by Cannot Act, Anchored, and Deployment Time.
    • A self-chosen move is the creature's one primary intent for the Day.
    • Pushing, pulling, or swapping a creature with an outside effect does not create a Movement Action for that creature, and ignores those restrictions.
    • Changing sides through Recruitment or Conversion doesn't create a Movement Action either (see 5.4 Recruitment & Conversion).

• Determinism: There are no random success/failure rolls. Randomness only happens where it's stated: cards that pick random targets, deck shuffling, the last step of tie-breaking, ties between Spell Actions, and which cards get revealed from a hand.
    • Once an action starts resolving, its outcome is decided by its text and the game state. Neither side makes further choices, unless a Watchdog, a Linked Meta-Action, or a state change during resolution says otherwise.

3.4 Combats: Start, End & What Carries Over

• Start of Combat: Both sides start at 20 Player Health. Your whole deck is shuffled and you draw an opening hand of 6 cards before Day 1.
• End of Combat: The game checks after every action resolves. The combat ends the moment either side's Player Health reaches 0, and everything still in the queue is dropped. If yours reached 0, the run ends.
    • Both at 0: If one action brings both sides to 0, it's a tie. This is PvE, so a tie counts as your defeat: game over, no win screen.
• What Carries Over to the Next Combat: Only your deck (all cards you own for the run, including newly gained and captured cards).
• What Does NOT Carry Over: Everything else is reset: your hand, discard pile and battlefield are gathered back into your deck; all status effects, Temporary Stat Modifications, Persistent Stat Modifications and Mandela Effect changes are removed; Slot Effects are cleared; Player Health resets to 20. Cards go back to their printed stats.

3.5 Cards, Hand & Deck

The Hand (the user, 2026-10-16)
    • Creatures: at Dawn the creatures left in your hand from the Day before are discarded, and you draw creatures until you
      hold 3 (from the top of your deck, skipping its spells). You always have 3 creatures to choose from.
    • Spells: they stay in your hand until you play them. You draw 2 more at Dawn, never past 5 in hand.
    • The opening hand is a Day's: 3 creatures and 2 spells.
    • Extra draws ("draw X next Day") are spells while you hold fewer than 5, creatures past that (and a creature when your
      deck has no spell left, rather than reshuffling for one). Fewer cards ("draw X fewer next Day") cut the spells first,
      then the creatures.
    • Reshuffling: when the deck has none of the kind you need, the discard pile is shuffled back into it.
    • Ripening (2026-10-16, from Slay the Spire's Retain cards that grow): at each Dawn, every Ripening spell still in your
      hand ripens: its numbers (damage, healing, Armor, cards drawn, stat changes, stacks) go up by 1, up to +3. Played or
      discarded, the next copy starts fresh. Holding a spell becomes a choice instead of a leftover.
    • Laid Off (2026-10-16, from Slay the Spire's discard cards): a card discarded from your hand (a creature left unplayed at
      Dawn, a card given up to pay a discard cost) does what its "Laid Off" text says, as your effect, its targets picked then.
    • Potions (an adventure's): Single Use spells carried from fight to fight (3 at most), in your opening hand, not counted
      among your spells. One you don't use stays with you.

Drawing Cards (Scheduled Draw)
    • Regular Draw: see The Hand above. Besides the opening hand, this is the only time cards move from your deck to your hand.
    • Draw Amount: Base Draw (2) + all draw modifiers gained during the previous Day.
    • Minimum Draw: You always draw at least 1 card, as long as your deck (or your discard pile, through reshuffling) has one.
    • No Mid-Day Draws: No effect lets you draw from your deck in the middle of a Day.

Draw Manipulation (Changing Next Day's Draw)
    • More cards: "Schedule X additional card draws for your next Day." This adds to a counter for the next Day's draw.
    • Every "draw X" that resolves during the Day or at Dusk works this way: the Day's creatures are about to be discarded, so the cards come with the next Day's hand (printed "draw X next Day"). Only a Dawn draw lands in the hand right away, before planning.
    • Fewer cards: "Draw X fewer cards next Day" or "Scheduled draw reduced by X." This subtracts from the counter (down to the minimum of 1).
    • All modifiers gained during a Day are added together and applied at the next Dawn.

• Discard Pile: Where used Spells, destroyed Creatures, and discarded cards go.

Discarding from Hand
    As an Additional Cost
        • The main way you discard cards is to pay an additional cost to play a card or use an ability. This applies to any card or ability with such a cost.
        • Additional costs (including Requisites and "As an additional cost..." text) are always paid during Planning, instantly, when you play the card or queue the ability. They are never paid while an action is resolving.
        • Pre-Check: The game first checks whether you can pay every mandatory additional cost.
        • Payment: If you can, you pay immediately. For discard costs, you choose the cards and they go to the discard pile right away. The card's effect is then added to the queue.
        • If You Can't Pay: The play is cancelled, the card stays in your hand, and you get a message explaining why (e.g., "Insufficient cards to discard," "No valid creature to sacrifice").
        • Condition Costs: Some costs are conditions instead of payments (e.g., "you have fewer than X cards in hand"). They're checked the same way.
    • Forced Discards Don't Exist: Effects that resolve from the queue can never make a player discard from hand. An effect that would traditionally say "Target player discards 1 card" instead says "Target player draws 1 fewer card next Day." Card effects change future resources, not your current hand, which keeps resolution flowing smoothly.
    • Hand Size: The end-of-Dusk hand size check (below) is the only other way cards are discarded from hand.

• Banish: Permanently removes a card (from hand, board, discard pile, or deck) for the rest of the run.
    • A Banished creature is not "Destroyed": OnDied and "when a creature is Destroyed..." abilities don't trigger.
    • OnLeaveBattlefield effects do trigger, with Banish as the reason.

Card Lifecycle & Reshuffling
    • A Spell is played -> its effect resolves -> the Spell card goes to the discard pile.
    • A Creature is destroyed (0 HP or a "destroy" effect) or discarded from the battlefield -> it goes to the discard pile.
    • When to Reshuffle: Only at Dawn, when your deck has fewer cards than you need to draw.
    • How It Works: Draw what's left in your deck. Then shuffle your entire discard pile to form your new deck, and keep drawing until you've drawn the scheduled amount. If there still aren't enough cards, draw everything available. No cards are created if both deck and discard pile are empty.
    • Reshuffled cards lose all their stat modifications (both Temporary and Persistent) and go back to printed stats.
    • Going over your maximum hand size is only checked at the end of Dusk.

Creature Stat Reset
    • Entering the Battlefield: A creature enters with full Health, based on its current base stats (including any Persistent Stat Modifications the card still has).
    • Leaving the Battlefield: Temporary Stat Modifications and Armor are lost, unless the effect moving it says otherwise. Persistent Stat Modifications stay on the card in hand or in the discard pile, so a Revived or replayed creature comes back with them.
    • Reshuffle: Removes all stat modifications from the reshuffled cards.
    • End of Combat: Removes all stat modifications from every card.
    • Mandela Effect: Belongs to the creature on the battlefield, not to the card. It ends when that creature Leaves Play, and at the latest at the end of combat.
    • Status Effects: Removed when a creature Leaves Play, unless the status or another effect says otherwise.
    • Deployment Time: Starts again from the card's value (if 1 or more) every time a creature enters play.

• Requisites: Some powerful cards have conditions you must meet to play them or use their abilities. They are additional costs: pre-checked and paid instantly during Planning. If you can't meet one, you can't play the card or use the ability, and you get feedback.

• Maximum Hand Size: 10 cards.
    • Checked at the very end of Dusk, after "Each Dusk" triggers and status processing.
    • If you have more than 10 cards then, you choose cards to discard down to 10.
    • Going over 10 at any other moment is fine.

• Enemy Resources: Most enemies don't have a hand. They act from their script. Bosses may have a hand-like mechanic (see 'Enemy Compendium').

Revealing Cards from Your Hand
    • Some enemy effects make you show ("reveal") cards from your hand. Enemy scripts and abilities can react to what they see.
    • One-Time Look: The enemy sees the card's full details when the effect resolves. It isn't continuously visible.
    • Stays in Hand: The revealed card stays in your hand.
    • Which Cards: The effect says how many cards and may require a type (e.g., "reveal a Creature"). The game picks randomly among the matching cards.

• Other Deck Interactions: Cards may let you look at, reorder, or move cards within your deck. These are defined on the cards themselves and are separate from Scheduled Draw and discards.

3.6 The Day (Turn Structure)

Both sides go through the same five phases every Day.

• 1. Dawn
    • You get your Scheduled Draw.
    • "Each Dawn" triggers (OnTurnStart), plus the "When Ready" abilities of creatures whose Deployment Time ended at the previous Dusk, are queued and resolve right away in a Dawn resolution pass, using the normal resolution order. Anything they cause (like a Burnout explosion) also resolves in this pass. You see the results before you plan.

• 2. Planning
    • The AI plans first, following its script. It queues 'Play Creature Actions', 'Movement Actions', 'Spell Actions', 'Attack Actions', and Active 'Creature Ability Effect Actions'. Its intents are shown to you, except for "???" parts and Concealed Actions.
    Then you plan. For each creature you control on the battlefield, you can assign at most one primary intent
        • A Movement Action,
        • An Attack Action, OR
        • An Active Creature Ability Effect Action (from the creature's card).
    • Assigning a new intent replaces that creature's previous one for this Day.
    • What's available depends on the creature's card, stats, and current state (e.g., no Attack stat means no Attack; Cannot Act means no intents at all).
    • Card Plays: Separately from intents, you can play creature cards ('Play Creature Actions') and spell cards ('Spell Actions') from your hand.
    • Costs: All additional costs, for both sides, are paid instantly during this phase.
    • Intelligence: Effects that only reveal information resolve instantly, so you can use what you learn this same Planning (see 2.5 Fog of War & Intelligence).
    • Passive Triggers: Planning doesn't stop passives from triggering if something meets their conditions, though most passives trigger from phase changes or resolving actions.

• 3. Briefing
    • Before anything resolves, Briefing Watchdogs look at the whole queue once and change it (see 3.11 Instant Effects - Watchdogs).
    • Only Watchdogs that were already active when the Day's resolution starts can brief.

• 4. Resolution
    • The queue resolves in a main resolution pass, following 3.7 Resolution Order.
    • Just before each action resolves, Monitored Watchdogs and Traps that match it react first; anything they create resolves immediately, ahead of it.
    • Passives that trigger during resolution add their actions to the queue.
    • Linked Meta-Actions are processed as part of the action they're linked to.
    • IMPORTANT: EFFECTS ARE DELAYED BY THE QUEUE.
        • Playing a card, or meeting an ability's trigger condition, doesn't make anything happen yet.
        • No creature appears, no status is applied, no damage is dealt, and no spell takes effect until its action resolves in a resolution pass (Dawn, main, or Dusk).
        • Costs are paid up front during Planning; the effects wait for the queue.

• 5. Dusk
    • Turn History is updated for both sides.
    • Every creature's Armor fades by 1.
    • "Each Dusk" triggers (OnTurnEnd) are queued and resolve right away in a Dusk resolution pass.
    • Status effect durations and stacks decay (see 5.2 Status Effects), and Deployment Time counts down (see 3.2 Tempo & Deployment Time).
    • Finally, the maximum hand size check happens.

Timing Map
    • Planning (instant): Scan layer. One-time reveals and Surveillance. E.g., Reveal, CCTV, Revealed.
    • Briefing (before resolution): Watchdogs set up on earlier Days that need the whole queue. E.g., "Nullify their strongest attack".
    • Just before each action: Reactions. Monitored Watchdogs and Traps; what they create resolves immediately. E.g., Firewall, Landmine, Binding Chains.
    • After each action: Retaliation and normal triggers, queued. E.g., OnAttacked, OnDied.

3.7 Resolution Order

• Layers: Every resolution pass goes through four layers, always in this order:
    1. Movement Layer: All 'Movement Actions'.
    2. Summon Layer: All 'Play Creature Actions'.
        • Summoning onto an Occupied Slot: If the target friendly slot is occupied, the creature already there is discarded (moved to its owner's discard pile). It is not destroyed: OnLeaveBattlefield triggers, but OnAboutToDie and OnDied don't, so it can't be saved. Then the new creature enters the slot. The same rule applies to multi-slot creatures.
    3. Spell Layer: All 'Spell Actions'.
    4. Ability & Attack Layer: All 'Creature Ability Effect Actions' (from passive or active abilities, plus traps and Slot Effects) and all 'Attack Actions'.

• New Actions During a Pass: An action created during a pass joins the queue in its layer.
    • If its layer is the current one or a later one, it resolves in this pass.
    • If its layer has already finished, it waits. After the Ability & Attack Layer, if any actions are still waiting, the layers run again from the Movement Layer. This repeats until the queue is empty.
    • Example: A Spell tells an enemy creature to move itself. The Movement Action it creates waits until the Ability & Attack Layer is done, then resolves when the layers loop back to Movement.
    • Exception: OnDamaged and OnAboutToDie effects resolve immediately (see 3.9 Creature Death Sequence).
    • Part of the Action: What an action does resolves as part of it, right away: an attack's hit, a spell's effects, a creature's entry ("When Ready") effects. Only abilities that trigger along the way wait in their layer.
    • Exception: Environment actions (triggered Slot Effects and Traps) also resolve immediately, before the next action, whatever their layer. Stepping on a Landmine hits before anything else happens. If several trigger at once, they resolve in random order.
    • Chain Limit: If one action sets off more than 20 follow-up actions in the same resolution pass (triggers setting off triggers), the rest fizzle. This is a safety net for edge cases, not a strategy (see 5.2 Status Effects - Bounces and Chains).

Order Inside a Layer
    • Movement Layer exception: Movement Actions resolve in an order the game picks so every planned move works, not by Speed (see 2.4 The Battlefield - Automatic Move Order).
    0. Environment actions always go first (see the exception above).
    1. Speed of the creature that created the action: higher first. For a 'Play Creature Action', use the stats the creature will enter with. Spell Actions have no Speed and go straight to a random order.
    2. Standard Tie-Breaking.
    • No Priority: There is no separate Priority number on actions. Order inside a layer comes from Speed only, so changing it means changing Speed (or a Watchdog's Reorder). This keeps the already layered resolution readable.
    • Actions Without a Living Creature: An action created by a creature that is no longer on the battlefield (e.g., OnDied effects, a Prophecy, a status ticking) uses that creature's last known stats for Speed and tie-breaking.

Standard Tie-Breaking (when Speed is tied)
    • One rule for everyone: it settles ties between your own actions too, never the order they were planned in.
    • Lowest current (Health + Armor) resolves first.
    • If still tied, lowest base Attack resolves first.
    • If still tied, random (a possible future Karma System might influence this).

Changing the Order
    • Speed changes: Raising or lowering a creature's Speed (a stat change) moves its actions earlier or later in their layer.
    • Watchdogs can also reorder actions within their layer (see 3.11 Instant Effects - Watchdogs).
    • Global Modifiers: Effects that change ordering for a set time.
        • Example ("Temporal Distortion"): For X Days, Speed order is flipped: lower Speed acts first. Ties still use Standard Tie-Breaking.

• Queue Visibility: Besides Watchdogs, some effects only affect what each side can see of the queue (e.g., Intelligence revealing pending actions). Those resolve instantly (see 2.5 Fog of War & Intelligence).

3.8 State Changes During Resolution

When a creature's state changes during resolution (e.g., it gets Muted, Cannot Act, or a Watchdog starts watching it), the change immediately affects its pending actions and how other pending actions interact with it.

• Disabling Statuses: States like Abilities Disabled (e.g., from Muted) or Cannot Act can cancel that creature's pending actions right before they would resolve. Statuses that make a creature untargetable (like Ostracised) can make actions aimed at it fizzle. Each status's exact behavior is in the 'Status Effects Compendium'.
• Watchdogs: Once active, they check every matching action as it comes up to resolve.
• Validity Check: Right before each action resolves, the game checks whether it is still valid, whether its target is still valid, and whether it should be cancelled, based on the current state.

3.9 Creature Death Sequence

• Retaliatory Triggers: Triggers that react to something being done to the creature: OnDamaged, OnAttacked, OnAboutToDie, OnDied, and any other "when this is targeted / hit / killed" trigger. They are never cancelled by their creature's death.
    • OnDamaged and OnAboutToDie effects resolve immediately, interrupting the normal resolution order.
    • OnDied effects are queued normally (Ability & Attack Layer).
    • Retaliation Always Fires: A retaliatory trigger always fires when its event happens, unless the creature's abilities are disabled at that moment (e.g., Muted, Exhausted, Deployment Time, a Jamming Field), it stands on a Scorched Earth slot (which stops its triggered effects), or an effect specifically blocks it. A creature killed before it's ready loses its death effect.
    • OnAttack and OnAttacked are not immediate: they're queued normally and resolve after the attack.

• The Sequence: When a creature takes damage, or an effect tries to destroy it:
    1. Damage is applied (Armor first, then Health).
    2. OnDamaged effects resolve, whether or not the damage was lethal. They can affect other things before the creature is removed. (Skipped if no damage was dealt.)
    3. Lethal Check: If its Health is 0 or less, or a destroy effect targeted it, the creature is marked for destruction.
    4. Cancel Pending Actions: All of its other pending actions that it created before being marked (Attacks, Movement Actions, Active Abilities, earlier passive triggers, and non-retaliatory status effects) are removed from the queue.
    5. OnAboutToDie effects resolve. They might save the creature.
    6. Destruction: If still marked, the creature is destroyed and moved to its owner's discard pile (even if another player controlled it). Its OnDied effects are queued.

3.10 Everything Goes Through the Queue

• No card effect happens instantly unless it is a Meta-Action (see 3.11 Instant Effects).
• Every effect from a Spell or creature ability (active or passive), including movement, becomes an action in the queue: Play Creature Action, Movement Action, Spell Action, Creature Ability Effect Action, or Attack Action.
• Every attack is an Attack Action.
• Every triggered Trap, Strategic Disclosure, or Slot Effect is queued as a Creature Ability Effect Action. Traps and Slot Effects (environment actions) resolve as soon as they're queued (see 3.7 Resolution Order).
• Paying costs, replacing Slot Effects, drawing at Dawn, and Intelligence effects that only reveal information are not queued actions.

3.11 Instant Effects (Meta-Actions)

Meta-Actions are game state changes that happen instantly instead of going through the queue.

• Slot Effect Replacement: When a new Slot Effect is applied to a slot that already has one, the old one is removed immediately and the new one takes its place.

• Paying Additional Costs: Discards, sacrifices, Requisites, and condition costs are paid during Planning, before the card's effect is added to the queue.

• "1 Trigger" Slot Effects: When a "1 Trigger" Slot Effect's condition is met, its effect is queued and the Slot Effect is immediately removed from the slot, so it can't trigger twice before its effect resolves.

• Information Is Instant: Intelligence effects that only change what a side can see (Reveal, Surveillance, applying Revealed) resolve immediately during Planning. Their consequences (Traps, Strategic Disclosures, OnRevealedByIntelligence effects) are queued normally.

Reactions and Retaliation
    • Reactions (Watchdogs, Traps, environment actions) happen before the action that triggers them. Anything they create resolves immediately, ahead of it. They're rare and expensive.
    • Retaliation (OnAttacked, OnDamaged, OnDied...) happens after, queued normally. It's common and cheap (see 3.9 Creature Death Sequence).

Watchdogs
    • What They Are: Intelligence assets that watch the action queue. They aren't creatures and never attack; they only change the queue when their condition is met.
    • Created Through the Queue: A Spell or creature ability creates a Watchdog when its action resolves. From then on the Watchdog is active.
    • Card Format: "Watchdog: [condition] -> [effect], N charges (timing)".
        • Conditions: each Watchdog card has its own, made for it (ad hoc): what it reacts to is part of the card's design, not a shared rule. The building blocks so far: action type (Attack, Spell, Move, Ability, Summon, Status), side (enemy or friendly), which creature or slot it comes from or targets, and, for attacks, a number ("Attack 3 or less"). Filters that keep coming back may later become shared templates.
        • Effects: Nullify (resolves with no effect, stays visible in the Turn History with the reason), Redirect (new target), Reorder (move within its layer), Create (queue a new action, which resolves immediately), Reveal (show the action, even if hidden).
    • Two Timings:
        • Monitored (default): Just before each action resolves, it checks whether that action matches. It also catches actions that were queued before the Watchdog existed, as long as they haven't resolved yet. It can only move an action later, never earlier.
        • Briefing: Once per Day, in the Briefing phase, it looks at the whole queue before anything resolves. It can pick ("the strongest", "the first", "every"), move actions earlier or later within their layer, nullify or redirect. One set while the Day resolves briefs at once, over what's still to come (the user, 2026-10-17: online the Day it's set, like a counter; it used to wait for the next Day). Once a Day either way.
    • Charges: Each action it changes uses 1 charge: the condition says which actions it reacts to, the charges how many times it can. A Briefing Watchdog that picks "every" match changes as many as it has charges for. A Watchdog can last several Days and disappears when its last charge is used (or when a duration on the card runs out).
    • Several Matches: When several Watchdogs match the same action, only the oldest one triggers.
    • Can't Match the Unseen: A Watchdog can't match an action it can't see (Concealed, Undercover). Encrypted creatures' actions are ignored, and no charge is spent.
    • Visibility: The opponent sees that a Watchdog exists and what it watches (a creature or a side). Its condition shows as "???" until revealed by Intelligence.
    • Turn History: Changes are always shown with their source, e.g., "Attack nullified by Watchdog (Firewall)", or "by ???" if the Watchdog is still hidden.
    Example
        1. During Planning, the AI queues Spell S: "Watchdog: attacks by Creature C -> Redirect to a random creature, 1 charge, this Day".
        2. You queue an Attack Action from Creature C targeting Enemy Creature O.
        3. During resolution, Spell S resolves in the Spell Layer. The Watchdog is now active on Creature C.
        4. In the Ability & Attack Layer, Creature C's attack comes up. The Watchdog sees it and redirects it to a random creature, using its only charge.
        5. Creature C's attack resolves against the new target. The Turn History shows the redirect and its source.

Linked Meta-Actions
    • What They Are: Follow-up effects tied to an action's success, usually phrased "immediately gains [Status X]" or "then [do Y]".
    • How They Work: If the main action resolves successfully, the follow-up is processed immediately as part of that same action. It doesn't go into the queue.
    • Dependency: If the main action fails (e.g., it's Nullified, or its target became invalid), the follow-up doesn't happen.
    • Example: "Homing: Deal 2 damage to target creature. If this destroys the creature, its controller draws 1 fewer card next Day." The draw reduction only happens if the damage destroys the creature. Likewise, "immediately gains [Status X]" after a control change (e.g., Taqiyya Infiltrator) is applied as part of the control change itself.

• Subscriptions: Using a Subscription during Planning resolves immediately, without the queue.
• Consumable Items: (Experimental, to be defined.)


4. GAMEPLAY STRATEGIES

4.1 Information Warfare

• See: Reveal "???" intents, enemy abilities and Watchdog conditions instantly during Planning, then plan around them. Avoid enemy Traps.
• Change: Use Watchdogs to nullify, redirect or reorder the enemy's key actions. Enemies have Watchdogs too.
• Protect: Hide your plan (Concealed, Undercover), lock it (Encrypted), or punish snoopers (Honeypot).

4.2 Board Control

Movement (Logistics)
    • Use abilities or Spells to move creatures (yours or the enemy's) or swap their positions.
    • A creature moving itself creates a Movement Action; outside pushes, pulls, and swaps don't.
    • Movement helps you line up attacks, blocks, and adjacency bonuses, and breaks enemy formations.
    • Tactics include "Hit and Run" (attack, then reposition) and Positional Relays (buffs triggered by moving into a slot that just became empty).

Movement Restriction
    • Use Heavy, Anchored (the creature can't make its own Movement Actions), abilities that stop Movement Actions, or Slot Effects to lock down enemies or protect your creatures.

• Slot Effects (Environmental Hazards): Ongoing or triggered conditions on battlefield slots. Each one is defined in the 'Slot Effects Compendium' (e.g., Landmine, Scorched Earth). Whoever applied a Slot Effect controls it.
    • One Per Slot: A new Slot Effect immediately replaces the old one.
    Visibility
        • Visible Slot Effects: Both sides can see them and know what they are.
        • Hidden Slot Effects: The AI doesn't know they exist until they trigger (e.g., an enemy creature moves onto a hidden Landmine) or are revealed (e.g., by an "Inspect Slot" Intelligence action). A hidden Slot Effect usually reveals itself when it triggers.
    • Triggers: Defined per Slot Effect, using events from the 'Trigger Events List'. Common ones: OnEnterSlot, OnBeforeLeaveSlot, "Each Dawn" or "Each Dusk" while occupied, or a continuous effect while a creature is in the slot.
    • "1 Trigger" Effects: Used up the moment their effect is queued.
    • What They Do: Deal damage, apply status effects to creatures in the slot, change stats while on the slot, change movement rules (e.g., make moving out harder or impossible), or restrict actions (e.g., a "Jamming Field" stops Spells from targeting the slot).
    • Removing Slot Effects: Effects with the Cleanse (SlotEffect) keyword remove them. A hidden Slot Effect cleansed before it triggers is removed without its effect and without being revealed to the AI (unless the cleanse itself reveals it). The 'Slot Effects Compendium' says which Slot Effects can be cleansed.

• Trends: what the world is into right now. Cards that set one rule for the whole board (printed "Trend: Rush Hour"; one word, Trend, everywhere), for the Days they print (no number: until another Trend replaces them). One Trend at a time: a new one replaces the old. A Trend card plays on either side of the table and resolves with the spells, so it already rules this Day's attacks. It's Zeitgeist's signature (the mood of the times changes the rules for everyone). A Trend sits in a banner in the middle of the table, with its Days left and its rule; the banner is hidden while there's none.
    • Zeitgeist (Trend): each Day, whoever has fewer creatures draws 1 more card, on top of the Day's hand (a tie: nobody).
    • Rush Hour (Trend): the slowest act first. Lower Speed goes first wherever Speed decides the order (summons, abilities and attacks); the other tie-breakers stay as they are.
    • The effect that sets a Trend works on any card (a spell, a creature's trigger); a card that only sets a Trend is a "Trend" on its type line.

• Flooding: Overwhelming the board with many weak or token creatures.

• Recruitment / Conversion: Taking control of enemy creatures, mainly to capture their cards (see 5.4 Recruitment & Conversion).

4.3 Buffs & Protection

• Setup: Effects that raise Attack, Health, or Speed of your creatures. Usually Temporary Stat Modifications; less often, and at a higher cost, Persistent Stat Modifications.
• Survivability: Healing, adding Armor, or redirecting attacks.
• Immunity/Protection: Immunity to specific negative effects.
• Cleansing: Removing negative status effects or stat decreases from your creatures (Cleanse), everything good and bad from a creature (Purge), or Slot Effects from slots (Cleanse (SlotEffect)). Neither removes Persistent Stat Modifications.

4.4 Disruption & Control

• Status Effects: Applying status effects (from the 'Status Effects Compendium') to enemy or friendly creatures.
• Red Tape: Delaying an opponent's actions, or adding Days to a creature's Deployment Time (a ready creature goes back to waiting, sitting out the rest of the Day and the added Days; its entry effects don't happen again).
• Disable: Shutting down key enemy functions, e.g., with Muted, or stunning a creature for 1 Day with Exhausted.
• Changing Queued Actions: Watchdogs are the only tool that changes actions already in the queue (see 3.11 Instant Effects - Watchdogs).
    • Briefing Watchdogs, set up on earlier Days, look at the whole queue before resolution.
    • Monitored Watchdogs (e.g., "Mental Breakdown") change matching actions just as they're about to resolve, including actions queued earlier in the Day.
    • UI: When a Watchdog changes an action, the UI must clearly show it and its source.
• Recruitment / Conversion / Mind Control: Taking control of an enemy creature or capturing its card (see 5.4 Recruitment & Conversion).

4.5 Recursion & Recovery

• Revive: Returns a creature from your discard pile to an empty friendly slot, at the next Dawn. It's ready at once (no Deployment Time), keeps the stat changes it had (and any Persistent Stat Modifications the card still has), but not its statuses. Its Arrival happens again, and its Last Will happened when it died (the user, 2026-10-15).
• Return to Hand (from Battlefield): Moves a creature from its slot to its owner's hand. It loses Temporary Stat Modifications (unless the effect says otherwise) and keeps Persistent Stat Modifications.
• Return to Hand (from Discard): Moves a card from your discard pile to your hand. It keeps any Persistent Stat Modifications it has.

4.6 Combat, Damage & Targeting

• Combat Damage Requires an Attack: Creatures only deal combat damage with an Attack Action. There's no automatic counter-damage unless an ability grants it (OnDamaged triggers play a similar role).
• Direct Damage: Reduces a creature's Health, or a Player's Health if the effect specifically targets a Player.
• Chain/Spread Damage: Damage that hits several different targets.
• Armor: Damage, including recoil damage, hits Armor first.
• No Minimum Damage: Damage can be reduced to 0 (e.g., an attacker with 0 Attack, or Cognitive Dissonance's -1). Damage is rounded down once, after all modifiers, like stats (see 3.3 Creatures & Action Types).
• Life Drain / Siphoning: Dealing damage also heals the source or its controller.

• Targeting Keywords: Cards say how they target. Without a keyword, an effect aimed at your own creature uses Homing (your side has no fog of war, so you know where your creatures will be, including one queued to be summoned), and an effect aimed at an enemy slot uses Fixed. "Homing:" on a card makes it follow enemy creatures too, which is why it's scarce.
• Card Keywords (like Magic's keyword abilities and Slay the Spire's keyword tooltips): card text is short and leans on keywords, each a highlighted word whose meaning is one hover away. Hovering a keyword on an enlarged card opens its explanation beside it; keywords inside that explanation can be hovered too, and so on, so players can follow a chain (Exhausted -> Exposed). The glossary lives in Keywords.cs.
    • Card text style: one rule per line, no full stop at the end ("Arrival: exhaust itself"). An effect a creature aims at itself says "itself" ("1 damage to itself", "give itself +1 Attack").
    • Enlarging: a hovered card shows enlarged only once the pointer rests on it a moment (0.35 s), so passing over cards on the way to the board never puts one in the way; going on from one enlarged card to the next takes 0.1 s, for reading a hand card by card. The same goes for card names in the menus (keyword glossary, deck builder).
    • Trigger keywords lead a sentence: Arrival (lands, after Deployment Time), Last Will (dies), Dawn, Dusk, Hurt (takes damage), On Hit (its attack lands).
    • Text conventions: what it does first, then whom: "3 damage to target", "All friendly creatures +1 [Armor]", "Exhaust all enemy creatures". "target" is what the card is dropped on, either side (only a card limited to one side says so); it's a plain word, not a keyword. A card aimed at a slot says "target Slot" (Slot is a keyword). Groups are plain words: all creatures, all friendly creatures, all enemy creatures. The same thing done to groups that make up a bigger one is written once for that group ("Exhaust all creatures", not "exhaust all enemy creatures and exhaust all friendly creatures"), a target named again is "it" or "them" ("Exhaust target and Heat Signature 2 on it"), and statuses on the same creatures share them ("Burn 1 [sand watch]2 and Heat Signature on all creatures"). "Random" (a keyword) picks one creature when the effect happens: random creature, random friendly creature, random enemy creature. "Everything" is every creature and both players ("3 damage to everything").
    • Numbers: a status with one number counts its Days ("Doom 3", "Conceal 2"); Bless and Heat count stacks. A status with a strength shows its Days after a sand watch icon ("Burn 2 [sand watch]3": 2 damage a Dusk for 3 Days), and so do timed stat changes ("+4 Attack [sand watch]1"). The sand watch keeps them apart from Attack/Speed/Health. Healing and Health gains are for good; Health that comes back each Day is Regenerate.
    • A 1 goes without saying: "Summon", "Bless", "Doom", "Heat Signature", "Shield", "draw a card", not "Summon 1" or "draw 1". Amounts always show their number, even 1: damage and healing ("1 damage", "heal 1"), and so the keywords that are damage or healing (Burn, Decay, Landmine, Barbed Snare, Regenerate), Armor, stat changes ("+1 Attack", Grow 1) and the sand watch's Days.
    • Faction coins: each faction has a coin in its color, ringed in brass, with a typographic sign for what it's about ($ Corporate, § Statecraft, † Myth, ? Shadow Ops., λ Tech, @ Digital Society, ☺ Zeitgeist, Ψ Mind, ∞ Utopia), on the player plaques and in the menus, always at the left of what it labels and vertically centered: decks and opponents (one coin, two for a two-faction deck; a deck's name reads its faction, then its name), the gallery's factions, the deck builder's tabs and cards. Your own decks show only their coins, the faction with the most cards first (ties by name).
    • Menus: Escape goes back, like the Back button (not while typing in a field). In the game it closes the deck or discard view first, then pauses.
    • Scrolling hides enlarged cards and explanations; they show again once the wheel has been still a moment.
    • Your hand, like Slay the Spire's: the cards are big (1.7 times their size on the board), fanned on a wide arc, overlapping and tilted along it, sunk below the screen's edge so only their name and picture show; each card's Deployment Time is a small square inside its top-left corner, so it's always in sight (only when it has one: a card with none centers its name on the whole title bar). The card under the pointer rises out of the fan, enlarged, straight and whole, its explanations beside it (keywords included), and its neighbours slide aside. Picked up, a card grows a little, trails the pointer and leans into its movement, and shrinks to board size as it's carried over the board. A cast spell leaps to the middle, swells and snaps shut in a ring of light; a discarded card fades into a glowing dart that streaks, trailing sparks, into the discard pile. The opponent's hand at the top is fanned the same way at board size; while its cards are face up (Fog of War off), the one under the pointer comes down out of it enlarged, like yours. Switching sides (F8) makes the other hand the big one at the bottom.
    • Clicking anywhere outside the open Queue panel closes it.
    • Input comes from the Input System package (GameInput reads the mouse and keyboard).
    • Card frames (like Slay the Spire's attack, skill and power frames): the art window's bottom tells the card type at a glance, with no icon. A creature's comes to a point (a pennant); a spell's is a shallow arch.
    • Keyword abilities replace effects that keep coming back, written as one word and a number: Counterattack (a creature that attacks it is hit back for its Attack), Retaliate followed by a status ("Retaliate: Exhaust", "Retaliate: Burn 2 [sand watch]3": a creature that attacks it gets that status, with those numbers), Decay X (takes X damage each Dusk), Regenerate X (heals X each Dusk), Grow X (+X/+X for good each Dawn), Rebirth (once per combat, survives a lethal hit with 1 Health: last life a worm, this life a dog). Only an effect that is exactly the pattern becomes the keyword; anything more is written out.
    • Statuses, Slot Effects, Watchdog terms, movement and creature abilities are keywords too, printed by name without reminder text.
    • Homing: Locks onto a creature and follows it if it moves. If the creature Leaves Play, the effect is aimed at the slot it was last in. Aimed at your creature that is queued to be summoned, it locks on to it; if the summon is taken back, it is aimed at that slot. Dropped on an empty slot, it targets that slot and behaves like Fixed. Against a Concealed enemy creature, Homing behaves like Fixed that one time.
    • Fixed: Aims at a slot, but only ever affects the creature in it when the action resolves. Doesn't follow creatures. Fizzles if the creature is an invalid target. If the slot is empty, a creature-only effect fizzles and damage hits that side's player.
    • Static: Aims at the slot itself. Can change the slot (e.g., place a Slot Effect) and/or affect the creature in it when the action resolves. Doesn't follow creatures. The creature part fizzles if the slot is empty.
    • Creature-Only Effects: Effects that can only apply to a creature (stat changes, statuses, Armor, e.g., "gains X status") fizzle when they resolve into an empty slot. Damage isn't creature-only: it hits the player whose slot it is.

• Damage to Slots & Occupants: Damage aimed at a slot hits the creature in it. If that creature is destroyed, leftover damage from that hit is lost. If the slot is empty, the damage hits the player whose slot it is.

• Attacking Empty Slots: If an Attack Action resolves into an empty enemy slot (whatever its targeting keyword), the defending player loses Player Health equal to the attacker's current Attack. This works both ways: enemy attacks into your empty slots hit you. Damage from Spells and abilities into an empty slot does the same, for its damage amount.
    • Creatures That Can't Be Attacked: If the creature in the slot can't be attacked (e.g., Ghosted or Ostracised), the attack treats the slot as empty and passes through to the defending player. The creature is "not really there".

• Targeting Restrictions: Many cards have limits (e.g., only the slot in front, only Exhausted creatures). These are on the individual cards.


5. SPECIAL SYSTEMS

5.1 Traps & Strategic Disclosures

Effects tied to actions or creatures that trigger under certain conditions, usually around information warfare. A Trap that reacts to an action resolves immediately, before that action (a reaction, see 3.11 Instant Effects). Other Trap and Strategic Disclosure effects are queued as 'Creature Ability Effect Actions'.
• Traps (Counter-Intelligence) are bad for whoever triggers them. Strategic Disclosures are good for you.

• Your Traps: Cards that set traps, e.g., hidden Slot Effects or creature abilities that react to enemy actions.
• Enemy Traps: Abilities on enemy cards (especially bosses and elites) that hurt you when you use certain Intelligence actions on them, or interact with them in ways their card or script defines.
• Strategic Disclosures: Benefits for you (e.g., a temporary combat advantage, Bestiary info, bonus currency (experimental, to be defined)) when you reveal significant hidden enemy information, "???" intents, or weaknesses.

What Triggers "On Being Revealed" Effects (the OnRevealedByIntelligence trigger):
• Your Intelligence action reveals a hidden "???" enemy intent or a hidden enemy card detail.
• You apply the Revealed status to an enemy creature that has the Undercover ability, the Concealed status, or a hidden ability with an "On Being Revealed" characteristic.

5.2 Status Effects

Status effects are temporary or ongoing conditions on creatures that change their behavior or stats. (Conditions on slots are Slot Effects, not status effects.) Every status effect is defined in the 'Status Effects Compendium', which says whether it can be cleansed, how it interacts with Dawn and Dusk, how it decays, and which faction owns it. Each faction owns at most 3 statuses; a small Neutral set (Revealed, Concealed, Exposed, Muted, Anchored) is open to every faction. Deployment Time isn't a status (see 3.2 Tempo & Deployment Time). This GDD always uses their exact names. New status effects must be added to the compendium before use.

Core Principles:

• Answering Statuses: Cleansing is the rare, expensive answer. Every negative status should also be answerable by playing the board: moving (spacing, Push, Swap), timing (every status waits a Day before acting), removing the creature (bounce, summoning over it, sacrifice), passing it on, or exploiting its downside. Each compendium entry lists these in its Answers line. Cleanse cards should be scarce and usually single-target.

Status Timing: Every status skips the Day it's applied. Its effects and its countdown start the next Day, so you always get a full Day to see it and react before it acts.
    • First Tick: A status applied on Day X first acts and loses a stack at the Dusk of Day X+1 (or the Dawn of Day X+2, for a Dawn status like Paranoid). Nothing happens at the Dusk of Day X.
    • Each Tick: The status's effect happens first (damage, healing, "must attack" checks), then it loses 1 stack. By default, statuses tick Each Dusk.
    • Last Stack: When it loses its last stack, the status ends and does whatever its entry says (evolve, spread, pay off).
    • Example: Earworm (2) applied on Day 1: 1 damage at Dusk of Day 2, 1 damage at Dusk of Day 3, then it spreads to adjacent friendly creatures and vanishes from this one.
    • Example: Exhausted (1) applied late in Day X still stuns the creature for all of Day X+1.
    • A status's compendium entry or the effect applying it can override these defaults.
• Fixed_Stacks: A status marked Fixed_Stacks only gets its stack count set when applied or refreshed, and only changes through its own decay or internal rules (like evolving or being consumed). Other effects can't add or remove its stacks unless they specifically say they can (very rare).
• "Upon Application" Is Implied: A status's description lists what it does immediately when applied (when the action applying it resolves), followed by ongoing behavior. "Upon application" is left out to keep descriptions short.
• Effect on Queued Actions: Applying a disabling status (like Muted or one that causes Cannot Act) can immediately cancel that creature's pending actions (see 3.8 State Changes During Resolution). The compendium entry for each such status should say so.
Statuses and Lethal Damage
    • A status that is part of the same action as lethal damage usually fails to apply, because the creature is marked for destruction first. Statuses applied by OnDamaged or OnAboutToDie effects do apply, since those resolve before destruction.
    • A status already on a creature that deals lethal damage to it (like Earworm's damage tick) still resolves that damage. Its follow-up effects that Day (like Earworm's spread) are cancelled, because the creature is gone.
• Duration and Intensity: A status can have two kinds of numbers.
    • Duration: Days left. It counts down each Dusk (see Status Timing). Card text always writes it with Days: "Muted (2 Days)". A status with no Days written lasts until removed.
    • Intensity: how strong it is (e.g., Heat, Burnout, Blessed charges). Time doesn't reduce it. Card text writes it as a count: "gains 2 Heat", "+1 Burnout".
    • A status can have one, both or neither. No gameplay caps for now; they come with balancing (99 is only the display limit).
    • Display: Each creature shows a list of its statuses. Every entry shows its intensity (if it has one) and its Days left (if it's timed), e.g., "Heat 3 | 2 Days".
• Passing a Status: When an effect passes a status (like a curse) from one creature to another without naming a target, it goes to the creature with the lowest current Health on the same side, other than the creature it leaves. Ties are broken randomly. It keeps its remaining stacks. Example: cleansing Doomed passes it on (Heresy, in the 'Status Effects Compendium').
• Bounces and Chains: Some abilities send effects back (No U, Return to Sender in the 'Abilities Compendium').
    • No Source Creature: If the effect came from a Spell from hand, or its creature has left play, it bounces to the caster's creature with the lowest current Health (ties broken randomly). If the caster has no creatures, it fails.
    • Bounced Once: An effect that has been bounced or reflected can't be bounced or reflected again. It simply applies or fails.
    • Chain Limit: As a last safety net, if one action sets off more than 20 follow-up actions in the same resolution pass, the rest fizzle (see 3.7 Resolution Order).
• Exposed from Exhausted: Exhausted is a 1-Day stun that includes Exposed. That Exposed lasts exactly as long as Exhausted and ends with it.
• Brainwashed: Changes the creature's controller (never its owner) at the Dusk of Day X+2, when applied on Day X. The creature is placed on its new controller's side using the Changing Sides rules in 5.4 Recruitment & Conversion.

5.3 Trigger Events

The game events that can trigger passive abilities and other effects are defined in the 'Trigger Events List'. Many triggers carry context (like who dealt the damage, or which status was applied) that abilities can use. Card text and this GDD write OnTurnStart as "Each Dawn" and OnTurnEnd as "Each Dusk".

5.4 Recruitment & Conversion

Two ways to take an enemy creature: Recruitment (temporary control, possible card capture) and Conversion (immediate card capture). Both sides can use them, but only you capture cards.

• Recruitment (used by you): You control the enemy creature for the rest of the combat. If it survives the combat (and meets any extra conditions on the card, like dealing damage to an enemy), its card is "Captured": a copy is guaranteed to be one of your post-combat card reward options.
• Conversion (used by you): Usually rarer or more expensive than Recruitment. The enemy creature is immediately Banished from the enemy side, and a copy of its card is added to your discard pile for the rest of the run.
• Used by the AI: Both effects make the AI take control of your creature for the rest of the combat. Nothing is captured or Banished; your card stays in your deck for the next combat.

• Who Can Be Captured: Most standard enemy creatures. Bosses and some elites (listed in the 'Enemy Compendium') may be immune or have special capture conditions.
• The Captured Card: An exact copy of the creature's card, with its printed stats and abilities.
• Balancing: Recruitment and Conversion are powerful because they gain you cards. They're balanced through cost, targeting limits, and rarity.

• Changing Sides: These rules apply to every control change: Recruitment, Conversion used by the AI, Brainwashed, and any card that changes control (unless the card says otherwise).
• Gift: A creature with Gift is played into an empty enemy slot and joins that side: they control it from then on (its "friendly" creatures are theirs). It stays its giver's card, and goes back to the giver's discard pile when it leaves play. Given into a slot that got taken in the meantime, it fizzles back to the giver's discard pile. Its new side can get rid of it by summoning over it. (Sleeper Agent, the poisoned gift.)
• Destroy: Kills a creature outright, whatever its Health or Armor; Untouchable still saves it. Some Destroys have a requisite checked when they land (Perfectly Timed Camera Malfunction: only a Revealed creature).
• Requisites and "the strongest": any effect can require something of its target, checked when it lands: Revealed, a free slot beside it (Fell Out of a Window), its side outnumbered (Coup d'État), Attack N or less (Operation Paperclip). "The strongest" is the creature with the highest Attack when the effect happens (ties at random).
• Take Control: the creature changes sides for good, by the Changing Sides rules above (Coup d'État, Operation Paperclip, Brainwashed running out, which also takes its ability).
• Back to the Hand: a creature sent back to the hand (Extraction, He Didn't Kill Himself) comes with the next Day's hand (this one is discarded at Dusk), as a fresh copy of its card. A faked death (He Didn't Kill Himself) is believed: its Last Will happens.
• Gone Dark: a creature off the board until the next Dawn (Witness Protection Program). Nothing reaches it; its slot is empty meanwhile. It comes back to its slot (or the first free one) with a new identity: Concealed 2, unknown, old statuses and stat changes gone (a Health raise it lived on can kill it). No free slot: it never comes back.
• Secret statuses: a Kill Order can only be seen by the side that gave it, and a spell that only gives secret statuses is played and cast unseen.
• Tokens and Decoys: a token is made by an effect (Ghost Army), is nobody's card and is gone when it leaves play. A decoy shows the other side a fake card (its numbers too) until it's revealed.
• Discard Cost: to play the card, N other random cards from the hand are given up (set aside, discarded when it resolves, back if the play is taken back). Fewer left: it can't be played. Shadow Ops.' information trades: Social Engineering, Bilderberg Membership, Dead Drop.
• Intelligence Watchdogs watch reveals (a Reveal, Revealed, or a spell that does either, instant ones included: Glomar Response). Backfire: the attack doesn't happen and its attacker takes its own Attack (Sabotage).
• When Revealed: an effect that happens when the creature is Revealed or a Reveal shows it; against the enemy it strikes whoever revealed it (the creature, or else its player).
    • Placement: The creature is always placed in the slot(s) directly opposite the slot(s) it occupied. Any creatures already there are discarded to their owners' discard piles, following the Summoning onto an Occupied Slot rule.
    • Not Movement: Changing sides happens as part of the effect that changes control, when it resolves. It doesn't create a Movement Action, so it ignores movement restrictions like Cannot Act, Heavy, or the multi-slot immobility rule.
    • Controller vs. Owner: The controller changes; the owner never does. If the creature leaves the battlefield later, its card goes to its owner's hand or discard pile.
    • What the Creature Keeps: Current Health, Armor, status effects (including Doomed: its Last Rites will strike the slot opposite, now on its former side), current base stats (including Persistent Stat Modifications), and current Deployment Time. Temporary Stat Modifications are kept or lost according to their own rules or the Recruitment card.
    • Queued Actions: All actions its previous controller had queued for it this Day are cancelled. Its new controller can give it intents from the next Day's Planning (or immediately, in the rare case the card allows it).

5.5 Multi-Slot Creatures

Some creatures (on either side) take up two adjacent friendly slots. Their card says so.

Summoning
    • Choose any two adjacent friendly slots.
    • When the 'Play Creature Action' resolves, any creature in those slots is discarded, following the Summoning onto an Occupied Slot rule. The multi-slot creature then takes both slots.
• Immovable: Multi-slot creatures can never move or be moved: no Movement Actions, pushes, pulls, swaps, or any other effect. This can't be removed or overridden. (This is only the immobility part of Heavy; they don't get its other effects.) Changing sides is not movement and still works.
• One Creature: They count as a single creature for targeting, status effects, and actions.
• Two Slots: They take up both slots for adjacency (e.g., "adjacent creatures" abilities) and for blocking movement. An effect aimed at either slot affects the creature. An effect that covers both of its slots (e.g., an area effect) still hits it only once.
• Leaving Play: Both slots become empty.

5.6 Mandela Effect

A rare, one-time global event, from a Spell, a creature ability, or a unique boss ability. It is queued like any other action (Spell Layer if from a Spell, Ability & Attack Layer if from an ability). When it resolves:

• Who It Affects: Every creature on the battlefield (both sides) at that exact moment.
• Stat Change: Changes one or more base stats of those creatures (e.g., +X Attack, set Health to Y), as described by the card or ability.
• How Long It Lasts: As long as that creature stays on the battlefield. It belongs to the creature on the board, not to its card, and is separate from Persistent Stat Modifications.
• New Summons Aren't Affected: Creatures that enter play afterwards use their normal stats.
• Leaving Play: If an affected creature Leaves Play and later comes back (e.g., Revived or replayed), it returns without the Mandela change. A card can override this only if it says so explicitly (very rare).
• End of Combat: Since the battlefield doesn't carry over, every Mandela change ends with the combat.

5.7 Prophecy

A Myth mechanic: an effect announced now that happens later, in plain sight.

• Creation: A Prophecy is created when the action that makes it resolves. It has a countdown in Days and an effect, both shown openly to both sides. Prophecies are never concealed.
• Fulfillment: A Prophecy created on Day X with a countdown of N is fulfilled at Dawn of Day X+N. Its effect is then queued and resolves in the Dawn resolution pass, in the same layer as the action that created it.
• Targets: Follow the card's targeting keyword when the Prophecy is fulfilled. A Homing target chosen at creation still follows that creature; if it has left play, the effect is aimed at the slot it was last in.
• Independent: A Prophecy stays even if the creature that made it leaves play.
• Stopping One: Only effects that explicitly mention Prophecies can delay, change or remove them. The usual counterplay is to see it coming and get out of the way.
• Example: "Prophecy (2 Days): Static: deal 4 damage to the slot opposite this creature." Played on Day 3, it hits at Dawn of Day 5, so the opponent has a full Day to move the threatened creature.


5.8 Policies (2026-10-16)

From Slay the Spire's Powers: a Policy is a spell that becomes a rule of your side.
• It happens when it resolves, then again at each Dawn (after the Prophecies), for the rest of the combat.
• It never goes to the discard pile: it's in force, listed in the queue view ("In Force"), both sides'.
• Its targets are its own (your side, a random creature, the player): a Policy is never aimed.

  Income (Zeitgeist), Publicly Funded Faith Schools (Myth).

6. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY & BALANCING

6.1 Tone

The game is intentionally cynical, funny, and lighthearted. It satirizes modern absurdities, bureaucracy, internet culture, and common myth tropes.

Naming: when to avoid a name and when not
• Avoid: a game mechanic's name from another game, used for the same mechanic here. If our ability works like another game's ability, it must not also be called the same (Pokémon's Technician is our Penny Stock, Baton Pass our Handover). The problem is copying someone's mechanic and its name together.
• Fine: anything that isn't another game's mechanic. Plain words and phrases used for what they mean ("Meta" Group Chat is a chat that is meta, not the company's), real people and historical figures (Fat Tony, a real mobster, is as fine as Cleopatra), quotes, titles and memes.
• Art: pictures stay as drawn unless a change is asked for; quirky is part of the game's look.

6.2 Balancing

Getting the balance right is extremely important. It will need ongoing playtesting, data, and adjustments.
Numerical Balance
    • Start with conservative numbers for stats, damage, healing, Armor, and durations.
    • Player Health starts at 20 for both sides by default (Relics, Stocks, or specific encounters may change it).
    • Cost stat modifications appropriately. Persistent Stat Modifications should be more expensive or harder to get, because they last the whole combat and standard cleanses don't remove them.
