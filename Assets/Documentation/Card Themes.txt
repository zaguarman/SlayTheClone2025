Card Themes

What every card is making fun of, the real things it points at, its twist, and what could make it land harder.
Written 2026-10-14 from every card's name, flavor text, rules, art brief and picture (244 cards). A review to pick
from, not a list of changes: nothing here is done until the user picks it.


1. THE TONE

The game is cynical, funny and acid, and isn't afraid to upset anybody. Read across all 244 cards, the joke works the
same way almost every time:

  - An official line, then the truth. The flavor text speaks in the deadpan voice of a press release, an HR email or a
    sermon, and the second sentence undercuts it ("We're here for you. 'You' meaning the company." / "Sent. Nothing else
    will be.").
  - The sacred or the terrible, next to the everyday. Jesus with two submachine guns; the Last Supper as beer pong;
    accountants in a Counter-Strike standoff aiming calculators; a coroner signing NATURAL CAUSES over a dart; a raw
    steak doing "AI" at a desk.
  - Everybody gets hit. Mostly it punches up (billionaires, states, churches, spies, tech companies), and sideways at
    everyday archetypes (Karen, Boomer, the Keyboard Warrior, the Hipster). Nobody is safe, which is what keeps it fair.
  - References people know at a glance. Memes (Wojak, This Is Fine, NPC, the pool meme), Monty Python, Bond, The
    Simpsons, films everyone quotes, and real headlines (Trump's casinos, Snowden, oligarchs and windows, shrinkflation).
  - The rules are the punchline. The best cards are the ones where playing them tells the joke: Inflation hurts
    everyone, the enemy most; Fell Out of a Window needs an empty slot beside the target (the window); Red
    Tape adds Deployment Time; Hipster is immune to anything that hits more than one creature; the Unpaid Intern is the
    fodder that Sacrificial Ritual's flavor asks for ("Ideally the intern").

A card is at its strongest when it has all three beats:
  (1) a real target (who or what it makes fun of),
  (2) a hook people recognise (an event, a meme, a film, a headline),
  (3) a twist in the rules, so the joke happens on the table and not only in the text.
The notes below mark what each card has, and suggest what it could add when a beat is weak or missing.

Each card:
  Aim:   what it makes fun of.
  Hooks: real events, people, memes and works it connects to.
  Twist: how the card turns it, in the art, the flavor or the rules.
  Push:  what could make it hit harder, or tie it to the rest of the game.
[px] marks the cards with pixel art.


2. WHAT EACH FACTION MAKES FUN OF

What each faction's cards make fun of today, then the themes it can grow into (agreed with the user 2026-10-14). The
names in brackets are already in the Brainstorm List's Card Backlog; "no backlog name yet" marks themes that still need
one.

CORPORATE
  Today:  Late capitalism: the gig economy, tax havens, layoffs by email, philanthropy as a tax trick, startup hype,
          private equity, the boss who calls the company a family.
  Rule:   Every Mafia theme belongs here. Organised crime is just everyday business, and legality is only a business with
          a high entry price (the user). Fat Tony is the model.
  Grow:
    - Planned obsolescence: the light-bulb cartel that limited bulb life to 1,000 hours, printers that stop at a page
      count. (Phoebus Cartel)
    - A human life as a line item: Ford's Pinto memo found fixing the fuel tank cost more than paying for the deaths.
      (Pinto Memo, The Value of a Statistical Life)
    - Accounting fraud and the auditors who sign it off: Enron, the Big Four. (The Enron Ledger Fantasy, Big Four,
      Round-Tripping, Substance Over Form, EBITDA)
    - Market manipulation by machine: high-frequency trading, the 2010 flash crash. (Flash Crash, Spoofing,
      Front-Running, High-Frequency Trading Scam, Layering)
    - Housing as an asset: a cupboard sold as a "cozy loft", generation rent. (Cozy Loft in an "Exclusive" Location,
      Live Better in a Home of Your Own)
    - Dark patterns and the hard sell. (Forced Upselling, Buy Now, Give Your Soul Later, The Art of Selling: Involuntary
      Product Bundling, Sales Funnel)
    - Copyright as eternal property, and the takedown as a weapon. (120-Year Mickey Mouse Copyright, DMCA)
    - Trump the developer: in 1980 he demolished the Bonwit Teller building, promised its Art Deco friezes to a museum,
      then had them jackhammered to save time. It joins the Trump thread. (Bonwit Teller Building Demolition)
    - No backlog name yet: greenwashing, fast fashion, McKinsey-style consultancies.

STATECRAFT
  Today:  The state: bureaucracy, nepotism, war sold as defence, propaganda, Orwell's ministries, the monopoly on
          violence, the court that answers to nobody.
  Grow:
    - Sanctions and tariff wars: war by spreadsheet. (Economic War)
    - Torture with a legal memo: Guantanamo, waterboarding renamed "enhanced interrogation" by the Bush administration.
      (Enhanced Interrogation)
    - "Think of the children" as a reason to read everyone's messages. (To Protect the Children, Who Watches the
      Watchers)
    - Gerontocracy. (Represented by Living Dinosaurs)
    - Clientelism: jobs for friends, votes for favours. (Patronage Network)
    - Imperial Japan's suicide weapons as the peak of state madness. (Kaiten, Shinyo, Yokosuka, Fukuryu)
    - Rome as the template every empire copies. (Alea Iacta Est, Crossing the Rubicon, Rome Does Not Pay Traitors)
    - State money that can be switched off. (Digital Money)
    - Censorship in wartime. (War Censorship Law)
    - Learning to love the bomb (Dr. Strangelove's subtitle). (Manhattan Project)
    - No backlog name yet: gerrymandering, lobbyists going through the revolving door, border-wall theatre.

ZEITGEIST
  Today:  Modern life offline: archetypes (Karen, Boomer, Hipster), moods (burnout, toxic positivity, YOLO), the films
          and memes everyone quotes, the culture war at the pub.
  Grow:
    - The Simpsons' best pair: Homer's motivational sign covered by Maggie's photos. (Do It for Her, Don't Forget.
      You're Here Forever.)
    - Disco Elysium's despair. (It's All Disco, Baby, What Is Reality Anyway?, I Don't Want to Be This Kind of Animal
      Anymore)
    - Dating and sex, deadpan. (Don Juan, After-Sex Cigarette, Passive Smoker, Ladies First)
    - Class contempt. (Stop Being Poor, Prince Charles Paradox, Lost Generation)
    - Gender at home and at work. (Glass Ceiling, Weaponized Incompetence, Absentee Parents)
    - Fads and self-optimisation. (Fidget Spinner, Morning Routine)
    - Cringe and nihilism as moods. (Second-Hand Embarrassment, Melancholy, Yesn't)
    - Propaganda posters turned inside out, from The Simpsons. (Yvan Eht Nioj)
    - Overtourism. (Tourist Trap, from the plain items)

DIGITAL SOCIETY

    - The internet's own memes. (Task Failed Successfully)
    - No backlog name yet: AI slop and deepfakes, doxxing (already in the faction's design notes), OnlyFans.

SHADOW OPS.
  Today:  Spies and cover-ups: Bond, the CIA's real history (MKUltra, Paperclip, Glomar), Russian windows and polonium,
          Epstein's cameras, false flags, lone gunmen.
  Grow:
    - Conspiracies that turned out to be true, the modern ones above all: the NSA's PRISM (2013), Cambridge Analytica
      (2018), Crypto AG (the Swiss encryption company the CIA secretly owned and sold to governments for decades,
      revealed 2020), Pegasus (2021). (Conspiracies That Turned Out to Be True)
    - Bio labs: the Soviet bioweapons test site on Vozrozhdeniya ("Rebirth") Island in the Aral Sea, abandoned in 1992
      with anthrax buried on it, then joined to the mainland as the sea dried up. (Secret Anthrax Laboratory)
    - Drug-carrying pigeons, an old prison-smuggling trick that still works. (Drug Pigeon)
    - Embassies as spy nests and hiding places. (Embassy)
    - Self-destructing messages, hidden data. (Self-Destruct Message, Steganography, Secure Deletion, Tracking Beacon)
    - Stuxnet: it reportedly spread into Iran's nuclear plant on USB sticks. (Suspicious USB Stick in the Parking Lot)
    - The Unabomber thread: Kaczynski as a subject of the Harvard experiments, next to MK Ultra Test Subject. A
      Unabomber theme would be Zeitgeist, but what was done to him makes it Shadow Ops. (You Can't Eat Your Cake and
      Have It Too)
    - No backlog name yet: spyware sold to governments (NSO's Pegasus), private spy firms (Black Cube), the CIA and the
      cocaine trade (Iran-Contra), Mossad.

TECH
  Today:  Software and its people: the sysadmin, legacy code, ransomware, "AI" that's a guy named Dave, antivirus that
          sells your data, the hacker who is twelve.
  Grow:
    - Y2K: the apocalypse that didn't come. (Effect 2000)
    - AI doom. (P(Doom), and Mind's The ELIZA Effect)
    - Developer culture. (Code Review, Pair Programming, Ctrl + Z, Ctrl + Y, Bounty Program)
    - Big Tech as a single landlord. (FAANG)
    - The user as the problem, or the target. (End User)
    - No backlog name yet: enshittification (Cory Doctorow's word for platforms rotting once they own you), cookie
      banners, "the cloud is someone else's computer", AI hallucinating with 83% confidence.

MYTH
  Today:  Religion and legend as business and spectacle: Monty Python's Grail, the televangelist, the crusade, the
          prophecy, the curse in the fine print.
  Grow:
    - Tech-bro spirituality: ayahuasca retreats, psilocybin as religion. (Entheogenic Sacrament, Drug-Induced
      Spirituality)
    - The Church's darker business: the confession box, faith schools on public money. (Confessional, Publicly Funded
      Faith Schools)
    - Death as a character. (Grim Reaper, Die by the Sword, Public Autopsy)
    - Deep-history oddities. (Gudea, Venus of Willendorf, Luxor, Bermuda Triangle)
    - Memes. (Gracias Jesús, Here Be Dragons, Blessing in Disguise)
    - Ambrosia: a pun across factions. It's the food of the gods and the name of the young-blood startup. (Ambrosia)
    - No backlog name yet: indulgences (forgiveness sold for cash, the original prosperity gospel), Scientology, the
      trade in saints' relics.

MIND
  Today:  The brain's bugs: biases, paradoxes, imposter syndrome, the trolley problem, the basilisk.
  Grow:
    - Philosophy as a fight. (Socrates, God Is Dead, Ontology, Epistemology, Tolerance Paradox)
    - The mind's slips and tricks. (Freudian Slip, Legerdemain, Hyperbole)
    - Needs and their pyramid. (Maslow's Pyramid)
    - Bonding with machines: ELIZA, a 1966 chatbot people confided in. (The ELIZA Effect)
    - Unreality. (Liminal Space, plus Memory Wipe / Mandela Effect, which the backlog already suggests moving here)
    - No backlog name yet: Dunning-Kruger, the sunk cost fallacy, gaslighting.

UTOPIA
  Today:  Science and the tech-bro future: accelerationism, biohacking billionaires, de-extinction, the breakthrough
          that blows up the lab.
  Grow:
    - Billionaire city-states: libertarian charter cities, floating nations, Saudi Arabia's The Line. (Seasteading,
      Longevity Hub, The Line)
    - The ageing West. (Inverted Population Pyramid)
    - Science's horrors: mad cow disease. (Prion Disease)
    - Paleontology jokes. (Coprolite, Ankylorhiza)
    - Physics puns. (White Dwarf, Quark, Femtosecond Laser)
    - No backlog name yet: Big Pharma (the Sacklers and OxyContin), fusion that's always 30 years away, the billionaire
      space race.


3. THE CARDS

CORPORATE (35)

Casino (spell)
  Aim:   Trump the businessman: the man who managed to lose money running casinos.
  Hooks: Trump's Atlantic City casinos (the Taj Mahal among them) went through several bankruptcies; "the house always
         wins"; The Apprentice.
  Twist: The flavor does the job ("The house always wins. Except the houses he owned"), and the rules make the whole board
         gamble on a weather wheel.
  Push:  Name the wheel's results after his career: Windfall as "Bailout", Hail as "Lawsuits", and a result that wipes the
         caster's discard and costs nothing ("Chapter 11"). He's the thread that ties Spite-Elected President, Reality TV
         Star, Golden Dome, Religious Instrumentalization and NDA together, so one caricature drawn the same way on every
         card turns them into a set.

Headhunter (spell) [px]
  Aim:   Recruiters who fish in your company's pond.
  Hooks: LinkedIn spam, the green #OpenToWork ring round a profile picture, "exciting opportunity, competitive salary".
  Twist: You discard a card to fetch a creature: to hire, somebody gets let go.
  Push:  Bait the hook with the #OpenToWork ring, or a pizza-Friday flyer instead of stock options. The flavor ("We found
         everyone on LinkedIn") is already sharp.

Counterfeit (creature)
  Aim:   Knockoff culture.
  Hooks: Fake brands like "Adibas" and "Pumma", Wish.com's expectation-versus-reality memes.
  Twist: Wannabe copies another creature's stats: the fake becomes exactly as good as the original.
  Push:  Make the art the expectation-versus-reality meme: a "Spider-Man" from Wish with the logo misspelled. If Wannabe
         copied only Attack and Health, never the ability, the copy would be honest about being a copy.

Private Equity (creature) [px]
  Aim:   Buyouts that load companies with debt, strip them and sell them off.
  Hooks: Toys "R" Us went bankrupt after its leveraged buyout; Goldman Sachs called a "great vampire squid" (Rolling
         Stone, 2009).
  Twist: A vampire sucking gold out of a family shop. Credit Grab steals the buffs other cards invested.
  Push:  Make the vampire a squid, after that famous line. A Toys "R" Us giraffe crying in the shop window would give it a
         face people know.

Shell Company (creature) [px]
  Aim:   Tax havens.
  Hooks: The Panama Papers (2016), Delaware and the Cayman Islands, the "Double Irish" tax arrangement.
  Twist: A literal shell game. Shell Game shuffles creatures around, the way the money gets moved.
  Push:  Label the walnut shells Panama, Cayman and Delaware. Shadow Ops.' Whistleblower could reveal it (the Panama
         Papers were a leak), a combo that tells the story.

Work Shift Rotation (creature)
  Aim:   Workers treated as interchangeable parts.
  Hooks: Amazon warehouses, "Time Off Task" tracking, zero-hours contracts.
  Twist: Handover passes its buffs and statuses to its replacement: the next shift inherits the burnout along with the
         vest. Rules and joke are one and the same.
  Push:  Put a handheld scanner in the art showing "TIME OFF TASK: 00:04".

Unpaid Intern (creature)
  Aim:   Unpaid internships.
  Hooks: "Paid in exposure", the coffee run, "great experience for your CV".
  Twist: A 1/1 born to be sacrificed. Myth's Sacrificial Ritual ("Somebody has to go. Ideally the intern") picks it first.
  Push:  This is the best running gag in the game. Make the intern token what Mass Layoffs, Inhuman Resources and
         Sacrificial Ritual eat by preference, and keep the art's tray of coffees with everyone's name but theirs.

Inhuman Resources (creature)
  Aim:   HR as the company's lawyer pretending to be your friend.
  Hooks: "We're a family", mandatory wellness webinars, the meeting with a box of tissues on the table.
  Twist: The flavor lands the turn ("'You' meaning the company"). The future rules (Layoffs) are the punchline.
  Push:  A "WE'RE A FAMILY" poster behind the pink slips, which Mass Layoffs answers ("We were never a family").

Micromanager (creature)
  Aim:   The boss who checks everything.
  Hooks: "Per my last email", remote-work tracking software, mouse jigglers sold to get around it.
  Twist: It boosts its workers with Coffeinated instead of a raise.
  Push:  Give it the remote-work angle: the magnifying glass over a webcam feed, or a mouse jiggler caught in the act.

Multilevel Marketing Seller (creature)
  Aim:   MLMs: the "boss babe" pyramid.
  Hooks: Herbalife, Amway, LuLaRoe, essential oils, "Hey hun!" messages.
  Twist: Recruits pay a share of their draws upward: the pyramid as a rule.
  Push:  Its flavor is word for word the same as Crypto "Entrepreneur"'s; one of them needs a new line. Keep "triangle of
         opportunity" here (it's the MLM pitch) and give the crypto bro his own.

Day Trader (creature)
  Aim:   Retail trading as gambling.
  Hooks: r/wallstreetbets, the GameStop squeeze (2021), the "Stonks" meme, Robinhood switching off the buy button.
  Twist: Insider Job: its damage ignores Armor. Up 300% in the morning, rent due in the afternoon.
  Push:  The art could be the Stonks meme man. A Robinhood-style trick (the enemy's buy button switched off) would make a
         great Corporate spell for the backlog.

Pluriemployed Hustler (creature)
  Aim:   Hustle culture and the gig economy.
  Hooks: Uber, Deliveroo, Glovo, "rise and grind", five-star ratings as a lifeline.
  Twist: Penny Stock doubles its small hits: small gigs add up.
  Push:  Tie it to Zeitgeist's Delivery Cyclist: the same rider, one exploited by Corporate and one fighting traffic. Its
         stars could fall each Dusk, like a rating.

  Push:  On the giant cheque, his name bigger than the amount.

  - Crypto "Entrepreneur" and Multilevel Marketing Seller shared a flavor. Done: the crypto bro says "Funds are safu."
    now, and "He Bought, Dump It" is in the backlog.
  - Strike's drawing was a fur hat. Done: the head of the works council eating lobster while the workers get a biscuit
    each, and the boss is happy.
  - Mental Health Day had no art brief. Done: a politician opening a "day" in front of a clinic closed by budget cuts,
    the reiki lady the only help left. Alternative medicine went into the backlog.
  - Scope Creep hid its project joke behind a pun. Done: the Gantt chart that outgrows the wall.
  - Surveillance Giraffe: kept as it is. Absurd, and nice to have a giraffe around.
  - Sex Clanker: its picture is its own (the old note was out of date). Kept.
  - Cleaning Robot lost the subscription joke. Done: Google Nest-style, your data uploaded for free.
  - Taxpayer Funds Venture Capital Profits had placeholder rules. Done: you draw 2, every creature on the board pays 1.
    Note: Solidarity Towards The Small Businessman (draw 2, only your creatures pay) now does less for the same; worth a
    look in the next balance pass.
  - Its A Trap: a double reference (Star Wars name, Simpsons art). Left as it is.
  - Mox Nox: no joke needed. Some cards are just Latin words the user finds interesting.
